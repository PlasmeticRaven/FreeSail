**Two things in this card that are the owner's.**

1. **A lateen taken aback: SETTLED, it is built.** The owner, 2026-11-18: "A lateen taken aback should increase the strain on the mast, as a backed square sail does, so that on the worse tack and not dipped, the sail may strain the mast and, in high enough wind, risk damage." So the step the session had drawn as an option (its A1d) is in the card: a lateen with the wind on its wrong face, held against its mast, loads the mast and the yard as a backed square sail does; and so does a lateen left on the bad hand, lying against its mast and not shifted; in wind enough the spar may be damaged, by the engine's own strain and carrying-away. It is a new rule of force in the physics and is named as one; one and a half to two days by the session's measure, and it changes how every lateen hull goes about (in a trial it took the polacre's tack from 164 seconds to 407). The figures (at what wind a yard strains and carries away, aback and on the bad hand) are judgement, proposed by the session and ruled by the lead, said as judgement. (The lead had recommended leaving it out; the owner chose otherwise.)
2. **Winfield's passages.** The session's sheet lists them by title and class (`docs/design/Decisions-Package90-2026-11.md`, Part D, on the branch `claude/pkg-90`); two are waited on (the three-decker's pounds by calibre and her manning), and the polacre's files wait on the polacras. The owner, 2026-11-18: he has not yet looked for them and will answer on them specifically when he has. They come into the tree as facts by page, in the lead's words, never as the book's text.

**The lead's rulings already given** (nudge 18, each said as judgement in the documents): the car passes abaft the mast; the bad hand draws two-thirds, as the lug does; the yard's change at the lugger's own rate, 8.7 seconds a metre of yard (the tartane's main about three and a half minutes), one hand for 2.5 metres; 22 and 12 knots and half an hour on the bad hand as the engine's defaults; `shift the mainsail` means the yard's change on a lateen hull, and `shift the mainsail for a new one` the sail room's.
