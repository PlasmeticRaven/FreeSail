Read the specification's §3 to §9 and `docs/dev/M1-WorkPackages.md` (whose "what package 3 provides" section still holds) before your section here. Working rules are unchanged: Python 3.11; `ruff check .`, `ruff format .`, `python -m pytest` clean before you finish; no randomness outside `core/rng.py` named streams; SI inside, nautical outside; log text in ship's-log voice; do not edit files owned by another package (say what you need in your report); commit in your worktree with the attribution lines you were given; finish with a report covering what you built, how to run it, assumptions, needs from others, and what the owner should check.

## Package 8: reference ships verified (`data/ships/*.yaml`, `tools/gen_ships.py`)

The two ship files are drafts generated by `tools/gen_ships.py` with approximate particulars. Make them defensible.

- **The frigate.** Pick a documented British 36-gun 18-pounder frigate class of the 1790s (the file is named *Amazon*; the Amazon class of 1795 or a near contemporary is fine) and set the hull particulars from published dimensions: gundeck and keel length to a waterline length, beam, draught, displacement or burthen converted sensibly, and a stated metacentric height typical of the type. Spar lengths and sail areas from period proportions: Lever (1808/1827) gives mast and yard proportions to the ship's length and beam; Luce 1884 Appendix A and E give rope and canvas tables; the OCR texts are in `docs/references/` and grep well. Where a number is a judgement, say so in a comment in the file and in your report.
- **The schooner.** A Baltimore-built topsail schooner of about 1804, around 90 to 100 feet on deck, from any documented example or the type's usual proportions.
- **Ratings.** Spar and line `rating_kn` from rope circumferences and breaking strains in Luce's tables (App. A) with a period safety margin, so that package 9's carrying-away rule produces truth 9 (royals and topgallants carried away in 35 knots on a reach; nothing in 20 knots under plain sail). Coordinate with package 9 through your reports if the rule and the ratings need to meet in the middle.
- **Brace limits and centre of lateral resistance.** Keep the raised brace limits (Luce: about 28° of yard to keel sharp up). For `clr_x_m`, give a justified value rather than the +2.5 m set at integration; if your value differs, note the effect on helm balance for package 10.
- **`hull_speed_kn`.** Set explicitly and justify (the derived 1.34√LWL is too generous for a 1790s hull; see `docs/dev/M1-TuningNotes.md`).
- **Aliases and groups.** Add the names a period officer would use that are missing, in particular anything the owner reached for at the gate. Check every alias resolves (the loader validates).
- **Keep the generator the source.** Edit `tools/gen_ships.py` and regenerate, so the script and the files stay identical (the test in your report should show `python tools/gen_ships.py` leaves `git diff` empty). Cite sources in the file headers and per-value comments where the generator allows.
- Tests: extend `tests/test_ship_loader.py` with checks that the frigate's plain-sail area, spar heights and displacement are within stated period ranges, so a later edit that breaks plausibility fails a test.

## Package 9: strain and carrying away (`freesail/physics/strain.py`)

Spec §7.5. Package 4 already writes `load_kn` on sails, spars and lines every substep. You add what happens as a result.

- `apply_strain(ship, dt, rng_stream)` called from `integrate.step` once per tick after the substeps (you may edit `integrate.py` for exactly that call and nothing else; say so). Uses the random stream `ship.extra["rng"].stream("strain")` if present, else a stream passed in; the session composer will provide `ship.extra["rng"]` (the lead adds this; assume it exists and write a fallback that makes the failure probability zero when it does not, so tests without a World are deterministic).
- Condition decay between ratio 1.0 and 1.5; probabilistic carrying away above 1.5 per §7.5, and deterministic at condition 0. `strain.warning` (notable) once per part per 10 minutes when above 1.0.
- Consequences, each with an urgent log line in the voice of the log: a parted halyard drops its yard (sail to `IN_THE_GEAR`); a parted sheet frees its sail (effective area 20%, load on the yard doubled, sail "flogging" until re-sheeted, which `set` or a new `sheet home` step must do); a parted brace lets the yard swing to the wind; a carried-away spar marks itself and all `dependents()` wrecked, their sails give no force, and the wreck adds windage (package 4 already multiplies wrecked windage by 3; confirm). A sail blows out above 1.8 of its cloth rating. Spars sent down (`sent_down`) carry no load.
- Repair is milestone 8; nothing recovers. But `cut away`: an order to cut away a wreck is out of scope; note what state the wreck leaves for it.
- Tests: decay rates; probability at a fixed seed; each consequence on the reference ships; determinism; nothing carries away in 20 knots under plain sail for 20 minutes; something does in 35 knots under all sail within 20 minutes (these two are truths 9, and they depend on package 8's ratings; if the draft ratings make them impossible, report the ratio you needed).

## Package 10: known truths and tuning (`tests/test_known_truths.py`, constants)

Starts after packages 8 and 9 have landed. Spec §7.6, `docs/dev/M1-TuningNotes.md`.

- Encode all fifteen truths as tests against the reference ships, plus truth 16: a turning circle from Luce 1884 Appendix L (read it in `docs/references/luce/luce-1884-*.txt`; if the OCR is unreadable, use a tactical diameter of about 4 to 6 ship lengths at 8 knots hard over). Each test runs a scenario through `freesail.api.session.make_world` and the Orders language, not by poking state, so it also exercises the evolutions.
- Then tune: `hull.py` constants, `sail_classes.yaml` curves, `hull_speed_kn` and `clr_x_m` in the ship files only if package 8's justified values still fail a truth (say so), and `evolutions/scripts.py` for heave-to and fill-away (truth 12: hove to under 1.5 knots with the head 45° to 60° off; fill away must bear away deliberately before bracing full, from any heading, without hanging aback). One change at a time, each recorded in `docs/dev/TuningNotes.md` (rename the M1 file) with the truth it moved.
- The owner's gate M1 heave-to observation is your starting point: seed 7, wind north 15 knots, frigate from heading 293 under plain sail braced sharp up, `heave to`, 900 ticks: apparent wind 51° on the starboard bow, speed 2.4 knots with sternway, helm +15°.
- **Truth 17 (getting under way).** From rest at heading 293° with the wind north at 15 knots (the gate scenario), `set plain sail` followed at once by `brace sharp up on the starboard tack` must leave the frigate gathering way and settled close-hauled within fifteen minutes, without being taken aback or ending in irons. At present she rounds up into the wind as the headsails, spanker and courses fill before she has steerage way (the helmsman cannot hold her at under a knot), and hangs aback at about 325° with sternway. The gate M1 sequence avoided it only because the fore topsail was set first. Expect the answer to involve some mix of balance (`clr_x_m`), the helmsman's low-speed behaviour and sternway rule, and possibly a `set plain sail` order that sets sails in a seamanlike sequence (headsails last when getting under way, per Luce ch. XXI "Casting"). Say which.
- Stop when all truths pass or when a truth cannot be met without a per-rig factor; then add the factor as a named, documented number and say why.

## Package 11: server and browser client (`freesail/ui/server.py`, `freesail/api/queries.py`, `client/`)

Spec §9.2 to §9.4, and the ship-view principles in `docs/DesignProposal.md` §6.1 (read them; they change how the profile is built).

- `api/queries.py`: `snapshot(world) -> dict` per spec §9.4, plus `ship_graph(ship) -> dict` for drawing: every spar with class, `x_m`, `height_m`, `length_m`, parent, side, brace angle; every sail with class, roles, area, state, reefs, sheet angle, centre height; hull dimensions; groups. SI numbers; the client converts.
- `ui/server.py`: FastAPI with the routes in §9.2 and a websocket that streams every log event and a snapshot at the cadence in §9.2. The driver (hold, go, compression, tick) lives here; the world ticks in a background task under a lock, exactly as the console does. `python -m freesail.ui.server data/ships/frigate-36.yaml [--seed] [--wind] [--heading]` then open `http://localhost:8000`. Add `fastapi` and `uvicorn` to the `server` extra (they are already listed) and `httpx` to `dev` for the test client.
- `client/`: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no build step, no framework. Four panels: log (severity-coloured, filterable, routine roll-up at high compression, a command line with history beneath); instruments; the **ship view**; the map (north up, hull symbol with heading, last hour's track, wind arrow, scale bar).
- **The ship view is built as a projected skeleton, not a drawing.** From `ship_graph`, build a small 3D skeleton in ship coordinates: masts as vertical segments at `x_m`, yards as horizontal segments rotated by `brace_angle`, gaffs and booms angled from their mast, sails as quads (square: hung from their yard, width the yard length, depth from area / yard length; gaff: between mast, gaff and boom; jib-headed: a triangle along its stay's line from the spar it is of to a point on the bowsprit or deck), studding sails outboard of their booms. Project it orthographically with a `facing` parameter (radians, the viewer's bearing from the ship) and the current `heel`, and draw it with SVG: hull as a simple silhouette from length, freeboard and sheer for now (package 8 art later), spars as lines weighted by class, sails filled by state (furled: a bundle along the spar; in the gear: a gathered shape; set: full; reefed: shortened by the reef fraction; backed: shaded; blown out: torn outline; wrecked: dashed and drooping). **Milestone 2 uses a fixed beam-on facing (viewer abeam to leeward) but the facing must be a parameter**, and a hidden query parameter or key should let a developer rotate it to prove the projection works. Running rigging is not drawn in M2 except braces and sheets as faint lines that brighten while their evolution is in progress.
- Style: quiet, legible, flat fills, limited palette, a sea line and a sky band. No animation beyond what state changes cause. Nothing invented: every element maps to a snapshot field.
- Tests: server routes and websocket with the FastAPI test client; the snapshot and ship-graph shapes on both reference ships; the skeleton projection as a pure function tested in JavaScript is not required, but keep `projection.js` free of DOM code so it can be tested later; the client must work in a plain Chromium with no network. A headless screenshot of both ships is welcome in the report if Playwright is available in your environment (it is in the lead's), but tests must not require a browser.

## Package 12: the Sailing Master's Primer (`docs/primer/`)

Documentation that teaches the owner, and later any player or LLM agent, how to sail these ships in the period manner, in the period words, using this game's Orders. Written for a reader with theoretical sailing knowledge and no habit of the terminology. Markdown, several short chapters, each with the orders to type and what the log will say:

1. The ship: the parts and their names, from the ship files and Falconer, for the frigate and the schooner (the reader should be able to point at any noun the parser accepts).
2. The wind and the points of sail: true and apparent wind, tacks, close-hauled, full and by, reaching, running; why a square-rigger lies six points off; what `state` reports.
3. Making and shortening sail: plain sail, the order of setting and taking in, reefing, studding sails; the words of command and their meaning.
4. Trimming: bracing the yards (sharp up, up, in, square), tending the sheets, the new `trim` order (package 13), weather and lee helm and what to do about them.
5. Going about: tacking step by step as the log shows it, missing stays, wearing, box-hauling (not yet implemented: say so), heaving to and filling away.
6. The watch and the log: bells, watches, the marks in the log, the driver commands, saving and replaying.
7. A first passage: a worked example on each ship, a page each, that a reader can type along with.
8. Where to read more: the chapters of Luce and Lever behind each evolution (`docs/references/LuceChapterMap.md`).

Every order shown must parse: run each through `freesail.orders.handle` against the reference ships (a small script in your worktree is fine; do not commit test-only scripts outside `tests/`). Add a test that extracts the fenced order blocks from the primer and checks they are accepted or rejected as the text says. Cite sources for the seamanship as the evolution files do.

## Package 13: the `trim` order (lead)

A period order the owner reached for at the gate: `trim sails`, `trim the yards`, `trim the sheets`. Braces every set square sail's yard to the best angle for the present apparent wind (the yard's chord at the apparent wind angle minus the class luff margin, capped at the brace limit, signed by tack) as a `brace` evolution per yard, and trims the fore-and-aft sheets at once. Lives in `data/vocabulary.yaml` and `orders/verbs.py`. Done by the lead in the main branch while the packages run.

## Package 14: completion as you type (lead, then client)

The owner asked for suggestions while typing, to bridge the primer and play. The grammar is data, so this is cheap: a completer that offers, at each point in a half-typed order, the verbs and synonyms that could start it, then the nouns this ship has (parts, aliases, groups, with contractions), then the modifiers the verb accepts, drawn from `data/vocabulary.yaml` and the ship's noun table (`orders/resolve.py`). Console first (prompt_toolkit completer; lead), then the same suggestions in the browser command line from a `/api/vocabulary` route that returns the tables (package 11 integration or a follow-up). Suggestions never invent an order the parser would refuse.
