## What this is

Three faults of the game as it is, two of them found by the owner in his own play (his notes 6 and 10 in `docs/playtests/2026-11-24-gate-m7-owner-runs/README.md`, "His later notes"), all three written down in `docs/dev/CarriedForward-M7.md`, section 4, as faults 16, 17 and 18. What is written here is what was seen and measured by the lead; the causes are yours to find.

1. **(17) A far ship at anchor that never sails, and costs the pace.** The owner played a cruise from off Dover and found the game slow by day: "the maximum pace on my computer seems to be about 60x, even when set to a higher compression". The lead replayed it with the pace timed and profiled. One trader of the background trade ("trade 33 tons of brandy from the Havre to Dover"), lying in the state `at anchor` off Le Havre, more than a hundred miles from the player, is told to weigh every minute of the day (`freesail/world/brains.py`, `FarCaptain._work`: `at anchor`, clear, daylight, `AT_ANCHOR_LEAST_S` passed, so `_weigh`), has no saved plan, lays her whole passage afresh (`_plan`, `Planner(self.world).legs_for(...)` in `freesail/world/captains.py`), and the plan's first leg is to anchor where she lies; so she stays in the state, and the next minute it is done again. Each laying cost about three-quarters of a second on the build machine (the sea-route search; the chart's tiles were loaded 1,789 times in a thousand ticks of that stretch): 7.7 of 11.6 seconds in six hundred ticks. By night she does not weigh and the pace comes back; at dawn it falls again. She never sails. One ship of ten in his sea did it.
   - *His world, so that you can find her or her like:* the region `channel-east`, begun 1805-06-12 05:00, seed 1805, the hermaphrodite brig at 51.1145 N 1.32 E, a King's ship by her papers, climatology on, no weather or ships written, the business on. By tick 44,500 the vessel `business-16` was so. The player's own orders are not given to you (his saves are not in the repository and are not handed on); a world made so and left to run, or any world with the business on, is enough if you count which far captain lays a plan more than once in ten minutes and what each laying costs. The lead's measuring scripts are on the scratch branch `claude/trial-6` under `lead-backup/perf/` (`git fetch origin claude/trial-6`): `who_plans.py` wraps `FarCaptain._plan` and prints each far vessel's state, intent and plan; they take a save, so adapt them to a world you make.
   - *Wanted:* (a) the cause found and mended: why a trader lies `at anchor` off the port she is to load at with a plan that begins by anchoring there, and what she should be doing (in port loading, or sailing): she sails. (b) Whatever the cause, no far captain lays a passage again and again to no effect: a weigh that leaves her at anchor is not tried again every minute. (c) The cost of one laying looked at: is the chart's tile cache thrown away and loaded again within one search, and can it be kept, without changing any route laid? If a route would change, do not change it: report it. (d) A truth of the pace: in a world with the business on, over a day of the far ships' time, no far vessel's plan is laid more than a stated few times an hour, and the far ships' minute costs no more than a stated share of a tick's time on the build machine (see how `tests/test_known_truths.py` pins a pace with `BUILD_MACHINE_FLOOR`; a pace truth is in the slow tier and must not be a timing test that fails under load: prefer counting the layings to timing them).
   - *Not in scope:* the rule that a ship within four miles of the player is made a fully crewed ship, its range and its cap (`freesail/world/crews.py`): they are the design's, and they cost what the design measured.

2. **(18) A ship made a King's ship in the harbour carries no private signals.** The harbour writes `papers: {service: kings}` for her. The month's table is given (`freesail/world/signals.py`, `table_of`) by the captain's role (a King's ship's, a cruiser's, a commodore's) or by a scenario's `papers: {private_signals: true}`, and the player's own ship has no rules-based captain; so `the private signal`, `show the private signal` and `answer the private signal` are refused aboard her with the merchantman's reason. The primer's chapter 23 (`docs/primer/23-colours-and-signals.md`) says in words that it is so. No scenario shipped with the game has `service: kings` in its papers; only a game made in the harbour.
   - *Wanted:* a King's ship by her papers carries her nation's table for the month; `papers: {private_signals: false}` still takes it away, and `true` still gives it to any ship; a privateer or a merchantman by her papers carries none, as now. The primer's sentence made true. A truth in the harbour's own form (a game begun as the harbour begins one) and one at `table_of`. Every other reader of "is she a King's ship" in the signals looked at for the same gap (`freesail/world/service.py` is the one answer for her service: `service_of`, `service_role`); say what you found.

3. **(16) A save of *the first action* has its checkpoint refused on loading.** Loading the owner's save of that scenario, the lead's loader said the checkpoint "names pathlib.PosixPath", refused it, and replayed the journal instead: the game loads rightly, a minute and a half slower. (`freesail/core/replay.py`: `_Pickler`, `_Unpickler`, `CheckpointRefused`, `tools/checkpoint_names.py`.)
   - *Wanted:* what in that world holds a path object found, and kept as words instead, so that a checkpoint of the first action written on one machine loads on another of either kind (his is Windows, the build machine is not); a truth that a first action saved after some minutes of play, with a fight begun, loads by its checkpoint and goes on as the game that was saved; and the other shipped scenarios swept for the same (each begun, run a few minutes, saved and loaded: how many load by checkpoint).
