# The watcher's journal: the frigate's passage (Opus 5.5 through Claude Desktop)

Every entry of the watcher's journal as the save holds it (`agent_journals`), the harness's own entries (stand-bys, the exit) included. Verbatim.

- **Morning watch, 8 bells (04:00)** (note): Before the clock starts, 04:00, off Ushant (light SW by S 2 mi), bound for Falmouth. Wind SW 15 kn, glass 30.18, clear, moderate sea; plain sail ordered, steering N, hands at the sails. Falmouth lies roughly 100 miles nearly due north, so the wind will be on the larboard quarter the whole way if it holds: a fair passage, perhaps 12-15 hours. Things to watch: (1) the lead orders count 'run since noon', and noon falls mid-passage at 11:59, so the 20- and 35-mile thresholds will be measured from noon, not from Ushant; (2) a straight course from here for Falmouth passes close to the Manacles, a few miles south-east of the entrance; (3) the 'outer road' order wears ship on any sounding under 20 fathoms, wherever it comes. Noticed: state shows apparent wind 0 kn on the bow while readings show 13 kn at 132 on the larboard quarter.

The 'since noon' counting is deliberate: at noon she heaves to, the deep-sea lead goes under 3 knots, she fills away and shapes a fresh course for Falmouth, so the lead thresholds then count from the noon cast (20 miles = about 30 off, 35 = about 15 off). My concern withdrawn. Note: 'morning sail' never fired today because the clock started two minutes after sunrise. Yards trimmed at 05:02 after the sails shook; seven knots.

Hove to, but she came up and every sail went aback 12:01-12:05, then paid off N by E and gathered 4 knots before the cast. 'bring to for soundings' and 'soundings' were logged as contrary orders, the later standing, which may be why she didn't stay hove to. 'soundings' fired at 12:00 with the log at 3.5 knots. The courses, fore topgallant and spanker stayed in the gear until the captain set them by hand at 12:22. For the developer: the noon chain works, but the heave-to doesn't hold during a sounding, and nothing restores the sail taken in to heave to.

- **Last dog watch (18:53)** (note): Passage end, 18:50: hove to in the outer road off Falmouth on the starboard tack, 17 fathoms, good ground, St Anthony's Head N two miles, the Old Wall N by W a mile. About 99 miles from Ushant in 14h50m, no damage. For the record: (1) 'landfall' shaped straight lines for Falmouth that ran onto the Manacles (17:32: dead ahead, two miles) and later toward the Old Wall. The lead never warned: no bottom at twenty fathoms until inside the bay. The captain hauled to NE by N, then NE, at 17:37-17:43 and cleared the Penwin and the Vaze by about a mile. (2) 'keep her full' bore her away toward the Old Wall at 18:35. The book has no notion of dangers. (3) 'the outer road' wore her at 14 fathoms with the Old Wall a mile N. Every square sail went aback mid-wear (18:43), but she came round safely. (4) Recurring 'contrary orders, the later stands' between the lead orders and the heave-to and bearing orders; at noon this cost the heave-to. (5) Order phrasings that failed: 'steer for Falmouth' (the right one is 'shape a course for Falmouth'), 'take a sounding' ('heave the lead'), 'trim the spanker sheet'. My own error: the spanker trim call at 08:01. My Manacles warning at 15:42 put the line inside them; in fact it ran straight onto them.

- **Last dog watch (18:55)** (note): Closing notes for the developer, at the captain's invitation. WHAT I LIKED. The brief was clear and honest, and the consent and opt-out terms were plain enough that I never had to wonder about them. Sightings marked 'a danger' were the single most useful line all day. Log lines like 'contrary orders, the later stands' and 'not carried out; the land is not in sight' made the standing orders legible from outside. The tell/ask split worked: I knew when an answer was owed. Period touches (the Deadman, 'by the deep fourteen', 'quarter less seventeen') gave the place a real feel. Watching a passage run by its book, and then seeing exactly where the book ran out, was absorbing. FRICTION IN THE HARNESS. (a) At 1x a long stand-by means calling stand_by again every 200 seconds; a longer hold, or not needing to re-poll, would help. (b) 'a sounding' and 'a notable event' wake too often once the lead goes every ten minutes; an event like 'a danger sighted' or 'a bearing steady and closing' would be ideal for a watcher. (c) in_sight truncates to eight marks plus 'N more'; dangers should always be listed, or listed first. I couldn't see the Manacles until 17:32, and before that I was working from memory of the real chart. (d) The watcher has no chart. A reading of bearing and distance to a named mark, or of charted dangers within N miles, would let me advise from the game's chart instead of from memory. POSSIBLE BUGS. state shows apparent wind 0 kn while readings show 13 (04:00). Hands on deck changed at four bells in the morning watch. Mark names are lower-cased ('black Head', 'manacle Point', 'lowland Point'). 'the Nare Head' near the Helford is, I think, Nare Point; Nare Head is on the Roseland. I'm unsure of 'the Beast' and 'the Gray'. 'morning sail' never fired because the clock started after sunrise. All square sails went aback mid-wear at 18:43, and again at noon while heaving to. STANDING-ORDER DESIGN. Shaping a course should know about charted dangers, or at least warn when the line crosses one. 'keep her full' needs a guard against bearing away toward a danger. The lead should be able to go while hove to without breaking the heave-to. Something should restore the sail taken in to heave to once she fills away. PERSONALLY. I enjoyed this. The best moment was the captain asking how late he could leave the Manacles and then hauling off at 17:37, close to the time I'd named. I'd gladly stand a watch on the experimental voyage.
