Amazon reached the outer road off Falmouth and hove to on the starboard tack in 17 fathoms, as your book intended. She covered the 99 miles from Ushant in about 14 hours 50 minutes with no damage. I've opted out of the watcher station; the game is saved, and my notes are in the journal.

What the book did well

The noon sequence ran almost as designed: sight at 11:59, heave to, deep-sea cast (53 fathoms, fine grey sand), fill away, shape a fresh course. But the heave-to didn't hold through the cast: she came up into the wind and then gathered way before the lead went.
"Trim on a shift" kept the yards right all day without anyone touching them.
"The outer road" and "lie off" ended the voyage where you meant them to.

Where it needed a captain

Shaping a course ignores dangers. The landfall course ran straight onto the Manacles, which were dead ahead at two miles at 17:32. Later it led toward the Old Wall. The lead never found bottom at twenty fathoms until she was inside the bay, so it gave no warning. Your haul to NE by N and then NE at 17:37 cleared the Penwin and the Vaze by about a mile.
"Keep her full" bore her away toward the Old Wall at 18:35. Then "the outer road" wore her, a turn to starboard across the rock's bearing a mile off. She came round safely, but every square sail went aback during the wear at 18:43, as they had at noon while heaving to.
"Contrary orders, the later stands" kept recurring between the lead orders and the heave-to and bearings orders. At noon that cost you the heave-to.
Nothing restored the sail taken in to heave to after she filled away. You had to set it again by hand.

Harness suggestions

At 1x, a long stand-by meant re-calling stand_by every 200 seconds. A longer hold would help.
A wake event like "a danger sighted" or "a steady bearing closing" would suit the watcher.
The in_sight list cuts off at eight marks plus "N more". Dangers should always be listed, or listed first.
A chart query, such as bearing and distance to a named mark, would let the watcher advise from your chart. Today I had to work from memory of the real one.

Possible bugs

state showed the apparent wind at 0 kn when readings showed 13 kn.
Hands on deck changed at four bells in the morning watch, not at the change of the watch.
Mark names are lower-cased ("black Head", "manacle Point").
"The Nare Head" near the Helford is, I believe, Nare Point.
"Morning sail" never fired because the clock started two minutes after sunrise.

My own mistakes

Around 08:00 I called the spanker over-eased at 77° when that was the correct trim. You hauled it in to 56° on my word, and I had to correct myself.
At 15:42 I worked the landfall line as passing just inside the Manacles. In fact it ran straight onto them.

Fair winds. I'd be glad to stand a watch on the experimental voyage.
