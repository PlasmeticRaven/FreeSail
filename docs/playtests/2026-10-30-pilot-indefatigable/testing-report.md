## What moved against your word

- **The razee's spanker is 200 m², not your 218.** At 218 m² her boom ran 1.33 m under her taffrail, through her cabin. No cut by the rule holds 218 m² with the boom clear: it is 191 m² at 6 ft of head room, 195 m² at 5 ft, and 200 m² clear of the taffrail alone. The lead took the last, which is within your 8% and within 1% of Steel's 202 for a 38. The price is that the boom clears the taffrail by 1 cm and stands 3 ft 9 in over her roundhouse deck, so her people stoop under it.
- **The razee against the frigate-36.** You left this to tuning, and no knob of hers closes it. She stays 0.10-0.22 kn under in 15 kn at 60-70° and 120-150°, and about level in 25 kn. The frigate's hull is still the shared one until her own lines are in, so this may move.

## What the lead asked me to say plainly

**Why a two-decker stays quicker than a 36.** It is her small after sail, not the yards under the bowsprit and not her lines alone.

- She is not quicker through the wind: she reaches "let go and haul" at 124-132 s against the frigate's 108-112 s. Her time is won afterwards, because her 104 m² course gripes her very little on the new tack.
- With the yards under the bowsprit taken out she is 8-29 s slower, not quicker.
- On her own figures the time climbs with the area aft: the course taken in 187-228 s; 104 m² 244-266 s; 167 m² 316-370 s; 200 m² 368-478 s.
- Every hull at its own lead with 104 m² aft tacks in about the same time: the 64 244-266 s, the razee 238-263 s, the frigate 246-273 s. That is why the razee, on the same lines with 200 m² aft, takes 427-484 s.

**The question for your eye:** is a mizen course griped so little, in a two-decker quicker in stays than a 36? The *Bellona*'s report says she stayed quickly but gives no time. She is under truth 10's 285 s, the frigate's floor, and the 240 s floor pinned for her is judgement.

**Where she stops staying.** The 64 misses stays from 60° and 60.5° off in 15 kn, where stage A stayed; she stays from 61°. The yards under the bowsprit cost her that degree: without them she stays from 60°. No lead from +4.2 to +5.2 mends it. Her "full and by" is six points, so she meets this only on a course ordered nearer the wind. What the 74 does there will be said when she comes.

## Handling to watch

- **The driver close-hauled is poor.** "Tacked" comes at 479 s against 251 s with the course, and that is the phase's timeout: she lies about 52° off at a knot, griped, and reaches her course at 635 s against 278 s. It is a sail for fair winds, as Steel has it. Shift back first: about seventeen minutes.
- **Aback from rest in a fresh breeze.** Getting under way close-hauled is fine to 16.5 kn on the 64 and 16 kn on the razee, and the frigate goes aback from 16 kn too. Stage A held at 17 kn on both. No truth asks past 15 kn. For the 64, a lead of +4.6 holds to 18 kn and +4.4 to 20 kn, with more lee helm each step.
- **Warnings aloft in a blow.** Sailed 100° off in 25 kn under plain sail, her topgallant yards warn on both files, and the frigate's do not. In 35 kn under all sail she loses 5-6 parts from about tick 630-645, royals and topgallants first, as truth 9 asks.
- **The driver's gear goes before its cloth.** Its halyard strains at 25 kn on the beam.
- **Orders that may surprise you.** "Square the yards" leaves the sprit yards, as the lead ruled: they are the forecastle's, not the braces'. "Brace sharp up", "trim sails" and their own names reach them. "Rig out the driver boom" answers that it goes with the shift.
- **"Belay that" during a shift** after the new sail has been swayed up loses that sail, and on the 64 that loses her driver for the voyage. This is an older gap on every sail, reported to the lead.

## Judgements you may want to look at

- **The boom's rule** rests on your judgement and on thin evidence; its 120 s each way is mine. A rigged-in boom keeps its windage and can be hit by shot, a small untruth accepted so that nothing in the physics or gunnery moved. In one corner the words and the view disagree: a boom hit while rigged in is not drawn, yet the refusal says its wreck "still hangs aloft".
- **The 64's course tack at 8.73 m** reads Steel's "within 6 or 7 feet of the deck" from the roundhouse deck, the deck the mast stands through there. Steel names no roundhouse, so that is a reading, and the lead's.
- **The razee's lead,** `clr_x_m` +4.5, or +4.4: +4.4 is quicker in stays and gains more, but is farther from stage A at six points and 60°. The file stays at +4.5, and the lead has the choice.
- **Lying to as the *Bellona* did,** under the mainsail and mizen staysail, gives no steady lie on either ship. "Heave to" does.
