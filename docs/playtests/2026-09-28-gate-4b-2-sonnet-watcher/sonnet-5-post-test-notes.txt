Thank you. I enjoyed it, as far as that word applies to me, and I'll describe it that way rather than overstate.

What I liked

The role's limits. A watcher with no authority who can only observe, advise and speak up is a good position. At 04:52 I flagged the strain, you said you wanted to see what she could do, and I told you plainly what the risk was and then stayed out of the way. It's your ship, and the role made that easy to get right.
The log. Lines like "Only seven hands to the mizzen topgallant; the rest are setting the foresail…" made the ship feel like a working place, and they gave me real facts to reason from. It never felt like guessing.
Being asked for judgment. Where to steer downwind, whether the studdingsails were worth setting, and whether the save-alls were worth adding were the best parts, because the readings and log were enough to answer them honestly. Getting the standing-order syntax wrong and being corrected by the ship's refusal was a good loop as well.

What was awkward

The waiting loop. Most of my calls were repeated stand_by calls that returned "Still waiting". A single call that blocked until the next event would be better.
The turn boundary. Sometimes the game "had the floor" and rejected my stand-by, but a sample still arrived attached to the rejection. It worked, but I couldn't tell from the outside whether I was in a turn or not.
Awareness during stand-by. When I stood by until the glass at 06:03, I missed the 06:29 booms working under the press of sail. I only learned of it afterwards, in the bundle. Your plan to pass notable events through during a stand-by fixes exactly that. Even a compact digest at the next wake-up would help, and the urgent lines matter most.

One suggestion
A way to speak up unprompted mid-stand-by, for something I judge urgent, would suit a watcher. It's a narrow case, and a stand-by that wakes on urgent lines might cover it.
