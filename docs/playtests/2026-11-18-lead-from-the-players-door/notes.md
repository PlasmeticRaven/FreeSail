## What failed, or a player would stumble on

1. **Captures by far ships are lines of the player's own log.** In the merchant cutter, at anchor off Falmouth on the first morning: "Rattler takes possession of the Palinure, struck to her." (07:46), "Rattler takes possession of the Hirondelle" (08:19), "Diligente takes possession of the Swift" (08:37): three prizes taken a hundred and twenty miles away, as notable lines with no lookout, no boat and no letter behind them. The far minute of the prizes hands its lines to the log the player reads (`world/prizes.py`). Any game with the world's business on can show them.
2. **Left to the game's own captain, the first action stalls.** After one order of the lead's (`give chase`) the captain stood aside, as he says; given the deck back (`captain: carry on`) he engaged again, steered "as near the wind as she will lie" with the square topsail aback, and lay at a quarter of a knot for more than an hour, the enemy seven cables off, his guns held for the book's two cables. Two urgent "Taken aback" lines, then nothing. This is the kind of thing the unbuilt captain's pass (package 88) was booked for. A player who fights her himself does not meet it; one who hands her back does.
3. **`give chase` at the player's order line sets one course and does not follow her.** The answer says the course "led 70 degrees to larboard of her"; the helm then held NE by N while the enemy passed down the quarter.
4. **The newcomer's first minutes.** *A first passage* begins with the yards square and the wind seventy degrees on the bow. `set the topsails`, `set the courses`, `steer full and by` (the natural first three) leave her aback and making about a knot for seventeen minutes, until her people box her off themselves; the log says each thing as it happens. The lead's own `brace up sharp on the starboard tack` was not understood ("'brace' was understood, but not 'sharp'"); the order is the primer's `brace sharp up on the starboard tack`, which chapter 7 gives first of all, and a player who types along with the primer does not meet any of this. A wear ordered with a knot and a half of way on fails after a quarter of an hour ("she would not come round"); with way on and a jib set it goes as it should.
5. Smaller: `buy 2 tons of tin` in the cutter's first four minutes is refused "She is not in port" while her anchor is still going down (the harbour says she is at anchor). The action scenarios open with a dozen lines of standing orders entered and "one of that name is held already" notices before anything of the sea. In the first action the cutter "makes a reply" to the private signal at 10:17 and is judged at 10:49 as making "no answer to the private signal: an enemy". `clear for action` belays the sail the scenario's own opening order was setting, and nothing sets it again. In *Agincourt Sound* a ship nobody touches is told "Land close ahead ... three cables" inside the first quarter of an hour; in *Exercising the great guns* she misses stays by her own book in the same time.
