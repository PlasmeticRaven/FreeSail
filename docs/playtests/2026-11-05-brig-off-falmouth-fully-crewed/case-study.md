# Case study: the brig off Falmouth, every station held by a different model (2026-11-05)

The owner's playtest `brig-off-falmouth-fully-crewed.json` on the build of his hotfix:
the brig *Harpy* off Falmouth on 12 June 1805, the player seated as the passenger only,
and five stations each held by a different model through the API door: the director, the
captain (Captain Bowen), the officer of the watch (Mr Pearce), the master (Mr Pascoe) and
the lookout. Four hours and a half of the ship's time (16,475 ticks), 4,701 acts of the
stations', seventeen orders of the player's. The brief to the director: cater to the world,
not to the ship, and let the story fall out from it. Read by the lead from the save as data.

## What the crew did

The captain took stock (the log, the library, his chapter, the starting book, the chart),
wrote his standing orders, asked the master for the course to clear the entrance and what
the line passed, asked the lookout to examine two strangers, told the officer to see the
hands ready to weigh and the gunner to examine the guns; sent the launch ashore with the
purser, read the shipping agent's letter the director had left at Falmouth, took its request
as a cruise toward the Lizard, weighed, boxed her off when she hung in stays, beat to
quarters and exercised the battery in dumb show before the mark, and worked her to windward
by boards off the Manacles with the master's fixes and the officer's and the master's
warnings of the inshore dangers. The master took fixes by cross bearings, hove the lead and
the log, kept his reckoning and said each to the captain in the period's form. The lookout
hailed the deck with sightings and the glass's words. The officer kept the deck when it was
given, worked the beat, called the captain at once when the stranger astern altered, and
refreshed his own reckoning. The director played the shore (a shipping agent made from
words, his letter left for the launch) and the unheld voices (the boatswain and the gunner
by `say as`), and corrected, in Mr Russell's voice, an officer who reported yard work in
hand after speech alone: "my acknowledgment will not bring the yards round."

## What held

- **A crew of five models worked as a crew**: the asks and tells between stations, the
  master's slate against the captain's, the officer's call to the captain, the lookout's
  hails, the director's voices for the people nobody held, and the player hearing all of it
  from the passenger's station.
- **The director as the world's keeper**: a letter at Falmouth with a purpose and no
  spoiler, the unheld warrant officers answered in character and in truth ("the guns are
  still secured; they must be cast loose by your order"), its running log of what it did
  and why, and its restraint ("no director action").

## What failed, and where each lesson goes

1. **The captain's word through the officer was no word.** The captain told the officer to
   see the topsails loosed and the hands ready to weigh; the officer, without the deck, gave
   the orders and was refused three times ("the officer of the watch has not the deck ... no
   order is given"), and had to say so plainly to the captain, who kept the deck through the
   departure and gave the sail orders himself. Only at 08:41, with `you have the deck` and
   `you may work the ship`, did the brig work under the officer. **Proposed as decision 63**
   (the lead to the owner, 2026-11-05): an order the captain tells a station to carry out is
   the captain's word for that thing, logged as given by the station at the captain's word;
   and the deck given when the captain leaves it (below wounded, ashore, in the boat, dead)
   is the command, the officer holding the captain's authority with the captain's standing
   orders as his guide, while the deck given with the captain on deck stays the watch. With
   65's step 3, which builds the captain's whereabouts.
2. **The lookout's hail read as `hail the pilot`.** "Hail The pilot's cutter is out of
   sight ..." was refused as an order of the ship the lookout may not give: the port's verb
   `hail the pilot` took the words before the lookout's hail could. The grammar reads a
   lookout's `hail` as his hail before any other verb; package 95's grammar work.
3. **Far events in the ship's log.** The director noticed "distant St Mary's convoy
   straggler events leaked into Harpy's log", and the captain read them as his own waters'.
   A line of a far body's story written to the player's hull's log: for 65's step 1 reach
   table (the line's place) or 84's far shape; to reproduce from the save.
4. **Speech reported as work.** The officer reported bracing in hand after telling the
   boatswain; the director corrected him. Decision 60's notice ("a thing is heard only when
   said") wants its sibling in the briefs: a word to a person is no order of the ship; the
   readings say what is in hand. 65's step 2.
5. **The master may not shape a course**, by design; the lookout's hail refused four times
   for its wording and put again in the station's form; the captain's stand-by for an hour
   refused (a glass at most): friction the models worked through, noted and not booked.
