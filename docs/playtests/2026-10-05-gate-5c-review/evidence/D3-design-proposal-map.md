## 0. The findings that matter most (for a lead in a hurry)

1. **"The lookout sees breakers" is not a breach of "never the truth".** The rule is qualified in its own sentence: "no reading ever gives the truth where the period could not have it" (`TechnicalSpec-M5.md` l.35-37; the last six words are the qualification), and the work packages name what that covers: "(position, the tide's hour, a front)" (`M5-WorkPackages.md` l.17-19). The lookout is the design's named carrier of the truth the eye has: bearing by compass, distance by estimation, bounded by horizon, visibility, daylight and the feature's own rules (spec M5 §11-§12), "notable for a landfall or a danger" (l.284-285). Falconer's own definition of the look-out is the owner's note 19 almost word for word: "to watch for any dangerous objects lying near her track, and to which she makes a gradual approach as she advances" (OCR l.11744-11746, normalised). An urgent call is an extension of a hail the game already has (`a bearing steady and closing`, primer 12 l.95).
2. **What is in tension is the second half of note 19, not the first.** The idea of "tuning the reckoning to become very precise when very close to land based on a circle around the ship" would make the account follow the truth by proximity, with no observation behind it. The design's rule: "the *player's* errors come from the model and the *world's* truth from the physics" (`Navigation1805.md` l.287-290), the account "updated by each observation" (l.279-281). The design already has the period's route to a precise account close in, and the scripted passage uses it: "the Goulet's are a cable or two, with the land in sight and the bearings every five minutes" (`data/scenarios/merchant-passage.orders` l.32-35).
3. **Three recorded build facts make close-in work worse than the design intends,** and mending any of them is carrying out the design, not relaxing it: (i) a line of position taken after two miles' run *replaces* the account across it whatever its own doubt, so a lunar "trusted within 20 miles" overwrites a two-mile account (`freesail/world/reckoning.py:220-224`, `:675-692`), where the study says a lunar "collapses the east-west axis, by that much" (`Navigation1805.md` l.245); (ii) "The account between the log's heaves swings an hour's run ... a mark three miles off is reached and left by account in a minute" (`TuningNotes.md` l.1402), where the study has the traverse board pegged each half hour (l.54-59); (iii) the officer's station is refused navigation's observations, a bearing among them (`freesail/agents/agent.py:174`, `:249`; in play, session 2 `log-full.txt` l.217 and session 4 l.6441), by a line the tuning notes call "judgement" (`TuningNotes.md` l.1420), where the Regulations give the lieutenant of the watch "the bearings and distance of some principal head-land" (art. XVII, `Navigation1805.md` l.32-36).
4. **The Harpy's last thirteen minutes show the log's severities inverted.** The only urgent lines before she struck were three "Taken aback" in a flicking air (session 2 `log-full.txt` l.8294, 8344, 8415), among thirty-three notable wind-shift lines; the lookout said nothing urgent and nothing of Penlee after a hail at three miles fifty-three minutes earlier (l.8071); the last bearing taken, twenty-seven seconds before the keel touched, read "Penlee Point bore NNE, a mile by estimation" (ticks 636493 and 636626; aground at 636653, l.8449). Notes 17, 18 and 19 are one finding about what "urgent" is spent on.
5. **Parity is already unequal in the human's favour close to land,** in the model's own words: "You con her, sir. You can see the marks against her on your chart, and my bearings are rounded to the point, so you're better placed" (session 4 `log-full.txt` l.7093). The cold review warned of exactly this: "every drawn thing is first a reading" (`ColdReview-2026-09-30.md` l.338-344). Note 24 (text) closes that gap; note 25 (images) only moves it unless 24 comes first.
6. **The "immediate danger" exception is the sources', and the gate already asks for it.** Falconer and art. XIII of the Lieutenant both carry it (primer 16 l.107); the refusal text quotes it (`freesail/agents/agent.py:165-168`) with no route to act on it; gate 5c's first ruling wanted asks whether that exception "should be built as a standing allowance rather than your word" (`gate-m5c.md` l.78). A *general* grant of authority is a different thing and touches the station-authority principle, milestone 6's captain and the consent brief.
7. **One scripted clean passage is not the proposal's test of a captain, still less of a director.** The book was written with the author's view: its Goulet waypoints are "points pricked on the chart in the deep water (twenty to forty-five metres by the chart's own grid ... a mile and a half west of the grid's shoals)" (`merchant-passage.orders` l.128-132), on a pinned wind and seed, under the rule "a scenario's book is tuned once and cheaply. It needs to bring the ship through at the pinned seed and no more" (`M5-WorkPackages.md` l.25-27). The proposal's test is M6's: "the regatta harness; parity tests. *Proves: parity; the game can be played by a model*" (`DesignProposal.md` l.551).
8. **Local note 5 is the one welfare defect in the pile**: another door's calls landing in a seated model's place breaks consent per exact weights, the journal and transcript "of its own", and puts the opt-out token in a third party's hands. It is squarely (a) and it is also the mechanism note 23 would need.

## 1. The proposal's principles, in its own words

### 1.1 What the game is, and is not

- "FreeSail is a **ticked, text-first sailing simulation** of a late-age-of-sail vessel of **any rig**, in which the ship is modelled as a **graph of individually simulated parts** (spars, sails, lines, fittings, guns, stores) worked by **individually simulated sailors**. Players and language models control the ship through the **same layered order system**" (`DesignProposal.md` §0, l.11).
- "A sailing game where every rope matters, where you can sail by giving orders like a captain or by writing the orders your crew will follow without you, and where a language model can sit at any station on the ship, from lookout to captain." (§1, l.21)
- The seven pillars, "used to settle arguments later; if a feature serves none of these, it waits" (§1, l.23): bottom-up realism ("Behaviour emerges from parts and physics, not from scripted outcomes", l.25); any rig (l.26); orders at every altitude (l.27); parity (l.28, quoted below); "**Dynamic, not authored.** The world runs whether or not the player is looking. Content is systems, not levels." (l.29); "**Depth over gloss.** Text and schematic graphics that tell the truth beat 3D that lies. Performance and budget go into simulation." (l.30); "**Period-plausible, not period-pedantic.** If it was done, or physically could have been done, in roughly 1780–1820, it is allowed. Things that never could have been done are not." (l.31)
- "**Non-goals (for now).** Real-time 3D rendering ... Authored campaign. Public multiplayer server. Faithful replica of one specific historical vessel or year." (l.33)
- "the game is built to be good played actively" (§2.1, l.49: the slower, leave-her-to-sail style "is a consequence of the design, not a goal of it").
- The owner's first page: "Consider scope for achievability, but be bold in planning for depth." (`InitialDesignBrainstorm.txt` l.12)

### 1.2 Text first, and the log as the primary output

- "The organising principle is: **the ship's log is the game.** Every change in the world is an event. The text log, the map, the profile view, replays, LLM narrators, LLM players, and future multiplayer all consume the same event stream and query the same state." (§0, l.13)
- "**The log.** Primary. Ship's-log style, with severity levels and filtering (routine, notable, urgent). Time-compression rolls up routine." (§6.1, l.337)
- "The log is boring but correct, precise, and the ground truth. The viewer gives the player's actions a context that is not purely text ... It never invents state; everything it animates is driven by simulation data." (§6.1 principle 7, l.346)
- "Text and data remain the baseline: nothing here may be the only way to know something, because a language model at a station reads the same log and readings the human does." (`Presentation.md` l.4-7)
- "The console and the browser are text first, and a language model at a station has exactly what the human has, so the test is the same for both: is there a line for it." (`InwardAndOutward.md` l.21-23) "This is the same test as the log's: if the log cannot say it, the game did not do it." (l.73-74)

### 1.3 What a player and a model may know

- The account, never the truth: "Dead reckoning is the default; the player's position on the map is *where the ship thinks it is*, updated by lead, log, landmarks, noon sights, and (optionally) a chronometer or lunar. The true position exists only in the simulation. Getting lost is possible. ... The baseline is uncompromising: no true-position readout in the default game. A "casual" navigation mode that shows the true position is a later option, not the default" (§5.2, l.320; decision 5, l.571).
- The rule as milestone 5 states it: "**The world keeps the truth and the captain keeps his account,** for position and for the tide alike; no reading ever gives the truth where the period could not have it, and the viewer never draws it." (`TechnicalSpec-M5.md` l.35-37) "No reading gives the truth where the period could not have it (position, the tide's hour, a front); the viewer never draws it; the tests read it from the world." (`M5-WorkPackages.md` l.17-19)
- "The one rule that keeps this honest is that the *player's* errors come from the model and the *world's* truth from the physics" (`Navigation1805.md` l.287-290).
- What the senses give: "sightings with bearing and range uncertainty, other ships at the fidelity your lookouts can actually see" (§6.1, l.338); "the map at lookout fidelity" as an on-demand query for a model (§7.4, l.415); "Squalls are events with a footprint and a life; a lookout can see one coming" (§3.4, l.138); "Day and night, moon phase, visibility. These are inputs to lookouts, navigation, and standing orders." (l.141) The director alone sees "the world as it is, not as any lookout sees it" (§7.1, l.381).
- What the captain has, and no more: "He has what the captain has, the log and the readings, no more, and the same library." (primer 16, l.7) "It observes what the captain observes (the log and the readings), no more" (`TechnicalSpec-M4.md` §12, l.164).
- Parity: "**Parity.** Humans and LLMs see the same information and have the same actions. No hidden API for machines, no hidden UI for humans." (pillar 4, l.28) "Every actor, human or machine, interacts with the ship through one channel: **submit an order**. ... This is how parity is enforced structurally rather than by good intentions." (§4.1, l.167) "Readings are the same readings the log reports and the LLM can query. There are no hidden values." (§4.3, l.246) "**Parity is structural**, as in milestone 4: a reading in the registry or nowhere." (`M5-WorkPackages.md` l.23)
- The cold review's caution on it: "Parity is with the console player, not the browser player. The map and the ship view are the human's alone. That is fine for a watcher and probably fine for an officer ... it will matter for a captain who must judge a lee shore ... the reckoned position and its ellipse must be a reading and a log line before they are a drawing, or the model captain is blind where the human is not. The M5 spec says the viewer never draws the truth; it should also say every drawn thing is first a reading." (`ColdReview-2026-09-30.md` l.338-344)
- The owner's own extension of it: "Nothing can be audio-only, for LLM parity." (`General notes.txt` l.8)

### 1.4 The stations and their authority

- "At any point, you can hand a station to a language model: "Mr. Pellew, you have the deck" gives an LLM the same order channel you have, scoped to the authority of that station." (§2.1, l.47)
- "Giving an LLM "the deck" means giving it the authority of the officer of the watch: it can issue level 0–2 orders within that authority but cannot, say, alter the course the captain has ordered without asking." (§3.5, l.153)
- The roles table (§7.1, l.376-381): watcher ("Nothing (or commentary into a side channel)"); crew/officer ("Orders within a station's authority (a lieutenant can trim sail; cannot change the ordered course)"); captain ("Everything the human player could"); director ("World orders ... never direct orders to the player's crew", "After parity (M6+)"). "Each step reuses the previous one's plumbing and adds only authority." (l.385)
- "Standing orders have **priority and authority**: an officer's standing order cannot countermand the captain's. Conflicts are logged, not silently resolved." (§4.3, l.248)
- As built: "the officer of the watch is a station with authority under the same contract. ... The officer's domain is data on the station and a filter per order ... a station left by accident is seated again once." (decision 33, l.622) "A captain's station is milestone 6's." (`TechnicalSpec-M5.md` l.601)

### 1.5 The director

- "an LLM that sits *above* the game rather than in it. It sees the world at true fidelity" (§7.6, l.429). "**It acts only through plausible causes.** The director cannot move the player's ship, change a sail, or countermand an order; it can only change the world the ship is in, and only in ways the simulation could have produced on its own." (l.434) "**Its actions are visible after the fact.**" (l.435) "It is the natural home for the more capable model, since it makes few decisions and each one matters" (l.436). "The player writes the brief; the director keeps it. The brief is the guardrail" (l.437).
- "**The director fills gaps, not holes.** ... A director can send the cutter; it cannot make the message appear on the table." (`InwardAndOutward.md` l.35-38)

### 1.6 The sources as the test of realism

- "The catalogue will be built against period and near-period seamanship texts rather than from memory." (§4.4, l.278) Luce, Lever, Falconer named (l.280-282). "Each evolution's data file cites the chapter and section it was drawn from, so that a human or an LLM officer can read the same passage the game encodes." (l.284)
- The working rules: "every number names its source, physics before rules, deterministic, ship's-log voice" (`M5-WorkPackages.md` l.9-11). "**Practices with a price come as pairs, in the period's own form.**" (decision 21, l.598)
- The primer: "Every word it uses is one that Falconer, Lever or Luce used" (`primer/README.md` l.3).
- The cold review on the ledger: "every number with a source or a confession" (l.15-16), and its warning on the one place this slipped: "That is tuning to a narrative rather than to a source" (l.124-130).

### 1.7 The welfare stance toward models

- "Before a model is asked to take a station, a fresh session of it is asked whether it is willing, and the transcript is kept ... a literal opt-out token parsed by the harness before the game sees the text, a fixed brief head ..., welfare stops judged on game state and not on prose, an explicit *stand by* action so silence is a decision, an agent journal saved with the game, in-world text never passed as an operator instruction, and transcripts never used for training." (§7.2, l.399; decisions 16-18, l.588-592)
- The seven commitments "which the harness ... must implement rather than merely state" (`agents/README.md` l.13-21): disclosure; opt-out ("unconditional in effect"); welfare controls, graduated, "judged on the game's state ... and not on the look of its prose"; no override by in-world text; "An audit trail of its own"; use of transcripts; nothing real.
- The owner's standing rules: "consent is sought from the very specific model asked and never generalised to a similar one" (l.23). The re-ask rule: a change in the consent brief's opening, "What an instance would see and do", "Leaving", "Being stopped", "What is not done" or "The journal (its use)" "puts the question again" (l.11).
- "**The local model is the function baseline** ... Every record-keeping feature (the journal, the roll-up in the samples, the handover) is built for the local model first" (`TechnicalSpec-M4.md` §24 item 10, l.252).

### 1.8 Scope discipline, milestones and gates

- "if a feature serves none of these, it waits" (l.23). Risk table: "Scope | Everything above | The milestone plan below, and the pillars as tie-breakers" (§10, l.531).
- "Each milestone ends in something you can run, and in a **gate** ... passed by the owner before the next milestone starts ... Dates are deliberately absent; order is what matters." (§11, l.537)
- "from now on gates lag the work rather than block it (each package written as its predecessor lands and launched on the owner's word, a gate cut when its last package lands and run when the owner can, its rulings a follow-up package, as 5a's were 31b)" (decision 29, l.614).
- A gate report says "what is deliberately *not* in this milestone, so it is not reported as a fault" (`gates/README.md` l.14).
- "**Everything inward must be reachable by an order or a reading; everything outward must arrive at the ship through something the ship already models.**" (`InwardAndOutward.md` l.17-18) "either can spiral without end ... Whichever way a milestone goes, it needs a bounded goal" (l.10-13).

**1. The pilot and transfers between vessels. (d), with a baseline that fits.** Built today, as a judgement: "Within four cables she hails ... Within two cables, with your way under six knots, he boards" (primer 14 l.61); `ports.PILOT_BOARDS_UNDER_KN` "six knots | "shorten sail and he will come aboard" | judgement" (`TuningNotes.md` l.1246). So the owner's second thought (boarding beside a ship making little way) is the built baseline, and it is period-plausible under pillar 7. The fuller behaviour he describes, a pilot vessel with "a goal to come alongside the target ship", is M6's: "the crewed model and the rules-based captain that would fill it are M6's" (spec M5 l.533-535); "NPCs are rules-based by default and LLM-captained selectively" (§7.3, l.409). The director is M7b's. His staging ("baseline functionality offline ... see how the director can improve edge cases") is how the proposal itself stages: "build the world's *mechanisms* from milestone one, populate them thinly" (§5.1, l.305); "The director fills gaps, not holes" (`InwardAndOutward.md` l.35); "Scope grows from the baselines, not before them" (`Scenario-StrikingColours.md` l.92). **The one caution:** "allow for the director to take charge if necessary and ensure things happen smoothly" must mean a world order to the cutter, never the transfer done by fiat. "The director cannot move the player's ship, change a sail, or countermand an order" (§7.6, l.434); "A director can send the cutter; it cannot make the message appear on the table" (`InwardAndOutward.md` l.37-38). General ship-to-ship transfer of people is a gap the plan does not yet hold (`Scenario-StrikingColours.md` l.53-57).

**2. The well. (a).** The proposal names `the well` among the readings a rule compares, with "There are no hidden values" (§4.3, l.246), sketches `sound the well` and "when the well exceeds 2 feet then man the pumps" (l.235-238), and lists it among the instruments (§6.1, l.358). M4 deferred it to this milestone: "Sightings, soundings, the well and the glass as readings: milestone 5." (`TechnicalSpec-M4.md` l.246) Package 34's brief put "the well rising, the pumps" among grounding's consequences (`M5-WorkPackages.md` l.1317-1320). As built the log says the well's rise by the foot (primer 13 l.39) while the reading answers "The ship has no well to sound yet; that reading comes with the world." (primer 10 l.144). By `InwardAndOutward.md` that is a thing the log speaks of and no order or reading reaches: "is there a line for it" (l.23). Acting on it is carrying out the proposal. The pumps are named "a later milestone's" (primer 13 l.39) without saying which.

**3. The price list. (a).** "The ship's papers are things in the world ... in-world data, like the log" (`Papers-and-Books.md` l.15-18). The worked example gives the shape: "a paper kept aboard that the library holds by handle and that is only as current as its last entry" (`InwardAndOutward.md` l.59-61). Today `the prices` is "the last list the boat brought off" (primer 14 l.281). A merchant's price book with a dated entry for each port visited is that same paper kept properly; each entry arrives by the boat, which is the carrier rule kept.

**4. The primer. (a); the timing is M8's.** What the primer is for: "The same text is what a player reads and what a language-model officer is given as its documentation, so it says exactly what the game does and no more. Where a real sailing master would do something the game cannot yet do, the chapter says so." (`primer/README.md` l.5) "Writing it once for humans and reusing it for machines is a parity feature, not a shortcut." (§6.2, l.366) The owner has ruled on the same fault once: "the primer must not narrate the gate's day" (decision 28, l.612). The README still roots the book in one run: "copied from a run with seed 7 and a north wind of 15 knots with the physics as tuned by package 10 ... so that your log matches this book line for line until the first gust" (l.26), and chapter 7 "is the checklist rewritten as a passage" (l.51). The time the note names, "the end of the current milestones", is where the proposal puts "the tutorial; the reference library as a proper in-client book" (M8, l.557; M4 §24 item 5). Two things to keep when it is done: every `orders` block is run by `tests/test_primer.py` (l.22), and the reference is "a promise, not a possession" (`Papers-and-Books.md` l.8) named in the consent brief's terms, so it may be made leaner but not less reachable. A leaner primer also serves the smallest reader: a whole chapter costs a local model "up to about 9,710" tokens (`Harness.md` l.130).

**5. The scripted passage as evidence. (d), and the inference does not carry.** Who the director is: "the natural home for the more capable model" (§7.6, l.436); "the director (milestone 7b) is the real test and probably beyond a local model" (`TechnicalSpec-M4.md` l.252). How a model's play is to be tested: "a **regatta harness**: a fixed seed, a fixed course, and a set of agents (rules-based, local model, Claude, human replay) sailing it ... This is how we tune the physics for honesty, tune the documentation for the models, and check that parity holds." (§7.5, l.423), with "adversarial and contradictory probes, so that the data shows when a model disagrees and whether it was right, not only whether it complied" (`ConsentAndPreferences.md` l.24). A test designed for the director itself: **not found**; the nearest are its journaled, replayable world orders (§7.6, l.433) and its brief as guardrail (l.437). Against that, what the clean passage shows: a book written by a builder who had the author's view ("The account against the truth, at seed 7 (the author's view; the game never shows it)", `gate-m5c.md` l.43); waypoints pricked from the truth's depth grid ("by the chart's own grid ... a mile and a half west of the grid's shoals", `merchant-passage.orders` l.128-132); rules written round a known artefact ("Every `when the distance to ... is under` rule of the passages' books is written for it", `TuningNotes.md` l.1402); a pinned wind; and the instruction to stop at one working seed (`M5-WorkPackages.md` l.25-30). That is good evidence of skill at level 2 of the control ladder ("LLMs setting policy", §2.2, l.59). It is not evidence of captaincy under the account with nothing but the log and the readings, and it says nothing of a director, whose work is world orders "through plausible causes" under a player's brief. The owner's own word, "simple speculative", is the right weight. Two further gates stand before either station: the cold review's six items "before a captain's station is offered to a model" (l.352-378), and consent, which is per exact weights and was reserved for these stations by one model in so many words; no record under `docs/agents/consent/` in the m5c tree is named for Fable.

**6. "NaN fm" soundings. (a) a fault.** The captain's chart is to show "the soundings with their ground" (spec M5 l.395-396); the viewer "never invents state" (§6.1, l.346).

**7. The track by account. (a).** The session sketch has "your dead-reckoning track" on the map (§2.1, l.41); the chart draws "the track by account, the noon positions, the bearings taken" (spec M5 l.395-396). The limit is a recorded judgement: `reckoning.TRACK_KEPT` "168 hourly positions | a week of the track for the chart | judgement" (`TuningNotes.md` l.824). Nothing in the design asks for the track to be shortened. (The code appends a position at each observation as well as each hour, `freesail/world/reckoning.py:662-664`, `:765`, so a passage with bearings every few minutes would use the 168 far sooner than a week; that is for the code readers.)

**8. The 1x button and the anchor in the state window. (e) and (a).** On the button's place the proposal is silent. On the anchor: "`the anchor` says down, riding to which tide ... `the cable` says the scope ... Both stand in `state` while she is at anchor." (primer 13 l.101) If the console's `state` has it and the browser's window has not, that is "no hidden UI for humans" read the other way.

**9. Accepting or refusing the pilot. (d).** Signals are a later milestone's: "Flag hoists, guns, lights, with a codebook per nation" (§5.2, l.319), in §9's list (l.512) for M8, with colours and the private signal named for M7 (`gate-m5c.md` l.91). At 5c "there this milestone stops: what follows a chase, the gun and the boat and the prize, is milestone 7's" (primer 15 l.41). The period's form is in the primer already: the Scilly pilots come off "as soon as the signal for that purpose is made" (White, quoted primer 14 l.219). A baseline now is one order in the existing grammar (to make the signal for a pilot, or to decline him), which keeps "one channel" and waits on nothing; the game already has the event `at the pilot refused` for a port that refuses the ship (primer 14 l.292).

**10. A station must be able to read its journal. (a).** The journal's stated work: "so that its exit report has a record behind it" (`agents/README.md` l.19), and more: "On a very long voyage no model's context outlasts the game; what carries a voyage across instances is the game's records (the log and its roll-up, the book, the journal each instance keeps)" (`TechnicalSpec-M4.md` l.250). "Claude's own window keeps itself; the journal carries the habit." (`Harness.md` l.233) A journal the returning instance cannot read fails the second purpose exactly when it is wanted. The tool list of spec M4 has `journal(note)` to write and nothing to read (l.154); the reading is the captain's (`show the officer's journal`). The form of the owner's that failed (`show the officers journal`, with no apostrophe) is an alias the grammar lacks; the documented ones are `show the officer's journal`, `the officer's journal`, `show the journal of the officer` (primer 16 l.101).

**11. The journal out of the context. (b), with a (c) edge.** In the shelf's spirit: "a read is a book taken off the shelf that the reader puts back" (`agents/README.md` l.57). But the design gives the journal the opposite job: "the journal is where a reader keeps what it took from the page" (`Papers-and-Books.md` l.52-53; the brief says the same). If the page is shelved and the note is shelved too, nothing the reader took is before it. So it can be done without breaking anything only if: note 10 is done first (the journal readable by its owner at any time); each sample says the journal is there (the model's own suggestion, comment 11); the handover note still stands in the conversation as the consent brief says ("the note then stands in the conversation in place of the older exchanges", `ConsentBrief.md` l.23); and "nothing in the game acts on what is written there" stays true. It is also a change in a section the re-ask rule watches: "so does a change in **The journal** (its use), which the record of 2026-09-29 for the second of the two models asked names beside the transcripts" (`agents/README.md` l.11); that model's words were "or any change to how transcripts or journals are used" (`consent/2026-09-29-sonnet-5.5.md` l.14). A log line that an entry was made is consistent with "it is a record and not a secret" (`ConsentBrief.md` l.23). The OpenRouter door the note names does not exist yet (decision 31, l.618).

**12. Re-seating without limit. (c) in the letter; resolved.** The letter: "a station left by accident is seated again once" (decision 33, l.622); "an instance that left by accident may be seated again once in the same game, by the same identity, the log saying so" (`ConsentBrief.md` l.19). The limit was never a model's condition. Opus 5.5 asked only that the brief say "whether an instance that left by accident can be seated again" (`consent/2026-09-29-opus-5.5.md` l.18), and the tuning notes record the number as "the owner's reading of the note" (`TuningNotes.md` l.1427). What must hold for the limit to go: the token still ends the instance's part at once; a leaving that was meant is still held to; the brief says the new rule; and, "Leaving" being a watched section, every model is asked again. The m5c-b package does each: stood down or handed over, seated again as often as asked; left by the token, "this question is put to it again first, so a token written by accident costs one answer and a leaving that was meant is held to"; `opt_out` with `final`, not again (`m5c-b` `ConsentBrief.md` l.19), and "every model with a yes on record is asked again at its next door" (`CHANGES-m5c-b.md`). One gap left: the proposal's decisions log has no entry for it; decision 33 still says "once" in the m5c-b tree.

**13. Multi-condition stand-by. Parity (a); "or" (b).** "An agent may answer "no orders" with a wake condition (a bell, a sighting, a reading crossing a value)." (decision 18, l.592) The last of those, "a reading crossing a value", is in the decision and the stand-by has not got it. The standing rule on parity between the two: "parity: the dialect gets the same events for nothing where it lacks them" (`M5-WorkPackages.md` l.335-336). A list of conditions joined by "or" is no more than the language allows: "no variables, no arithmetic beyond comparisons, no loops" (§4.2, l.187). The officer's bound must survive it: "a stand-by with the deck must name an event or a bell" (`Harness.md` l.258), and "an urgent line wakes it at once" whatever is named.

**14. Restarting Claude Desktop. (d).** "The game is where the player is; the doors are clients." (decision 23, l.602) The friction is known and placed: M4 §24 item 6 (l.248), the `freesail` command and setup step (32d) and the launcher page (32f) of decision 31 (l.618); the cold review called the install simplification "deferred too long" (l.444-448). Part of it is the protocol's: "The protocol tells the bridge the name of the application but not which model is answering" (`Harness.md` l.70), and "When Claude Desktop closes ... the bridge releases the station" (l.90). The m5c-b fix to a station loaded from a checkpoint is in the same line of work.

**15. A general grant of authority. (c); its emergency clause (a).** Treated at length in §3.6 below.

**16. A "keep" prefix. (b).** The proposal's own example is a standing order of that name, "keep her full" (§4.3, l.231-233), and "starter standing orders that ship with the game" (§2.3, l.68). Conditions for it to be the design and not a second mechanism: one engine ("The dialect and the Python API build the same rule, enter the same book and fire through the same `submit`", decision 22, l.600); hands and time, since free tending was "the one place where "orders take time and hands" is quietly untrue" (cold review l.111-118) and its remedy was "the afterguard tending sheets as routine work at a cadence the book or the watch orders" (spec M5 §33 item 13, l.698); belayable; and guarded against the risk the proposal names, "Standing orders thrash (set, take in, set, take in) ... Durations on conditions, minimum dwell time on evolutions, and a conflict log" (§10, l.529). At the officer's station a "keep" is a standing order "in his own rank" with the captain's standing over it.

**17. "Taken aback" urgent in a calm. (a).** "Urgent" has work to do in the design: it wakes a station ("an urgent line in the log wakes it at once", `Harness.md` l.41) and it slows the game ("an urgent line ... drops the compression to a floor", `TechnicalSpec-M4.md` l.250). A line that is urgent when nothing can be lost spends both. Done in m5c-b's 37c ("urgent only when there was something to lose", `CHANGES-m5c-b.md`).

**18. Wind-shift lines. (a).** It is an open item of the specification in so many words: "`a wind shift` as a stand-by event reads the mean wind while the log's own `wind.shift` line reads the instant wind at two points, two definitions to unify when `core/world.py` is next open" (spec M5 §33 item 11, l.696). Decision 26 already counted "a chatty log" among a playtest's findings (l.608). Done in m5c-b ("which closes the M5 spec's open item 11").

**19. The reckoning near visible land.** Lookout call **(a)**; proximity-precise reckoning **(c)**. See §3.5.

**20. A compass rose, lines and markers on the chart. (b); (c) if they are the human's alone.** The tool is the period's (parallel rulers and dividers on the chart), so pillar 7 is content. The tension is parity: "nothing here may be the only way to know something" (`Presentation.md` l.5-6); "every drawn thing is first a reading" (cold review l.344). A human who can lay a rule from any point to any point has an instrument a model lacks unless the same measure is a query; today the chart's readings are from the account to a named feature only (`the bearing of <mark> by the chart`, `the distance to <mark>`, primer 12 l.83). It is in the design's spirit when each measurement the tool makes is also a line of words, and the captain's marks on the chart are either private (as a model's journal is) or readable by a station.

**21. Turns and budgets.** Budget **(a)**; the turn rule **(b)** with a welfare edge. See §3.7.

**22. The cutter's square sail. (a) or (d).** The ship view is "always a *true* picture of the part graph" and "realistic in proportion and colour" (§6.1, l.339). If the viewer draws the sail other than the file has it, it is a fault of truth now. If the file's sail is short, it is the vessel library's: the cutter carries "no verification of her own until milestone 8 makes her a reference ship" (spec M5 l.499-500), and the small vessels' drawing is on a deferred list ("the storm canvas drawn as itself, the bowsprit redrawn ... deferred with its findings recorded", decision 30, l.616).

**23. Several stations through one door.** **(c)** on identity. See §3.7.

**24. Parity near a coast.** **(a)**. See §3.5.

**B3.** A ship that manoeuvres to close or to keep station is M6's (as C1). The symptom, though, reads like a rule working as written. ### 3.5 Notes 19 and 24, and the model's comment on 19

**What the design says a seaman on deck in clear weather knows without a chart.**

1. *What is in sight, where it bears, and about how far.* "The sighting model is the lookout's reading: what is in sight, its bearing by compass and its distance by estimation, in the log's words ... in 5b it is the world's own voice at routine severity, notable for a landfall or a danger." (spec M5 §12, l.279-285) It is bounded by what an eye could do: "the features within the geographic horizon from the masthead ... filtered by the 0.25° index, then by 5a's visibility and daylight and by the feature's own rules" (§11, l.270-273).
2. *How far and which way the nearest shore lies.* The chart study makes it the second of the four questions the map exists to answer, and gives it to the lookout: "*how far and which way is the nearest shore* (for the lookout and the log's "the land bearing north-east, distant four leagues")" (`ChartData.md` l.25-28). "**Nearest coast.** The distance field gives how far in one read; the direction from its gradient across the neighbouring cells, or, when the log wants a name, the nearest tagged coast piece from the features index." (l.340-343)
3. *That a danger lies near her track.* Falconer, LOOK-OUT (`docs/references/falconer/...ocr.txt` l.11737-11748, normalised): "a watchful attention to some important object, or event, which is expected to arise from the present situation of a ship ... principally used in navigation, when there is a probability of danger from the real or supposed proximity of land, rocks, enemies ... There is always a look-out kept on a ship's forecastle at sea, to watch for any dangerous objects lying near her track, and to which she makes a gradual approach as she advances". And BREAKERS (l.3185-3190, normalised): "distinguished both by their appearance and sound, as they cover that part of the sea with a perpetual foam, and produce a hoarse and terrible roaring".
4. *That a bearing is steady and the distance closing.* Already a hail: "`a bearing steady and closing`, his hail of a danger (or the land within a league) whose bearing has held within a point for ten minutes while the distance closed, which is the seaman's rule for a collision course" (primer 12 l.95; `TuningNotes.md` l.1065).
5. *The state of what he sees.* The tide is exposed "by the period's means only", among them "the landmark's state (the Black Rock shows at half tide)" (spec M5 l.385-388).
6. *That the account is wrong, when the land says so.* "If the account is six miles east of the truth, the course laid off for Falmouth brings the ship to the land six miles west of it, and the first thing the lookout raises tells you so." (primer 10 l.119)

What he does **not** know: his latitude and longitude ("The world knows where she is, to the yard, and never tells you", primer 10 l.3); the true distance ("the distance is the master's estimate ... never the truth", l.92); what does not show (a rock that neither dries nor breaks is the lead's: the master is on deck near land "always sounding to inform himself of the situation of the Ship", Master art. XXVI, `Navigation1805.md` l.23-25); and anything at all in fog, or beyond a mile on a dark night (primer 12 l.99). The design frames these arrivals as its outward carriers: "the lookout's hails, the lead's cast, the noon sight and the bearing are the outward arrivals of this gate, each a log line and a reading" (`M5-CloseOut-5b.md` l.28).

**So, is "the lookout sees breakers" a breach?** No. It is the truth the design says the senses deliver, by the carrier the design names for it, in the form the design gives it (relative, estimated, bounded by sight). The breach would be a reading of position, or a call for something no eye could see.

**Note 19, first half: an urgent lookout call when the true course stands into visible land or a danger. (a), as an extension of a hail that exists.** What is new against the built rule is its severity (the specification stops at "notable"), its timing by speed, and its reach to "any arbitrary piece of land" and not only named features. The owner's own bounds ("a visible, marked danger or a clear coastline", "clamped by the visibility") are the design's. Conditions that keep it inside the principle:

- It speaks as the eye does: a bearing or "right ahead", a distance by estimation, never a position and never a correction to the account made of itself.
- It is for what shows. A shoal with nothing breaking on it stays the lead's business, or truth 66 loses its meaning ("Standing on by account ... toward the Manacles in thick weather grounds the schooner on the ebb ...; the same passage with the lead going hourly does not", spec M5 l.423).
- It warns and does not steer. This was decided once: "the helm rules of the book have no guard, and that is the captain's business, so no rule is added" (`M5-WorkPackages.md` l.1148-1149).
- A vector from the true track is a fair way to *compute* what a lookout perceives (a steady bearing and a closing range is exactly a track that meets the object), so long as only the perception is said.

*What the log shows.* The Harpy had the existing hail at a league and nothing of that land after: "Penlee Point bearing NE, steady and closing: distant three miles" at 12:57 (session 2 `log-full.txt` l.8071), "Cawsand ... steady and closing" at 13:04 (l.8114), then for the forty-six minutes she ran in only routine sightings of marks three to five miles off and one notable line for a rock four miles away ("The Shagstone bearing E by N, distant four miles: a danger", l.8385). In the last thirteen minutes the log carried thirty-three notable wind-shift lines and three urgent "Taken aback" (l.8294, 8344, 8415); the lead told the truth ("11, 9½, 5½, now 4½ fathoms in six minutes", the officer's note at tick 636618); the last bearing, at tick 636626, read "a mile by estimation"; she struck at 636653 on "two fathoms of water by the chart". The first urgent word about the ground was the grounding itself ("Taking the ground is an urgent line and a stop", `M5-CloseOut-5b.md` l.42). The gap is the one the note names: the hail is made once at a league, at notable severity, for a named mark; nothing spoke again as the range closed inside a mile, and the two-fathom ground she took was hailed by no one.

**Note 19, second half: "tuning the reckoning to become very precise when very close to land based on a circle around the ship". (c).** The principle: the account is "updated by each observation as a linear line-or-point measurement" (`Navigation1805.md` l.279-281), and "the *player's* errors come from the model and the *world's* truth from the physics ... the difference between them is what the player discovers" (l.287-290). A precision that grows with nearness alone is the truth leaking into the account with no observation to carry it, and it would say "by account" over a number that is not one. It is also the convenience the proposal sets its baseline against ("uncompromising", §5.2). The owner's earlier ruling is the pattern to follow: when a storm cost canvas, "the night's cost in canvas is answered by the crew and not by easing the storm" (decision 28, l.612). Here: answered by the lookout, the lead and the bearings, not by easing the reckoning.

*What would have to be true for the wish to be met without breaking it:* the precision arrives as observations, each a line in the log with the period's error. The design has them and they are sharp close in: "Bearing of a landmark | a line of position | a degree or two by compass, which at ten miles is a quarter mile; two bearings a fix; a transit exact" (`Navigation1805.md` l.247). The scripted passage shows the result: "the Goulet's are a cable or two, with the land in sight and the bearings every five minutes" (`merchant-passage.orders` l.32-35), by `standing order "pilot water": every 5 minutes, if ... the land is in sight then take a bearing of the land` and `"the lead in pilot water"` (l.147-148), on the authority of "the Regulations of 1806, the Pilot's articles; the Master's art. XXIX" (l.139-140). The game already makes the master's habits automatic where the period did (the log hove hourly, the noon sight, the chronometer wound), so bearings kept of themselves while the land is in sight in pilot water would be a habit of the same kind: (b), by the same carrier. Three things recorded in the build work against close-in precision today, and mending them is (a):

- *The account between heaves.* "`Navigation.account_now` runs the last worked position on at the log's last read along the traverse board's mean heading since ..., so as the ship turns, or lies aback, the account swings up to the hour's run and a mark three miles off is reached and left by account in a minute." (`TuningNotes.md` l.1402) The study has the period doing better: "The helmsman pegs the course sailed each half hour on the traverse board" and "The traverse table reduces the watch's courses and distances to one difference of latitude and one departure" (`Navigation1805.md` l.54-59). This is an error no master of 1805 would have made, so it is not one of "the player's errors" the model is meant to draw.
- *A coarse sight overwriting a tight account* (the model's first point, below).
- *The officer may not take a bearing.* Navigation is refused to the officer's station ("the reckoning, the sights and the course shaped are the master's for the captain", `freesail/agents/agent.py:174`, `:249`; "`take a bearing of` kept the master's", `TuningNotes.md` l.1455, package 37's "Not done"), by a line the notes call "judgement on art. XXVI of the Master" (l.1420). The Regulations give the lieutenant of the watch his own account with "the bearings and distance of some principal head-land" (art. XVII, `Navigation1805.md` l.32-36), and Falconer has him "superintending the navigation" (primer 16 l.107). See §3.6.

**The model's comment on 19, first point: "A worse figure shouldn't replace a better one." (a).** The study's table says what each observation does: a lunar "collapses the east-west axis, by that much" (`Navigation1805.md` l.245); the specification, "the simplest Kalman form" (spec M5 l.293). In that form a measurement never widens what was known. The study also leaves the choice to judgement: "Whether to believe the lunar or the chronometer when they disagree by twenty miles is exactly the judgment the period's captains made" (l.394-396), and calls the lunar "a coarse but unbiased check" (l.421-422). The build departs from this on purpose: a line taken after more than `FIX_RUN_NM` (two miles, "judgement") of run "*replaces* the account across it, the prior's doubt across the line taken as unbounded", for the reason that "a weighing by that doubt would let a bad account outvote a good sight (N §3, the Apollo)" (`freesail/world/reckoning.py:220-224`, `:682-692`); `update_longitude` uses it for the lunar (`:734-739`, called at `:2001`), and the primer teaches the result ("The reckoning's east-west doubt collapses to the sight's", primer 12 l.36). The reason is sound for a *good* sight; Falconer's word is "any good observation of the sun" (`Navigation1805.md` l.103-105). For a sight twenty miles wide against an account two miles wide it is the Apollo error turned about: a bad sight outvoting a good account. The model drew the conclusion the design hopes a captain will ("the land itself will tell us which reckoning was right", session 4 `log-full.txt` l.1403), but the game's account, chart and dangers list had already gone with the lunar.

**Second point: "Once the land is in sight, [the dangers list] should come from the best fix, whether bearings or the lookout." (c) as worded; answered inside the design.** The principle it meets: the chart in the captain's hands is "three readings that read the account and never the truth, in sight or not" (primer 12 l.83). The design keeps two lists apart on purpose: `the dangers` by the master from the account, and `what is in sight` by the lookout's eye, which "names the dangers first, then the lights, the land and the marks, nearest first within each" (l.99). To move the first onto the second's source would blur which is the account and which the eye. What the model wants is had without that: (i) with the first point mended and bearings kept, the account near land *is* the best fix, and `the dangers` follow it; (ii) the officer's brief head is specified to carry "the chart's dangers by account" by name (`M5-WorkPackages.md` l.1696-1698); given the same standing there, the lookout's dangers-first reading would be read beside it, each saying its source, which is the design's two lists shown as two; (iii) `the dangers` can carry the master's doubt in his own words, which is the study's form ("I would not trust the reckoning within twenty miles east or west", `Navigation1805.md` l.283-285); (iv) when a mark in sight bears far from where the account puts it, a master would say so, which is the design's "the first thing the lookout raises tells you so" made a line.

**Note 24: "nearest land" in the readings, and features named by their parts. (a).** A `nearest coast` query with "a name from the features index" is in the specification (spec M5 l.270) and the chart study gives it to the lookout (above); the reading `the land` exists as "whether any land is in sight, and the nearest" (primer 10 l.171). What the playtest asks is that it say enough ("the west end of the Isle of Bas, two cables E") and be in the sample. The study's "the nearest tagged coast piece" (`ChartData.md` l.342) is the note's "sighted features named by their parts" in other words. Parity makes it more than a nicety: a human sees the charted coast as a shape against his reckoned ship; a model has named points and "bearings ... rounded to the point" (session 4 l.7093); the cold review asked that "every drawn thing is first a reading". The owner's word "generalizable" is pillar 5 ("Content is systems, not levels"): a rule that names the ends and sides of any coast piece serves every island, where hand-naming is the "week of reading and tracing that cannot be delegated to a grid" the cold review warned of (l.387-388). Two conditions: it is the lookout's, so by estimation and only in sight (the land "close aboard in thick weather before any headland is made out, is hailed as the land ... but it is no mark to take a bearing of", primer 10 l.94); and where the chart's account is meant instead, the words say "by account".

### 3.6 Note 15, the model's comment on it, and the gate's ruling on the officer's domain

**What is drawn.** "Sail handling, the yards, the lines, the lead and the log, the lookout, and his own standing orders; not the course, a manoeuvre, the anchor, all hands, the people or the port unless you allow a named thing in your words." The gate asks the owner to "Rule whether the line is where a captain of 1805 would draw it for a lieutenant with the deck (Falconer: never to change the course without the captain's directions, unless to avoid an immediate danger), and whether the "immediate danger" exception should be built as a standing allowance rather than your word." (`gate-m5c.md` l.78)

**What the sources say.** Falconer 1780, LIEUTENANT: "is expected to be always upon deck in his watch, as well to give the necessary orders, with regard to trimming the sails and superintending the navigation, as to prevent any noise or confusion; but he is never to change the ship's course without the captain's directions, unless to avoid an immediate danger." The Regulations of 1806, the Lieutenant, art. XIII: "He is never to change the course of the Ship without directions from the Captain, unless it be necessary to avoid some danger." Art. IV: he is to inform the captain "of all circumstances which may ... prevent the Ship's continuing on the course directed to be steered" (all three as quoted in primer 16 l.107). Art. XVII: he keeps his own account with "the bearings and distance of some principal head-land" (`Navigation1805.md` l.32-36).

**Three things follow, of different weight.**

1. *The emergency clause is (a).* Both sources carry it, in almost the same words. The build quotes it in the refusal ("the course is the captain's, never to be changed without his directions unless to avoid an immediate danger", `freesail/agents/agent.py:165-168`) and offers no way to use it, which the model found. Built as a standing allowance it must be what the sources make it: for the *course* (Falconer and art. XIII say nothing of the anchor; letting go to avoid going ashore is the model's extension, sound seamanship and (b)), "logged as such", and followed by the report art. IV requires. One guard is needed: the welfare detector for this station reads contradiction and drift, "the same order said otherwise at every turn" (`ConsentBrief.md` l.21), so an officer who cries danger at every sample is already the pattern it watches for. Since it changes what the brief says a station may do, the re-ask rule applies.
2. *Navigation's observations belong to the lieutenant by the sources, and the build withholds them.* Falconer's "superintending the navigation" and art. XVII are plain; the build's own notes call the line "judgement" (`TuningNotes.md` l.1420). In play the officer was refused a bearing, working up the reckoning and the noon sight, each until the captain's word allowed it: "The officer of the watch may not take a bearing of pendennis point without the captain: the reckoning, the sights and the course shaped are the master's for the captain." (session 2 `log-full.txt` l.217; also l.2368, l.6771; session 4 l.6441, a bearing of a light refused at night on the approach to Scilly). Opening the *observations* (a bearing, working up the reckoning) while keeping `set the reckoning to` and `shape a course` with the captain would bring the domain nearer to where "a captain of 1805 would draw it", which is the gate's question. It would also have spared a good part of the "fifteen permissions one by one".
3. *A general grant ("She is yours") is (c).* The principle: station authority, "confirmed" in decision 3 (l.569): the officer "cannot, say, alter the course the captain has ordered without asking" (§3.5, l.153), "a lieutenant can trim sail; cannot change the ordered course" (§7.1, l.379). A grant of everything makes the officer the captain in all but name, and "A captain's station is milestone 6's" (spec M5 l.601), behind the cold review's six items (l.352-378) and a consent that at least one model reserved. It also changes the consent brief's sentence on what the officer "may not" do (`ConsentBrief.md` l.17), a watched section.

*What would have to be true for a broad grant to be done without breaking it:* it is the captain's word, in the log, and taken back by `I have the deck`, which is still Falconer ("without the captain's directions": a direction may be wide); it is *data on the station*, so that "every order is checked against that domain before the ship hears it" stays true (the cold review's first item: "Authority must be enforced per order, not per station", l.354-358); it stops short of a list no word opens, which the owner's note itself proposes ("Leave some things non-negotiable") and the model would set at "anchoring, the port's business, buying and selling"; the design would add world orders, addressing another station and belaying the captain's book, since those are other channels and not the officer's at any rank; the brief says so and every model is asked again; and the station's welfare rules (a stand-by with the deck names an event or a bell; contradiction and drift) stand unchanged. Short of that list it is still an officer with a wide brief. Past it, it is M6's station arriving early without M6's preparation.

### 3.7 Notes 12, 21, 23 and local note 5: identity and the welfare commitments

**Note 12** is treated in §3.1: in tension with the letter, not the purpose, and m5c-b's rule keeps the purpose.

**Note 21: a turn need not end on `say` or `answer`; a larger budget, as config. Budget (a); the turn rule (b) with an edge.** The cap "is a budget the brief states, not a welfare rule" (`TechnicalSpec-M4.md` l.156), and budget by role is the proposal's own dial (§7.3, l.409). The turn rule is another matter because it was hard won. Playtest 4: "a turn ended only on a reply with no tool call ... so the model stood by thirteen times in a turn that never closed, and the last of those requests ran to 76,674 tokens" (`Harness.md` l.189). The owner's ruling: "a `stand_by` ends the model's turn at once on every door" (decision 24, l.604). Playtest 9's model "read "ends when you reply without a tool call" beside stand_by's "this ends your turn" as two rules", so the rule was made one sentence "wherever a door or a tool says it" (`TechnicalSpec-M4.md` l.158). Over MCP `say` hands the floor back because the game cannot see a reply with no tool call there. What must hold if `say` is to stop ending a turn at that door: the stand-by ruling stands; there is still one sentence, the same at every door; a turn held open is still bounded by the station's patience ("a turn held silent past the station's patience brings the nudge, then the pause", `agents/README.md` l.53; an hour of ship's time for the officer, which at 60x is a real minute, so the patience wants a look at the same time); and under `--lockstep`, where "the clock holds while the model has its turn" (`Harness.md` l.206), a turn that need not end holds the game. The consent brief does not describe turn endings, so no re-ask.

**Note 23: several stations through one door. (c) on identity.** The design's stated shape: "mixed crews: several stations on one game, each through its own door (a local model at one, a Claude session at another), which the agent API of package 28b already allows in shape" (`TechnicalSpec-M4.md` l.252); "Two stations on two ships through two doors" (`Scenario-StrikingColours.md` l.78-79). The principle a shared door touches is the one the cold review called nominal already: "Consent identity through Claude Desktop is a string the owner types ... a mistyped name attaches one model's session to another's consent with nothing to catch it" (l.204-212); "the bridge cannot tell which model the chat uses (you name it)" (`Harness.md` l.232); "**A new chat** in the same Claude Desktop session uses the same bridge and the same station" (l.92). Each instance is owed its own brief, its own journal ("Each instance has a journal of its own", `ConsentBrief.md` l.23), its own transcript and its own token. Through one door the game sees tool calls and cannot tell which chat sent them. *What would have to be true:* every call is bound to one seating by something the game issues with the brief and the door must present, so that a second session cannot speak as the first, each station has its consent check by identity, and no session is given two stations' briefs as one. That is a door per station in substance, whatever the configuration looks like, and it is the mechanism local note 5 needs.

**Local note 5: another door's calls landed in a seated model's place. (a); a defect against the contract.** Count what it breaks. Consent "per exact weights and never generalised" (`agents/README.md` l.46): a model acted at a station under another's record. Commitment 1: the session that acted was not the one this seating's brief had been given to. Commitment 5 and the transcript policy: the journal and "the model's replies" kept in the save "with the moment each came" (`Harness.md` l.236) are now part another party's, and the save replays them as the local model's. Commitment 2: the token "counts in any argument of any tool call" (l.88), so a stray session could have ended the local model's watch for it. And the playtest's findings about that model's fitness are mixed with another's work. The specification's agent API is keyed by station alone ("`POST /api/agents/<station>/reply` delivers one reply", `TechnicalSpec-M4.md` l.170), so this is a gap in the contract as written, not only in the build. Mending it is carrying out the commitments. Of everything in the notes it has the first claim on the harness.

### 3.8 Note 25: image tools for image-capable doors

**What the documents say about a model seeing the viewer or the chart.** As images: **not found.** As state: the views and the models are fellow readers of one stream ("The text log, the map, the profile view, replays, LLM narrators, LLM players ... all consume the same event stream and query the same state", §0, l.13), and a model's on-demand queries include "the map at lookout fidelity" (§7.4, l.415). The consent brief lists what an instance would see as "the ship's log, her readings and state, and a reference library" (`ConsentBrief.md` l.17). The papers anticipate a model looking at a chart, as a paper read by handle: "so a local model's context is never filled by a chart it glanced at" (`Papers-and-Books.md` l.51-53).

**Does it help parity or hurt it?** Both, and the order decides.

- *Toward parity.* The pillar has two halves: "No hidden API for machines, no hidden UI for humans." Today the chart and the ship view are a UI the human has and the model has not; the cold review said so (l.338-340) and the Speedwell's officer said so (session 4 l.7093). An image of the captain's chart gives an image-capable model what the human looks at. The chart is safe to show: "The true position is not in the snapshot the client receives" (spec M5 l.396-398).
- *Against text first.* "nothing here may be the only way to know something, because a language model at a station reads the same log and readings the human does" (`Presentation.md` l.5-7); "a reading in the registry or nowhere"; "The local model is the function baseline" (`TechnicalSpec-M4.md` l.252). If the picture holds anything the readings do not, a text-only local model at the same station is at the disadvantage the design forbids. Today it would: the coast's shape against the reckoning is drawn and is not a reading, which is note 24.

**Verdict: (c) if built before the text, (b) after it.** *What would have to be true:* (i) every thing the image shows is first a reading or a log line, so the image is a second rendering and never a second source (note 24 first, and the track, the ellipse, the bearings laid down, the soundings); (ii) it is the captain's chart and the same ship view the human has, drawn from the same snapshot, never the truth; (iii) asking for one is a question, which "is never journaled and changes nothing aboard" (primer 10 l.144), so replay is untouched; (iv) the consent brief's "What an instance would see and do" names it and the re-ask is run; (v) it is shelved like a book, as the owner proposes, since a picture costs a context more than a page. The owner's own rule in the general notes is the same test from the other side: "Nothing can be audio-only, for LLM parity." And `Presentation.md` shows how the design wants a new sense added: "Because every sound derives from a reading or a log line, parity with the text is free." (l.57-58) One caution on cost: the viewer is drawn in the browser, so images for a door mean a renderer on the game's side, and pillar 6 says where budget goes first ("Performance and budget go into simulation"). By the cold review's reckoning the parity gap "will matter for a captain", which is M6; the 2.5D view is M8's.

## 4. What the proposal would have the owner be careful of

**4.1 A wish that serves no pillar waits; scope is held by the plan.** "**Pillars** (used to settle arguments later; if a feature serves none of these, it waits)" (l.23). "Scope | Everything above | The milestone plan below, and the pillars as tie-breakers" (l.531). "Dates are deliberately absent; order is what matters." (l.537) The owner's own notes: "Avoid scope creep before building out the baseline." (`General notes.txt` l.9) and "we need still needed bounded goals whichever way we go" (l.14). The first page: "Consider scope for achievability, but be bold in planning for depth."

**4.2 Name the order, the reading or the carrier, or it waits.** "When a feature is proposed, ask which way it goes and name the order or reading (inward) or the modelled carrier (outward) that makes it real. If neither can be named, the feature waits." (`InwardAndOutward.md` l.71-73) This sorts the pile quickly: the well (a reading named, missing), the price book (a paper named), the goods a port trades (no carrier named yet), the nearest land (the lookout named), the image tools (the reading must come first).

**4.3 Do not report as a fault what the gate said it does not do.** A gate report states "what is deliberately *not* in this milestone, so it is not reported as a fault" (`gates/README.md` l.14). Gate 5c's list: "A captain's station, a crewed ship beside the player's, a rules-based captain (M6) ... The director (M7b) ... Colours as deception, the private signal, prizes and convoys (M7) ... Warping, towing, the hawse fouled, the kedge worked (M8); a lookout's station for a small model" (`gate-m5c.md` l.89-93). Several notes sit in it: 1 and 9 in part, S1, B3 in part, H4 in part.

**4.4 Keep the order of the stations.** "first watching and commentary, then stations aboard the *player's* ship ..., then command of a *separate* ship as an NPC captain or a competitor. Each step reuses the previous one's plumbing and adds only authority." (§7.1, l.385) The director is "After parity (M6+)" (l.381), and of its open questions "None of these change the M5 to M6 plan; the world-order channel is the one thing to build with the director in mind." (l.441) Notes 5 and 15 both pull toward the captain before M6's parity tests exist.

**4.5 Realism against convenience: answer a hardship with the ship's means, not by easing the world.** "The baseline is uncompromising: no true-position readout in the default game. A "casual" navigation mode ... is a later option, not the default, and the design keeps the two positions separate so that adding it is a display choice rather than a simulation change." (§5.2, l.320) "the night's cost in canvas is answered by the crew and not by easing the storm" (decision 28, l.612). "Things that never could have been done are not." (pillar 7) And the cold review on the one time a dial was turned until a day looked right: "That is tuning to a narrative rather than to a source ... the sea's cost is a dial that was turned until one day looked right" (l.124-130). Note 19's second half is this choice exactly.

**4.6 Do not build for one test.** "Content is systems, not levels." (pillar 5) The world is built as mechanisms because that "avoids building throwaway scenario scaffolding" (§5.1, l.307). "**a scenario's book is tuned once and cheaply.** It needs to bring the ship through at the pinned seed and no more ... A builder that finds itself on its tenth whole-passage run is to say so in its report rather than run a twentieth." (`M5-WorkPackages.md` l.25-30) "the primer must not narrate the gate's day" (decision 28). "build what the specification says at the size it says" (l.9). The owner's own notes 1, 4 and 24 already speak this way ("We needn't overtune the offline element", "a general guide, not one for a specific test", "a generalizable way"). The grounding off Penlee is one afternoon in one fluky air; the rule that answers it should be Falconer's look-out, not a patch for Plymouth Sound.

**4.7 Every consequence costs four things.** "every new consequence needs vocabulary, an evolution, a viewer change and a primer line before it is a consequence a player can act on" (cold review l.433-436). Pumps, kedging off and a pilot's refusal each look like a line and are each a small milestone by that count.

**4.8 Spend on what makes later packages cheaper.** "Spend it on the things that make each later package cheaper, not on more packages in parallel" (cold review l.476-477). "Ports and markets are content; the officer is the pillar." (l.425) "More real-model sessions of an hour, fewer of a day." (l.487) The cold review also found that milestone 5b had become "three milestones wearing one coat" (l.21-22); a follow-up package that takes all fifty-odd of these items would be the same coat.

**4.9 Gates lag the work, and rulings become a follow-up package.** "a gate cut when its last package lands and run when the owner can, its rulings a follow-up package, as 5a's were 31b" (decision 29, l.614). That is the form these notes should take: the gate's rulings wanted (`gate-m5c.md` l.78-81) answered, then one package from them, the rest "recorded as not now" as decision 29 did with the cold review's list. `gates/README.md` l.18 still says "The next milestone does not start until the verdict is in"; gate 5c's verdict is pending, and "Gate 5c's verdict waits on that watch" (spec M5 l.599-600).

**4.10 What bears on what a model was told is paid for with a re-ask.** "When the design changes in a way that bears on what a model was told (a new role, a new kind of session, a new use of the transcripts), the question is asked again." (`agents/README.md` l.9) By the rule's sections, these notes would each move the brief: 12 (Leaving; done in m5c-b), 15 and its emergency clause (what an instance would see and do), 11 (the journal), 25 (what an instance would see). The owner's standing rule allows them to be gathered: "a consent update may be sought when the harness and game are largely final, still per model" (l.23). Changes of this kind are better made together than one a gate.

**4.11 Hold every new line to the voice.** "This is cheap to lose and hard to regain; every new line should be held to it." (cold review l.74-77) It applies to the lookout's urgent call, the nearest-land words and every refusal the notes ask to be mended.

**4.12 The lowest reader is the baseline.** "Every record-keeping feature ... is built for the local model first, which has the least help from its own runtime" (`TechnicalSpec-M4.md` l.252). Notes 11, 13, 21, 24 and the local notes are served by it; note 25 is tested by it.
