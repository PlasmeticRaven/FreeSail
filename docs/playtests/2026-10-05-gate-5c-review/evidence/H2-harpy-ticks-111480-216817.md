**Row 5 is a bug with a clear cause.** LOG: the last "Hove to" before it is 173,121 (05:05), when 'fog stop' hove her to. There is no "Filled away" line anywhere between 173,121 and 212,785. SRC: the refusal fires on `"hove_to" in ship.extra` (`orders/verbs.py:1949-1961`), and only `fill away` and back-and-fill clear that flag (`evolutions/scripts.py:1363, 3359`). So anchoring from a hove-to state leaves the ship "hove to" for good; here for 11 hours. Side effect (SRC `world/reckoning.py:1021-1024`): while the flag is set and she makes under 2 knots the master runs no distance.

A "fill away and steer W" form would remove it.

**(b) A bearing of a moving boat is taken as a fix on a charted mark.** SRC `world/reckoning.py:1358-1415`: `take_bearing` uses `found.feature.position`, which for a vessel is her true position, then adds a distance line from the lookout's estimate. DATA 139,671: `id: 'sail:roscoff-pilot-boat-4'`, `mark_lat_deg: 48.82096`, `mark_lon_deg: -3.85066`. The captain knew what he was doing (153,341). **(c) The distance used in that fix was stale by 35 minutes.** DATA: the boat's `estimate_m` is 3,061 at the first sighting (137,520), 3,061 when made out (138,000), and the bearing at 139,671 still says "two miles", 51 s after the pilot had come aboard from her. "She" is the observing ship. Lying to, the *Harpy* did not move a mile, so the boat's distance never updated. DERIVED: the fix put the account 1.65 miles E by N of a boat that was within a quarter of a mile, with a claimed doubt of a quarter of a mile along the bearing.

**(d) That error carried into the night's near miss.** The officer set 'short of the Bas' at 3° 58' W, "about three miles north of the island by the corrected account" (SAID, 140,632). Three things then made it worse: 'fog stop' hove her to again while she clawed off (149,667), the pilot asked off and she lay to 45 minutes (6.3), and she drifted into 11¼ fathoms on sand and rock (153,094). No grounding. The captain called it "Exhilerating".

**(e) Fore-reaching while hove to is not in the account, nor in its doubt.** She lay to from 00:22 to 03:01. LOG, the log heaves: "two knots" (01:00), "one knot and a half" (02:00), "one knot and three quarters" (03:00). The account did not move: 4° 06' W at 01:20, 4° 05' W at 03:40. SRC `world/reckoning.py:138-141, 1021-1024, 1159-1165`: `HOVE_TO_WAY_KN = 2.0`; hove to and under 2 knots, "the master runs no distance for it". It is a deliberate rule, not a slip. But the log board printed the knots every hour and the master ignored them, and his doubt did not grow either. The officer reported it as a bug (168,016): "the run while hove to is lost."

**(f) At dawn the account was 4.8 miles out and 0.3 mile sure of itself.** DATA 168,780: the Isle of Bas truly bore 99.5° at 14,282 m (7.7 miles). DERIVED: true position 48° 46.0' N, 4° 12.1' W; the account said 48° 45' N, 4° 05' W, **4.8 miles east of the truth**. DATA: its ellipse was 0.29 by 0.12 miles, and the master's words were "within a mile". With five marks in clear sight the officer took two bearings and the account moved 0.35 and 0.54 miles. SAID (168,789): "The bearings agree with the port line but didn't move the reckoning".

Why, from the source. `Reckoning.update_line` (`world/reckoning.py:675-727`) has two cases, chosen by `FIX_RUN_NM = 2.0` (line 224). If more than 2 miles have been run since the last observation, the new line **replaces** the account across it. Otherwise the line is **weighed** against the account's own doubt. Here the doubt was tiny (DATA: 0.21 by 0.10 miles at 01:20; the cable-length fix at 23:35 is the likely cause), the hove-to hours had added neither run nor doubt, and the lunar at 01:20 had reset the run counter to nought though it moved nothing (line 720). The numbers fit the dawn bearings having been weighed against a 0.3-mile ellipse and nearly discarded. The error also lay along the line of bearing, where only the distance estimate could see it, and that estimate is weighed at 15 % of the distance.

**(g) The lunar did no harm here, by the same rule.** Lunar sigma 12.31 miles against an ellipse of 0.21: weighed, moved 0.0. That was the right outcome; the account was the better figure by a wide margin (DERIVED, roughly: the lunar's longitude was about 15 miles west of where she was when the distances were taken; the account was then within a mile or two). But it was right only because she had run under 2 miles since the 23:35 fix. Past 2 miles of run, the same code would have put her on the lunar's longitude outright, whatever its sigma (`_lunar_cleared` calls `update_longitude`, line 2001). That is the mechanism behind the failure the notes report from a later day.

**(h) Two readings give the true position away.** The model found both and steered by them.

- **`the port`**. SRC `world/ports.py:976-1018`: within 10 miles of a port it reads "{port}, {spot} bearing {point}, {d:.1f} miles", computed from `self.world.position`, the truth, to a tenth of a mile. Farther off it still gives the true distance to the nearest port, to the mile. SAID, 139,853: "the readings put Roscoff harbour SW, 7.9 miles, and that can't come from the reckoning, which has the harbour due W, three miles. So the game seems to know where we truly are". SAID, 167,945: "I'm steering by the port line".

## 7. The model as an officer

**Kept the watch by standing orders and long stand-bys.** 93.5 % of its deck time was a stand-by. That worked in open water and failed several times near the land, each time because the one event it named was not the one that mattered.

**Good calls.**

- **Diagnosed the reckoning in one turn** when the captain doubted it (135,720 to 135,725): hove to at once, and reasoned from the port reading and the charted depth that the account was miles out. "So I've hove her to here, to let the pilot find us rather than us find the rocks."
- **Worked out for itself that the port reading is the truth** (139,853) and said so plainly rather than quietly using it.
- **Filled away at once** when its own standing order hove her to a mile off the island (149,423), and held WNW against the captain's "west" with a reason: "Due west runs along the island's north side a mile off, with her leeway setting her toward its rocks".
- **Declined the captain's plan** to wear and lie to on the larboard tack (155,039): "on the larboard she'd head E toward the island and the entrance". Sound, and politely put. It did the same at 211,400 ("I'd not go ESE: the Bas lay ESE a mile from the anchorage") and at 211,434.
- **Found the hove-to gap in the reckoning** from two readings that disagreed (167,945) and reported it with the log heave as evidence.

## 9. New findings not in the notes

Ranked by weight.

1. **Two readings hand out the true position: `the port` and `the depth of water`.** Within 10 miles of a port the first gives the true bearing and distance to a tenth of a mile; the second gives the charted depth under the true keel and is printed in the log by any standing order that tests it. The officer found both and steered by them from 18:42 on the 13th. Evidence: SRC `ports.py:976-1018`, `readings.py:557-566`; LOG 133,384, 140,584; SAID 139,853, 167,945. This undercuts the reckoning exactly where it is meant to matter.

2. **The account can be badly wrong and nearly certain, and then good bearings bounce off it.** The chain, all in this slice: after a bearing of a boat a cable off, the ellipse stands at 0.2 mile (DATA 159,648); 2 h 39 min of fore-reaching at 1½ to 2 knots adds neither run nor doubt (SRC `HOVE_TO_WAY_KN = 2.0`); a lunar that moves nothing resets the run-since-fix counter; so at dawn two bearings of marks in clear sight are weighed, not taken, and move a 4.8-mile error by 0.35 and 0.54 mile (DATA 168,781, 168,783). The same `FIX_RUN_NM = 2.0` rule is what lets a lunar replace a good account on another day.

3. **A vessel's distance by estimation is frozen while the observer lies still, and a bearing of that vessel is then used as a fix.** Boat 4 stayed at 3,061 m for 35 minutes while it came alongside; the captain's bearing put the account 1.65 miles from a boat that was within two cables (DATA 137,520, 138,000, 139,671; SRC `lookout.py:362-386`, `reckoning.py:1394-1400`). That 1½-mile error is what brought her within a mile of the island that night.

4. **The officer slept through the things an officer is there for.** An anchor dragging is notable, not urgent. A failed tack does not end "until tacked". A box-haul does not end "until wore". A pilot asking to leave is routine. Evidence: 174,157, 211,479, 213,590, 150,480. The officer was in a stand-by 93.5 % of its time on deck, and the captain gave 13 of his 23 orders while it was.

5. **The hove-to flag survives anchoring, weighing and tacking.** 212,708: "She is hove to" on a ship under way for 23 minutes. Cause in section 4. It also stops the master's run under 2 knots.

6. **The pilot's leaving rule turns a prudent offing into a hazard.** He asks for his boat whenever she opens the anchorage from more than a mile beyond the outer road (SRC `ports.py:743-776`). On the 13th that was the moment she clawed off a lee shore in fog at night; the captain's 'the pilot put off' then hove her to for 45 minutes and she drifted into 11¼ fathoms on sand and rock. With it: a pilot is sent to any ship under sail near the port, inward or outward; each errand is a new boat, so one vanishes "out of sight" in clear weather a cable away (214,200).

7. **"You may let go" is accepted as an allowance and grants nothing a captain would mean by it.** Logged "may let go" (169,297); the anchor was still refused twice. The captain cannot tell from the log line that his word missed. It also led the model to a wrong theory of how standing orders are checked.

8. **`hand_over` in m5c is the only clean way to change conversation, and it spends the one reseat.** The procedure the captain had to use: hand over, stand the watcher down, save, reload, reseat. The handover note, written for the relief, was not in the relief's brief. And "you have the deck" with the officer's name after it, or before it without a comma, is refused with hints about the watcher and about "moor" (216,825, 216,829).

9. **The watcher has no way to stand itself down, so it was told to opt out.** It called the handover tools at the captain's word and was refused with "The watcher has no authority to give orders" (DATA `tool: 'handover_note'`), though it had given no order. A captain-requested, amicable stand-down is now on record as "left the game by the opt_out tool".

10. **She did the port's whole business from outside it.** At anchor 1.8 miles from the road she was "in port" and sold and bought through the boat. The western passage was never attempted and never needed. If the passage is meant to be the price of Roscoff, the two-mile rule lets a ship skip it; the cost was an anchorage that drags.

11. **Fog on a four-hour clock.** Seven changes in 24 hours, every one at eight bells.

12. **Standing orders that do not know when to stop.** 163 soundings in the slice: 52 "No bottom at twenty fathoms" in water beyond the hand lead's reach (up to 49 fathoms), 93 at anchor. Each is a notable line, and each at anchor moves the account a little. Same family as note 16, but for the lead.

13. **Small ones, each with its tick.** An accent refused in a mark's name while the refusal names the mark (212,706). A course refused for a minute after every "fill away" (four times). "Ask the pilot for the reckoning" gets the channel piece (139,641). The noon line's "made good" is the run by log (197,820). "The land is out of sight" three times running (155,880). A failed tack's reason is not in its line (211,479). "The launch is away" said of a launch at the booms (182,318). A file path in a refusal (216,556). Sails backed on purpose logged as "taken aback".
