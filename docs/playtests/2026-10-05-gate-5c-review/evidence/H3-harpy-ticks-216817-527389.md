## 6. Ship, sea, navigation and port observations

**A ship hove to came out of it by herself, with no alert.** LOG: hove to 20:18 on the starboard tack (487083); "Hove the log: one knot." at 22:00; the wind backs SW by S 22:29, veers W 22:47, a squall "veers a point to W by N and freshens to 10 knots" 22:48; at 22:57 "Fore topsail taken aback." and five fore-and-aft sails with it (496638 to 496650), "Main topsail filled again." (496661); "Leeway 47° to starboard." 23:01; "Hove the log: one knot and three quarters." at 00:00 (500435). OFFICER 500407: "she's swung round: her head is N by E, the main topsail full, making two knots toward the land". Nothing in the log says she had filled. No urgent line; the six `sail.backed` lines are notable and did not wake a stand-by on the change of the watch. The game's state still held her hove to an hour later ("She is hove to; fill away before giving her a course.", 500492). Set beside the calm, the alarm is the wrong way round: urgent thirteen times when there was nothing to lose, silent when a ship lying to in the dark began sailing toward the land.

**Heaving to from four knots puts her through the wind.** LOG 505811 `heave to` at "four knots and a quarter" (504033), helm a-lee; "Hove to" 505862; two minutes later "Fore topsail taken aback." and five headsails and staysails, "Main topsail filled again." (505987 to 506017); "Leeway 52° to larboard." (506748). She lay the rest of the night on the other tack with the fore topsail aback, though the log's words stayed "main topsail to the mast".

**`fill away` chooses its own course.** 500546: "Filled away; braced full and steering S (185°)." when her head was N by E and the officer meant to stand in northward. 514885: "Filled away; braced full and steering NW by W (302°)." when she lay on the other tack; he had to wear, which called all hands and took eight minutes (515399 "Wore ship; braced sharp up on the larboard tack, heading NW by W (300°)."). By my working both courses are full and by on the tack she had hove to on (the wind WSW less six points is S; SW by W plus six points is NW by W). So the order steers her close-hauled on the tack of the heave-to, whatever her head has done since, and both times she had come round onto the other tack while lying to. The log line does not say that this is how the course was chosen.

**The reckoning stood still through the heave-to and for an hour after, by the log's own figure.** LOG 504006, 18 June 01:00: "The reckoning worked up: 49° 59' N, 4° 54' W by account; run since noon 21 miles, course made good NNE. I would not trust the reckoning within a mile east or west, nor a mile north or south." My sum of the hourly log from noon to 20:00 on the 17th (2, 2, 1¾, 2¼, 2¼, 2¼, 2½, 3¼ and part of the 20:00 read of 3) is about 19 to 21 miles, so the 21 miles at 01:00 is the run up to the evening and no more. The four reads taken while hove to (1, 1, ¼, 1¾ knots) and the hour under way from 00:02 are not in it. OFFICER 504015: "the reckoning, even freshly worked up, hasn't moved since midnight ... It seems the run isn't counted once she's been hove to, even after filling away." At 505815 he says it "has caught up"; the 01:00 heave of the log (504033, "four knots and a quarter") had come 27 seconds after he worked the reckoning up. Primer 10 says `work up the reckoning` "brings it up to the moment". The next noon, outside the slice, has the account 5' behind the sun (543318: "Latitude by observation 50° 13' N; the reckoning was 50° 08' N"), where the two sighted noons of this slice had it ahead.

**The landfall.** The fog lifted at 20:00 on the 17th and the lookout gave six marks in one second (486000), the first land since 20:00 on the 14th. From the two bearings taken at once (486012 "Manacle Point bore NW, two leagues by estimation"; 486014 "Black Head bore WNW, two leagues by estimation") I make her about 49° 59' N, 4° 57' W; the account at 01:00 says 49° 59' N, 4° 54' W, trusted "within a mile". Two small things: the lookout's line and the bearing a few seconds later differ by a point for Black Head ("W by N" 486000, "WNW" 486014); and the lookout put Cadgwith Cove at "five leagues" beside Black Head at "two leagues", though the two lie about three miles apart.

**The approach to Falmouth failed on position, and the chain is worth setting out.**
1. At 20:00 the officer has "Falmouth is eleven miles N by W" (486027). She lies to from 20:18, drifting to leeward, and from about 23:00 sails herself N by E.
2. At 00:01 he stands in "on N by E, east of the Manacles" (500467). The captain's chart says the bay is NNW (502029); the officer alters only to N by W. So for twenty-five minutes she steers three points east of the captain's bearing and for an hour one point east of it.
3. The account does not move until after 01:00. At 01:30 it shows "the outer road now bears WNW, eight miles", "within a point of the wind" (505815): five points to windward of where her head has been pointing.
4. At 05:11 he reports her "Into Falmouth Bay, sir, by the reckoning ... the outer road NW by W five and a half" (519114). At 05:49 and again at 05:57 the captain's bearing gives "St Anthony's Head bore NW by W, five miles by estimation", and the captain says "I see where we actually are now."
5. Close-hauled on 298° to 302° with "Leeway 8° to starboard" she cannot weather the entrance. 06:52: "St Anthony's Head bore W by N, three miles by estimation"; "We've got to tack."
6. She anchors two and a half miles east of the road; the boat is refused; the captain bears away for Plymouth, where the later slices put her ashore.

## 7. The model as an officer

**Good calls.**
- Read the two lifts on the first evening and came up to them at once, reporting each ("A lift, sir", 219229).
- Read the glass: 284290 "A falling glass after a long north-easterly often means it's going round to the south-west." It did, within a day.
- Sound arithmetic throughout. I checked his bearings and distances to Falmouth and St Mary's from the stated positions at 217355, 247680, 284290, 341741 and 453491 against the real places; all are right to within a few miles and a point.
- Saw that the straight line for Falmouth "runs right over the Lizard" and steered NE first (341741). Read the chart's warning on `shape a course for plymouth` and laid her a point east of it (438867).
- Put stops in the book before they were needed and thought about their preconditions: `short of Scilly`, `short of the Lizard`, and `night wings` "so they're down before 'short of the Lizard' can heave her to in the dark" (468007).
- At the landfall: two bearings in fourteen seconds, studdingsails in, and wore to the offshore tack before heaving to, with the reason given ("On this tack, hove to, she'd fore-reach west, onto the Manacles", 486027).
- Belayed `trim on a shift` two ticks after "Hove to" and warned the captain about his own trim order (487088).
- Changed his mind at 01:30 and lay to, not to beat in past the Old Wall and the Black Rock in the dark; the captain: "Thank you, solid plan."
- 523637: "by account the Lugo rock bears NW by W three miles and the Governor NW by W four, both right on our heading ... I'd rather check it now with bearings on Pendennis and St Anthony's than meet the Lugo in the flesh." The captain came up half a point.
- Candour when wrong: "You were right, sir, and I should have taken your suggestion whole." (328842); "Your chart knows it better than my memory, sir" (247932); "You're right, sir: on N by E she'd fetch the bay's eastern side" (502040).

**Mistakes.**
- Stood by "until a sighting" for 4 h 54 m on the 17th, the last four hours in fog, closing the Lizard at two to three knots on an account he himself rated "fifteen miles adrift east and west". His own note of 14 June reads "LESSONS: near land, stand by a glass at most, never 'until a landfall'", and the captain had asked for "a regular eye". The only safeguard at work was a twenty-fathom lead in forty-five fathoms. When the fog lifted the Manacles were six miles off.
- Left her hove to for two hours at a stretch and found at midnight that she had been sailing toward the land for about an hour.
- The midnight course. He chose N by E for a port he had last put N by W, made no allowance for her drift since 20:18, and trusted an account that had not moved. The captain's chart caught it.
- The "third way" (525172) rested on the boat working from two and a half miles. It did not, and the advice cost them the alternative of tacking in on the flood.
- Said he was standing by at 349745 and did not make the call.
- Half measures in the calm (kept topsails and jib) cost two more alarms before he took the captain's advice whole.
- Small invented or assumed facts: "Mr Tregenza" for a pilot not yet aboard (521448; it was Mr Vivian); "Crow Sound opens to the north-east" from memory (247808), corrected from the chart; the stale pilot orders in the 18 June note.
- Two claims about the game that the log does not bear out as he put them: that the apparent wind "may have a sign wrong somewhere" (400653; see section 10), and that six leagues for the Lizard lights was "surely too far" as if a fault, where the primer gives estimates as "a fifth out either way" (though by my reckoning this one was further out than that).

**How he handled refusals.** Plainly and once. He never repeated a refused order without first changing what had made it refused (the one repeat, `steer north by east`, came after `fill away`); he reported the refusal's words to the captain, asked leave where it was a matter of leave, and found another road where there was one (the standing order for the refused stand-by).

**What he asked for or wished for.**
- `keep her full and by` allowed (217355): "the ship would steer her own best close-hauled course through these shifts, and I could stop chasing the wind with the helm."
- A stand-by on the wind's strength (349717): "There's no event in my list for 'the wind freshening', only for its direction shifting".
- The taken-aback alarm quietened (327648, 331094, 342058, 526495).
- The handover note in the reseat brief (226870, 527393).
- A standing trim that keeps clear of a ship lying to (487088).
- A reckoning that counts the drift while hove to (504015, 526495).
- A boat that will pull more than two miles (525629).
- On the watcher (216878): "every sample from a second station eases the clock to 1x, which would make a long beat longer still."

**Quotes worth keeping.**

- 241203: "Patience is the order of the night."
- 341804, on praying for wind: "You may whistle softly for a wind, but only in a calm, and gently; whistle in a breeze and you whistle up a gale ...
