## 2. What happened

**Session 3, 21 June.** The captain anchored in Cawsand Bay and gave the officer the deck (83). The officer's standing order for bearings was refused (317). After seven tries at the wording the captain allowed buying, selling and sending the boat. The officer sent the boat (3037); the captain belayed it seventeen seconds later, meaning to send the mate in her. The boat stayed "away, hoisting out" for good, a rejected order had put the mate in her while he held the deck (3237), and the officer waited 59 minutes on a stand-by before raising it. They agreed to start again (6753).

**Session 4, 21 June.** Fourteen allowances in two minutes, then the deck (109). Three boat trips, 05:01 to 15:20, fetched the prices, 40 tons of coal and 20 of pilchards, bought for Roscoff on the officer's general knowledge. She weighed at 15:35 and hung head to wind until he backed her off by hand; the Plymouth pilot boarded unasked meanwhile and was put off at 16:58. The captain took bearings of the Eddystone and kept the con overnight while the officer stood by.

**22 June.** At 05:07 a lunar moved the account 33' east; the officer advised steering by the old account and kept a reckoning of his own. The noon latitude was ten miles north of the account. At 22:00 he wore and hove to, head offshore, the danger list showing the Triagoz five miles off.

**23 June.** His standing order "trim" filled her out of the heave-to at 02:01, and again at 10:41. The Isle of Bas came up at 07:37 and the captain's bearing put the account right. The Roscoff pilots' boat hailed at 10:29; she lay hove to 1 h 33 m and was not boarded. The captain chose to go in without; she shoaled from 15 fathoms to 3½ north-west of the island (12:41) with no word from the lookout, hauled off, and the pilot boarded as she passed his boat (12:46). The wind died and she anchored outside the channel. Roscoff would not take coal; the officer bought 16 tons of brandy for £1,600.

**24 to 25 June.** She weighed cleanly, put the pilot off at 05:08 and shaped a course for St Mary's, 112 miles. From 20:00 she lay twenty hours without way: 90 "sails aback" lines and 223 per-sail lines, none urgent, none waking the officer. At 04:22 on the 25th the captain tried hand-over and re-seating, found that "I have the deck" stands the station down, and kept the deck fifteen hours. When he gave it back the officer's conversation had been replaced and he could not reach his own handover note; the captain printed the journal into the log.

**26 June.** Two-knot airs worked to windward by compass courses; then the wind freed her. He shortened sail on the look of the sky (21:31), was taken aback by a shift (21:57) and hove to off the Wolf rock for the night.

**27 June.** St Agnes light at 03:29: the officer's bearing was refused; the captain's moved the account seven miles. Pilot Ellis boarded at 05:51. She stood in by the captain's unnamed chart marks, tacked to seaward at 07:02 to wait for the flood (which by rule sent the gig for the pilot), tacked back, lost the pilot at 07:17, and turned out when the lead went from 14¼ to 5¼ fathoms. Fog; she anchored in 46 fathoms. A second attempt (12:05 to 13:15) ran by the account at the Gilstone; she anchored again, a second pilot boarded at anchor, and the boat, ordered while all hands were anchoring, stuck "away". A third attempt (15:27 to 16:05) was beaten by a wind boxing the compass. The officer handed over at 16:07.

### 3.7 Ship handling and ground tackle

## 8. The model as an officer

Careful, quick and honest. He planned every landfall for daylight; kept a second reckoning and chose the course safe under either (141901, 147615); put the lead on every three minutes six minutes before the shoal; turned out seventeen seconds after the five-fathom cast (526866); shortened on the sky (491474); took the con from the captain, with his reasons, when the captain was steering for the Gilstone (526355); would not "send it and pray" (558236). His reports of the game's faults were accurate, seven of them confirmed in code or checkpoint [P5], and he owned his errors.

He erred by leaving "trim" standing through two heave-tos after promising otherwise; by giving ten helm orders without trimming; by never using the deep-sea lead, `heave the log` or `ask the pilot`, all his to give; by taking no bearing in the Sound once allowed; by conning on the account alone towards the Gilstone; by ordering the boat away mid-anchoring; and by getting the tide wrong at Roscoff and twice at St Mary's. He stated stale or held figures as fact ("two miles ahead", "a mile north-north-west of the Lavandière") and worked two fixes too fast (47619, 182230). He invented little (fog signals "as the custom is", 532406).

The game's share: readings that do not say where they measure from, a failure line with no reason, no chart, no bearings, one-condition stand-bys, and a turn that closes on speaking.
