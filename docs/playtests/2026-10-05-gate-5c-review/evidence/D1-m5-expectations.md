## 4. Rulings

### 4.1 The four the gate asks for (GATE:77-81)

**Ruling 1. The officer's domain as drawn.** GATE:78: "Sail handling, the yards, the lines, the lead and the log, the lookout, and his own standing orders; not the course, a manoeuvre, the anchor, all hands, the people or the port unless you allow a named thing in your words. Rule whether the line is where a captain of 1805 would draw it for a lieutenant with the deck (Falconer: never to change the course without the captain's directions, unless to avoid an immediate danger), and whether the "immediate danger" exception should be built as a standing allowance rather than your word."

- Context in the documents: WP:1626-1635 (the domain is data on the station; "unless the captain's brief for the watch allows a named thing"); P16:46 quotes Falconer and the Regulations of 1806 art. XIII ("unless it be necessary to avoid some danger"); TN:1420 "the line between the lead and the log (his) and the sights and the reckoning (the master's for the captain) is judgement"; TN:1455 "`take a bearing of` kept the master's; the boat's errands the captain's". The playtest form asks for exactly this (docs/playtests/README.md, 5b: "an order a lieutenant of 1806 could have given, refused by the game, is a finding").
- On record before the playtest: Opus 5.5's consent answer of 2026-10-02 (`docs/agents/consent/2026-10-02-opus-5.5.md:14`), "offered as wishes, not conditions": "an officer of the watch who can't heave to or wear will sometimes see danger before the captain does; a clear way to call the captain urgently (a hail that wakes him, or that the log marks as urgent) would let the officer do the duty a real one had."
- What the notes say: owner's 15 (a general allowance, "She is yours", with "some things non-negotiable, or requiring explicit authorization?"); the model's comment on 15 (keep an explicit-only list of anchoring, the port's business, buying and selling; an always-open emergency clause to let go the anchor or alter course "to avoid an immediate danger", logged as such; "The refusal text already uses that phrase, but there's no route to act on it"); the model's "Full and by" bullet.
- What the record adds: the captain typed 34 `you may` orders on the Harpy (S2), 29 on the Speedwell (S4), 20 on the merchant ship (S8), 6 to 14 on each cutter (counted in the inputs, misspellings and repeats included). The domain refused `take a bearing of ...` in five of the seven officer sessions (S2, S3, S4, S5, S7; for example S5: "may not take a bearing of the land without the captain: the reckoning, the sights and the course shaped are the master's for the captain"), `keep her full and by`, `come up half a point`, `fill away`, `heave short`, `send for the carpenter`, `pipe down`, `take a lunar`, `set the reckoning`. Nearly every one the owner then allowed by hand (the lunar he ordered himself). That is evidence that the line is drawn too tight for a watch near land.
- A correction the lead should carry: the model says of the emergency clause "On the Harpy that clause would have mattered." The log does not support it as a cause. At S2 635868 (13:37) the officer's `let go the anchor` was **accepted** ("By the officer of the watch: letting go the anchor"; the captain had given `you may let go`); the captain belayed it at 635891 ("avast all ... letting go anchor") and wrote "I'll con her, don't worry" (635913); the officer's second `let go the anchor` at 636631 was again accepted, twenty-two seconds before she struck (636653). The officer's own last journal agrees: "I should have let go on my own word at the first warning, not the second" (S2 689840).

**Ruling 2. The sample's size on a local model.** GATE:79: "The officer's brief head is about 4,300 tokens and a glass's sample about 1,500 at its fullest ...; say whether the local model on the cutter kept the thread through a watch, and how its context stood."

- Context: TN:1431-1442 (the head 4,265 tokens at the runner; a sample 1,511 at its fullest, 193 quiet); HAR:256 (the handover asked at "six tenths of the context"); TN:1425 `HANDOVER_AT_FRACTION` 0.6 "under the runner's own dropping point (the context less the reply budget and the tool definitions)"; HAR:179 Ollama "is the quicker look, and `llama-server` is the exact one"; HAR:187 "the owner's Ollama runs every model at 262,144 tokens".
- What the notes say: local notes 1 to 4 are the answer. With llama.cpp and a set context "the experience is quite smooth and the "game-sense" is reasonable" (Qwen 3.8 27B and Gemma 4 26B); the handover "worked as an "in-game" compaction" and its figures "matched reality"; the blocker was the per-sample tool-call budget, not the sample's size.

**19. Reckoning and the lookout near visible land.** The weightiest note, and the one the documents constrain most. Three parts.

- *What exists.* The lookout already speaks at notable severity "for a landfall or a danger" (SPEC:283-285). TN:832: "`lookout.SHORE_CLOSE_NM` 3 miles: the shore itself hailed close aboard **when no headland of the chart is in sight**". - *What is on record as a limit.* WP:1147-1149: "the helm rules of the book have no guard, and that is the captain's business, so no rule is added". TN:1402: "`Navigation.account_now` runs the last worked position on at the log's last read ... so as the ship turns, or lies aback, the account swings up to the hour's run and a mark three miles off is reached and left by account in a minute." P10:39: the master's doubt "is honest about what he knows and silent about what he does not", so the ellipse understates the error by design. P12:99: the lookout's distance is "drawn once for each sighting of a mark and held until the ship has made a mile from where he judged it". Open item 16: Roscoff's narrows are on the modern grid.
- *What is new.* An urgent lookout alarm from the true course against a danger or any land within the visibility; and the account made "very precise" near land.

What the record shows: the Harpy struck in clear weather with the captain conning, marks in sight and bearings just taken. S2 635331 (13:28) the officer fixed her by Rame Head and Penlee and planned to "pass about a quarter mile outside Penlee"; 636493 and 636626 "Penlee Point bore NNE, a mile by estimation"; 636271 "And a quarter eleven"; 636653 "She has taken the ground forward ... two fathoms of water by the chart". Think twice before implementing as written. (a) The alarm is consistent with the chapter's rules **if it is the lookout's**: SPEC:33-37 "everything outward arrives through something the ship models", and SPEC:279 "The sighting model is the lookout's reading". An eye sees the truth, so a hail of "breakers ahead" or "the land close aboard on the bow" from the true geometry is the period's channel, and it is already how `SHORE_CLOSE_NM` works. (b) Making the **account** precise from the truth is not: SPEC:36-37 "no reading ever gives the truth where the period could not have it", decision 5 and PROP:320. The documents' own way for the account to tighten near land is observation: P10:81 "a bearing of a mark of the chart is a line of position ... Two bearings cross to a point; a transit of the chart's two marks in one is exact", and the lead. A design that keeps the rules: the master takes cross-bearings of himself when two marks are in sight (as "The day's work is automatic at noon", SPEC:317), which narrows the account by the specified means. (c) A lookout that judges and calls is open item 6's station, deferred. (d) The alarm must be a reading and an event as well as a line (WP:23; WP:985-986 already names `a danger sighted`). Cross-reference: owner's 24, the model's comment on 19, B3 (held estimates), S8 (account at anchor).
