**What the director knew from truth:** its last truth books were 27 and 28 at 09:08, which put her at 38° 31′ N, 28° 34′ W, W by N at 1.8 kn. It knew that the wind was low 11 at 992 against its own high. It opened no truth during the anchoring or the drag. Its stand-by woke at 09:34 (the drag) and at 09:39 (five minutes). Both times it renewed the stand-by without acting in the world.

**What the captain's chart and readings showed:** the browser chart draws the ship "by her account" (`client/map.js` l.1380, "the chart by account"; M5 §17; M7b §12a). From the moment the best bower was `DOWN`, the account stopped moving, because `Navigation._riding()` is true whenever any anchor is down (`reckoning.py` l.2322–2329; `_peg`: "riding at anchor or aground, when the account stays where it is"). She then sailed about a mile in eleven minutes at 5–7 kn with the anchor dragging. The account, and with it the chart's ship, stayed at the let-go point. That is the "mile WNW of the account" the 09:44 fix found. `depth_of_water` ("at the position by account") would likewise have kept reading about 45 fm while she crossed into a fathom and a half.

**What the lookout said:** one notable land-ahead at 09:31, at a mile, before the let-go. The urgent ("Land close ahead … She will be on it in four minutes") should have come at about 09:39. It never came, because `Lookout._land_ahead` returns nothing when `_riding(world)`, and `_riding` is `world.at_anchor`, which means any anchor down (`lookout.py` l.938, l.1273–1277; `core/world.py` l.855). The docstring states the intent: "nothing at anchor, aground or without way".

**Judgement:** the owner's complaint is accurate, and its two causes are **bugs, not realism**:
- The game treats "an anchor is down" as "she is riding", although she is dragging under sail at 5–7 knots.
- That one predicate silenced both channels a captain uses to catch the error, the chart by account and the lookout, at the moment they mattered.

The drag alarm compounded it. Its only cure, "veer more cable, or let go the small bower", is right for a ship riding and wrong for one sailing: more scope gave her more room to sail.

The captain also made real seamanship errors, and owned them in game at 09:45: "my lack of attention and failure to bring her head up as the anchor dragged".
- He let go at about 7 kn with sail drawing, instead of `bring ship to anchor` or rounding up. The officer had recommended both at 09:11.
- He belayed the topsail's take-in.
- He belayed the lead.
- He let go in 46 fm.

A real master would have been saved by the leadsman, by the shout from forward and by hands taking the sail off first. In the game:
- the lead was belayed;
- the shout was suppressed;
- the furl, by the director's reading of the log, queued behind the cable.

**What would have prevented it**, in order of leverage:
1. The account running whenever she moves over the ground, anchor down or not. Then the chart, the depth by chart and the fixes all agree.
2. The lookout's land-ahead keyed on her motion over the ground, not on `at_anchor`.
3. `let go` warning, or doubting, when she has way on with sail drawing: "she has seven knots on her; the anchor will not bite".
4. A drag line that sees sail set and way on: "she is dragging under sail at six knots: take the sail off her".
5. Taking sail off before veering cable.
6. In-world, the director voicing Pascoe at 09:39, which was legitimate world texture and four minutes before she struck.
7. Terra steering a cutter in July to the small-vessel summer berth the same port words give (15–20 brazas nearer the town), rather than the any-wind winter berth of 35–40.

## What worked well

## Open questions for the owner
