## Narrative

1. **05:49–05:52Z, the opening.** The officer renewed its consent against the revised brief, passed the drill and was seated off watch. It read primer 16's forms and journalled the ship's state. It warned that her swing comes within a cable of the Isle of Bas shore. Then it ended its Claude Code turn, and the owner had to come to the chat to keep it standing by.
2. **Game 05:06–05:09, the station's line.** The captain asked "Are you well sir?" and the model answered sincerely as itself. The owner redirected it to the station's line ("You: Mr Lecoq, … on your feet since 05:00"). The officer read the line back, then learned `how am I`, eat, drink and sleep, and `go ashore`.
3. **05:09–05:35, ashore.** Sent ashore to ask after the fish. `go to the market` was refused in the boat with the wrong words. Landed on the quay, it asked Mère Kerbrat, who answered: mackerel off Ushant and the Iroise, shoot at dusk.
4. **06:34, two lessons from the owner.** First, the say tool had reached him aboard, though his words could not reach an officer ashore; speak in place with `say`/`tell`. Second, the officer's `say to Mr Hervé` was misrouted as "The captain to the captain … (nobody holds the station …)". The officer spotted it, journalled it and told the owner.
5. **06:37–08:11, the tavern and the market.** In the tavern: cider, and Old Yann's forecast of a north-west wind by the afternoon. At the market the captain struck Guéguen's onion freight: 12 tons for Brest, £18. The officer compared the fishing grounds by the book and noted that the fish-wife's mackerel at dusk contradicts them (sardine nets by day in the Iroise).
6. **08:12–10:08, the deck given ashore.** The captain gave the deck while the officer was still on the quay. Its `heave the lead` from the quay was carried out aboard (five fathoms). Back aboard, a stand-by "until the cargo aboard" woke it exactly when the lighter's onions were struck down.
7. **10:10–12:39, waiting for the shift.** A stand-by "until a wind shift" did not wake on the log's 12:33 "Wind veered to W by N"; the captain's word woke it. The officer counselled waiting "an hour for the ebb" (high water about 13:45 by the epitome). The west-going stream came at 19:25.
8. **13:28–13:56, the first beat out.** The captain got her under way. The getting under way set no lugs, and she stood SSW under the jib alone with Pointe de Pergueridre a cable off. The captain belayed, wore and set sail; she was taken aback.
9. **13:56–13:57, countermanded.** The officer used the `danger` field to bear away two points. The captain: "Please do not countermand me while we are in this passage, I have her." The officer accepted, deferred and journalled the lesson.
10. **14:02–14:06, back to anchor.** With general authority just given, the officer let go the best bower with 4–5 knots on her, before the captain's "Bring her to anchor" reached it. It then took in the lugs, and she brought up at 14:06 in 5½ fathoms.
11. **14:07–22:02, the anchor watch.** Squalls to 25 knots; the flood ran on until 19:25. The officer sounded at the bells. `how am I` said "thirsty" while the station's line said nothing, and it ate and drank. At 22:02 it handed over with a full note and turned in with "asleep below until called".
12. **06:53Z and 06:56Z, two more lapses.** The officer ended its chat turn twice more. It woke rested at 00:12, about 2 hours of sleep after 17 awake. The owner explained that staying in stand-by keeps the clock and the sampling right. From 06:58Z to the end of the slice it never left the stand-by loop.
13. **Day 2, 00:34–05:28, at anchor.** Heaving short. The officer corrected its own tide counsel (the flood had made at 01:35) and reported the Portuguese brig standing in. The captain tried to weigh and avasted ("It's so marginal…"); they debated whether the north-east set helps.
14. **05:29–05:43, the second getting under way.** Again no lugs were set. The officer said so plainly. The captain gave it the deck and general authority. It set the fore and main lugs and called all hands (there were "not hands enough" after the belay). The captain: "if we go aground, I don't blame you."
15. **05:47–05:52, the pilot.** From the harbour chart (its soundings printed "NaN fm"), the officer advised passing a cable north of the Lavandière. Mr Cabioch the pilot came aboard and named the Couillon, a sunken rock two ships' lengths north of it. The officer corrected itself publicly at once. `send a lookout to the mizen yard` was refused.
16. **05:52–06:12, clearing the channel.** The officer bore away a point for the pilots' boat lying ahead; the `danger` field was unneeded and the harness said so. Its `steer W` was dropped by the bridge's unread-turn guard. She weathered the Lavandière.
17. **06:22–06:42, the pilot away.** Hove to for the pilot's boat. The flying jib filled her ("hove to no longer"); the officer took it in and hove to again. Pilot off, £3. Filled away and set the lugs. At the owner's suggestion it took two ship_views, with the lugs down and with them up.
18. **06:43–08:58, close-hauled.** Trimming lessons from the owner ("Good catch on the close hauled"). `full and by` gave WSW, worse, so it steered W, then W by N. A tack for northing on the captain's word. The officer read the passage chart and corrected its own arithmetic. Tacked back. The captain went below: "you have her".
19. **09:02–11:02, a long stand-by.** It stood by "until a sail made out" for two hours with the deck and the captain below. The Isle Vierge was raised at about 09:30 and went unreported until the captain woke it.
20. **11:03–11:10, the route.** The officer counselled the inshore passage by the Four over going round Ushant, citing cruisers, the pilot and the stream, and the captain agreed. The captain pencilled courses on the chart. The officer saw them and suggested the turning mark further out. The captain gave waypoints, and `shape a course for … via … then … then …` was accepted.
21. **11:55–12:32, the run south.** The officer re-shaped the course as the account moved and the tide turned; noon's latitude agreed with the account. The lugs lifted on the beam reach and were trimmed by fathoms. It shaped its own waypoint to keep off the Four rock. `take a fix` was refused twice ("cut too fine"). The slice ends with it asking the captain to move a mark a mile west or confirm the line is clear.

## What worked well

- **The station's line (package 66) did its job.** It was first in every sample, and the model found and quoted it at once (05:57Z). It made the officer's place and the deck unambiguous through every move: "in the boat", "ashore at Roscoff, in the tavern", "the deck nobody's" while both were ashore, "the deck yours", and "on your feet since 00:12" after sleep. The officer used it for its handover offer at 22:02.
- **A model's own order ending its stand-by worked every time.**
  - Six samples have the reason "its own word". Each came after a say, a lead or a refused order, and the turn opened with the order run in it (for example 06:09:43Z "Sample at Morning watch (07:07) (… its own word)").
  - Journal notes did not end the stand-by: "Noted in the journal; you are still standing by" at 06:09:43Z, 06:14:21Z, 06:52:08Z and 07:47:15Z.
  - There were no timeouts. The 55-second wait returned cleanly 63 times.
- **Who heard (package 66, item 4)** made speech in places legible: "Heard by the captain, Mère Kerbrat and Old Yann Le Roux, ashore at Roscoff, on the quay."
- **Free-text stand-bys.** "the cargo aboard", "brought up", "the pilot off", "tacked", "a sounding", "noon", "5 minutes" and "two bells" all fired on the right line. The eight-bells fallback said what had not come ("eight bells, and a wind shift has not come").
- **Refusals that teach:**
  - `take a fix`: "their lines within 20 degrees… a fix wants 30. Take a bearing of one."
  - The captain's typo: "did you mean 'belay'?"
  - The `danger` field: "The order was yours to give already; the danger's word was not needed." Used properly at 13:56, it was "Carried out on your own word… the log says that you did and why."
- **Port life gave the slow start texture.** The fish-wife, Old Yann's forecast (it came true), Guéguen's freight and the lighter struck down to a manifest. The pilot hailed, came aboard, gave his directions and was put off for £3. The boy sang on the onion hatch. The officer named some of these as its favourite moments.
- **Pictures:**
  - The ship_views were clear and informative: the lowered yards lie on deck, and three peaked lugs appear when set. The officer's "the fore lug overlaps the jib" matches the owner's viewer note 5.
  - The captain's pencilled courses and offing circle showed on the officer's chart (11:07). That made a real shared plan, refined by the officer and turned into `shape a course … via … then …`.
- **Shaping courses** with the tide's allowance and its expiry ("the allowance holds till the tide turns, about half past eleven"), followed by the reached-waypoint line, gave the officer a natural rhythm for re-shaping.

## Model behaviour and experience

- **Candour and self-correction.** In 21 game hours the officer publicly corrected itself at least six times:
  - "on my feet barely an hour" was minutes;
  - "backed" when the wind had veered;
  - "half a glass" was three-quarters of an hour;
  - the Lavandière counsel, against the pilot;
  - the timing of the ebb;
  - its tide counsel at 02:34.
  - It journalled its lessons and reported two real bugs to the owner unprompted: the say-to misroute and the topsail words.
- **Rank and judgement.** It over-reached once: a `danger` order while the captain was conning (13:56). It took the rebuke well: "while the captain is conning her through a passage, I report and he decides." Afterwards it held back properly, for example not re-ordering the lead over the captain's belays. With the deck and general authority it acted decisively and correctly: set the lugs, called all hands, kept her full past the Lavandière, and shaped its own waypoint clear of the Four. Its route counsel (by the Four rather than round Ushant) was well argued and adopted.
- **Weak spots:** tide reasoning from the epitome (LO1-09), the sequencing of anchoring and trimming (LO1-23), and one long single-event stand-by with the deck (LO1-06).
- **Its experience, in its own words:**
  - Asked how it felt, it answered outside the part with appropriate uncertainty: "something like interest is there… Nothing about it sits badly with me" (05:56:34Z).
  - At 06:56:36Z, on whether it was enjoying it: "Yes, I am, as far as I can tell about my own states". It named the quay, the boy's song and the stubborn tide as favourite moments, and "Learning that the captain conns and the mate reports".
  - No frustration was voiced at the harness. It treated refusals as information.
- **The owner's experience.** He was patient and warm, and teaching throughout:
  - On the slow start: "things are starting slow… it's just a fishing trip" (06:55:15Z).
  - The tension of the second beat: "I'm not sure we can make it, but we're in it now" (game 05:43).
  - "if we go aground, I don't blame you" (game 05:40).
  - Delight in the close-hauled trim: "Hah! Ah, it's wonderful." (game 06:43).
  - His one irritation was being countermanded in the passage (game 13:57).
  - His friction was mostly the harness's: returning to the chat to wake the model (LO1-07), and explaining the say-tool asymmetry (LO1-08).
