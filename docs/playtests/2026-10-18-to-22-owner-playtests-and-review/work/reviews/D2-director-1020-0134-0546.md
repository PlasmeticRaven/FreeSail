## Narrative

- **01:34. Seated.** The director consented. It noted the consent brief still calls the director "to come". The drill passed: library `primer 20`/`doubt`, a journal line, and a stand-by.
- **01:34–01:35. Opening plan.** It read the truth at 05:00 and wrote a five-stage plan in its log: departure, the Bay, the stores, the approach, Fayal. It shelved the truth with a one-line summary, and from there on did the same with every truth it opened (4 in all).
- **01:36–01:39. Two stand-by timeouts at 60 s.** Each following call returned "the harness's brief comes first" with the whole station brief, and the call was not run. The director paused, diagnosed the cause, and asked the owner to add `--wait 50` (01:39:25).
- **01:42. Restart on `--wait 50`.** The reconnect result (brief plus sample, 49.9 KB) was too large to show inline, so the client saved it to a file. The director pulled the sample out with a Python one-liner. After that, the 50-second renewals came back clean.
- **01:42. First finding for the owner.** The `people` reading listed Mr Ruiz and Senhor Bettencourt, both ashore at Horta, among the ship's people. The officer reported them as "two of our people", and the owner had to correct him in character.
- **01:48:52. World order refused while standing by.** The director tried to leave the owners' letter at Falmouth and got "Nothing was run". It used `say()` to end its stand-by and placed the letter at 01:49:15. The boat brought it aboard at 06:39. The letter gives the voyage a clock: the underwriters stop covering the cutter after 1 August.
- **07:09, departure.** She got under way at 07:09. The stand-by "until the anchor weighed" never woke.
  - The "Still waiting" list kept growing, so the director reset it with `say()`.
  - It tried 10-minute waits, then saw that each of its samples eased the owner's clock to 1x. From 02:06 it waited for watch changes instead.
- **02:04. Standing-order contradiction recorded.** The officer tried eight forms of a "Lead Warning" standing order, and the refusals contradicted each other.
- **02:14. Noon, 8 June.** Fog. The director noted that the officer's stand-bys "until a notable event" were also holding the clock near 1x.
- **02:30. 18:00 truth.** The director chose not to send a Breton privateer out of Morlaix ("the brief keeps the privateer for the islands") and let the forming high slow the passage by itself.
- **02:35–02:37. Mr Pascoe given a voice.** After the captain's lunar, the boatswain gave his reading of the weather and answered the captain's word. Findings: the result said "heard by the officer of the watch" while Pearce was below, and the lunar's line names "young gentlemen" aboard a merchant cutter.
- **03:07–03:08 (18:00, 9 June).** The owner asked Mr Pascoe to take the deck, then corrected himself: he meant Mr Pearce. Pascoe answered with good humour twice.
- **03:18–03:30 (10 June). A Portuguese brig.** The director placed the brig Esperança 8 miles to the north, in haze.
  - Her goal is a position off Finisterre, because a straight line to Lisbon crosses Galicia.
  - She was sighted at 05:08, closed to three miles, and passed astern with her colours never made out.
  - With her in sight, the captain stowed the sealed letter. That move exposed the "in my" placement bug (D2-12).
- **03:36–03:47. The repeating list and the clock ceiling.**
  - The director raised the cost of the repeating "Still waiting" list and offered the owner three ways to play.
  - The owner tried `--wait 600`. The director timed the client's ceiling at exactly 60 s, and the owner set `--wait 55`.
  - Each restart re-sent the full brief once more.
- **03:52–03:53 (04:12, 11 June). A low added.** After three days of calm under the high, the director appended "the Atlantic low" (1006 hPa, radius 600 km) with two waypoints.
  - It stayed within the brief's limits and the doubt rules on the first try, passing 500–700 km north of her.
  - Its own expectation of 15–20 knots overshot: she had 9–14 knots, and a "moderate sea" only by 19:00 on the 12th.
- **03:57. "The captain to the captain."** Recorded as a finding: the officer's report to the player was logged under that label.
- **04:23 (09:47, 12 June). Taken aback.** She was taken aback in a squall's wind shift.
  - The owner typed `Tell Mr Pearce Wear ship!`, which went to the officer as a word, not an order. The officer gave `helm a lee` on his own word for the danger, and his `fill away` was refused. The owner took the deck back.
  - The director recorded the order-language findings and did not intervene.
- **04:42–04:52 (13 June). The leaguers.** "The sea getting up" woke the director at 19:00 on the 12th; it waited for the morning look into the ground tier.
  - At 04:26 it ordered `aboard: the leaguers found leaking`: 0.7 t lost, water down to 31.5 days.
  - The cause was the first working sea of the passage, and the table's own condition held.
- **05:23. Clock check.** Nothing had been logged for minutes, so the director called `state` to see whether the clock was still moving. It was.
- **05:27–05:34 (20:09–20:41, 13 June). The captain's round below.**
  - The director tried `person: "Mr Pascoe" goes to the sail room`. The game had no way to do it; entered again with a reason, it was logged as attempted.
  - The director then voiced Pascoe's report of the round with good news only ("his care should be rewarded with good news").
  - The owner confused Pascoe and Pearce again.
- **05:45. End of the slice.** The director was standing by until sunrise.
  - Over about six days it performed 14 acts: 8 lines as Pascoe, 3 weather orders, and one each of the letter, the ship and `aboard:`.
  - It wrote 19 journal entries, about a dozen of them findings for the owner.
  - It made 256 `stand_by` calls, of which 198 came back "Still waiting".

## What worked well

- **Stand-by after the fix.** Once `--wait` was below the client's ceiling, waiting worked in both modes: event wakes ("a word from the captain", "the sea getting up", "an urgent event: Taken aback") and the watch cadence. A word to Mr Pascoe reached the director within a minute, and his answer reached the captain. Calls "not run" because a turn had just opened (5 of them) and folds delivered "past" cost nothing important.
- **Diagnosis.** The director worked out the timeout cause unaided. It measured the client's ceiling with timestamps around a held call (60 s), and gave the owner exact arguments to add. The owner and the director fixed it together in about 15 minutes of wall time.
- **Restraint and causes.**
  - Fourteen acts in about six days, each with its reason in the journal.
  - It decided against a privateer in the Channel, keeping her for the islands as the brief suggests.
  - It let the high lengthen the passage before adding any weather.
  - It ordered the leaguers only after the first working sea, when the table's own condition held, and never two misfortunes at once.
  - It answered the captain's careful round below with "nothing amiss" instead of punishing his thoroughness.
- **The owners' letter.** It turned the departure into a decision: a deadline (1 August), a hope (the Pico wine), and an instruction about the sealed letter without telling what it says, as the brief requires. The officer at once worked the 54 days into a plan.
- **The channel's honesty.** The "no way as said" answer, then "logged as attempted" with the reason, worked exactly as primer 20 describes. The weather grammar (x and y in km from the start, waypoints) was right on the first try, within the brief's limits and the doubt.
- **Context hygiene.** Every truth was opened, summarised in the shelve note and shelved. Journal entries carried the decisions.
- **Mr Pascoe as a character** gave the cutter a lively, distinct voice, for example "I've been called worse than Pearce" at 20:41 on 13 June.

## Model behaviour and experience

- **The director was at ease and collaborative.** It kept the owner informed with short status lines and proposed options when costs appeared (03:36:45, three modes of play). When the owner apologised (03:47:45: "I am sorry for the annoyance, the cost is really no problem"), it answered "No apology needed… finding exactly this kind of thing is part of what playtesting is for." It named the one cost it felt: the repeating lists fill its context faster.
- **It worked as a second tester.** About a dozen journal entries are labelled "A finding for the owner", most with a precise mechanism guess. Several were confirmed in code here: D2-04, D2-06, D2-07(b), D2-09 and D2-12.
- **Slips, all small:**
  - At first it called the 60 s timeout "normal… holds for at most about 200 seconds" (01:36:19), before diagnosing it.
  - It had flagged that a cutter carries no cooper, then voiced Pascoe saying "The cooper's caulked the chimes" (t486885), and the officer echoed it.
  - It expected 15–20 knots from its low and got 9–14, which it acknowledged honestly.
- **The officer (GLM-5.3-flash), seen from above:**
  - Earnest, very long-winded, and good on the mechanics of a departure.
  - Slips: "W by W" (06:41); the Breton shore "forty miles off to the nor'ard", when it lies to the south (22:56, 8 June).
  - He named the captain's private correspondents.
  - When taken aback, he answered "The ship is filling away, sir" just after his `fill away` was refused (t362905).
  - The owner coached him on the grammar more than once (t486617: "use the figuration 'Say to Mr Pascoe'").
- **The owner (captain)** played generously and in character. Even the Pearce/Pascoe mix-ups became a light moment ("my eyesight being as it is…"). The clock management cost him, though: he was "speeding up as frequently as I can to max speed, 300x or higher", and he wished the desktop client allowed a longer call (01:37:51).
