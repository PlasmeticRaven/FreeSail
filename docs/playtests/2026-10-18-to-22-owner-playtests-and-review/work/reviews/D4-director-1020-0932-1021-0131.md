## Narrative

1. **09:32–09:40Z.** The owner laughs off the GLM mate's emoji slip.
   - At 09:38 the owner proposes a scheduled wake in place of the held stand-by call.
   - The director explains that a pushed MCP notification would not start a turn. It proposes a background "await" command plus a cheap fix: renewals that list only the lines since the previous call.
   - The owner defers both to the lead ("a real work package", 09:40:24Z). The director promises short waits ("a watch") to keep the notable lists small.

   - Pascoe sights a sleeping turtle and asks leave to heave to. The captain heaves to.
   - "Send the boat out" is refused at sea. The director tells the catch in words and makes the turtle a thing in the galley, with no stores effect, and journals why.
3. **23 Jun.** The cook keeps the turtle alive "by the coppers" and serves it: soup for the hands, calipee for the cabin.
   - The captain's "Go to the galley" puts him in the gunroom, so his pick-up of the turtle reads "as said; nothing changed" (t1292416–457).

   - "A whaler" is refused as a far shape, so she goes in as merchant brig *Lydia*, with Captain Coffin in Quaker speech and his news planned.
   - She is ordered to "steer NE by E" to close, but she passes astern twice (24 and 25 Jun) without coming within hail.
   - A lookout line about her try-works smoke is refused ("nobody answering to 'the lookout'"), and the director lets it drop.
5. **25 Jun.** Back on half allowance.
   - The captain issues the owners' Madeira; the director moves the case to the galley, has Pascoe share it out, and later returns "one bottle and a heel".
   - It also journals a light hook for Horta: the case was the owners'.
6. **26 Jun 04:46 (11:52Z).** The weather intervention.
   - The truth shows the climatology's summer highs off Portugal, holding the cutter in westerly calms ("19 days and 500 mi still to go").
   - The director asks the owner first, by AskUserQuestion. The owner picks "Restore it (Recommended)".
   - It moves its own Azores high by waypoint. The order to move seeded "high 9" is refused. It adds an Iberian heat low.
   - The northerly arrives within the hour, at once rather than over the day, and the glass jumps. The director journals that systems "step in instead of ramping".
7. **26–28 Jun.** The best runs of the voyage: 107, 147, 156–162 miles a day.
   - The OpenRouter 402 drops the GLM mate, and the owner reseats claude-sonnet-5-5.
   - The director logs the reseat's losses (his reckoning, the captain's grant) and the new mate's "I've eaten and drunk" with no such order in the log.
8. **27 Jun 09:38 onward.** The director finds the routing bug: the mate's "Tell the captain…" and "Say to Mr Bowen…" become "The captain to the captain: … (nobody holds the station)".
   - It tracks the bug over three days and finds that only the open "Say, Mr Bowen, …" reaches the deck.
9. **28 Jun.** The truth shows the account 40, then 56 miles west of the truth.
   - The director decides not to reveal it, and notes that the error is "safe side: they come late to the land". It keeps planning the privateer.
10. **29 Jun 04:00 (12:56Z).** Before dawn and beyond sight (about 34 nm), the director places the topsail schooner *Basque* of France, no colours, with the intent of keeping station off Santa Maria. It makes Capitaine Etcheverry (8 guns, 60 men).
    - Its journal sets the rules: a chase ends within hail, in words; no gunfire; nobody dies; refuge under São Brás.
11. **29 Jun 05:53.** Landfall.
    - The Pico Alto of Santa Maria and the east end of São Miguel; the cross bearings move the account 17½ leagues east, good to two miles.
    - The director calls it "a fine moment, and the game did it truly".
12. **06:58–07:13.** The director re-aims the *Basque* onto the cutter's new track.
    - Its first order is dropped ("Nothing was run") because it was still standing by.
    - It ends the stand-by with `say("Land.")` and `say("The channel.")`, then shapes her course and changes her intent to the east end of São Miguel within 25 miles.
13. **13:00.** The *Basque* is sighted 2 leagues to leeward. The director orders "give chase", and Pascoe names her: raked masts, no colours, "the black schooner".
    - The chase order holds only half an hour, so it is renewed 8 times, 13:00–16:17.
    - In the truth she closes slowly, from 7.7 to 5 miles.
14. **15:48.** Fog comes down from the weather systems (no director weather order since 26 Jun; checked in the save).
    - The director stops renewing the chase. Pascoe suggests hauling the wind, silent; the mate declines to heave to on a relayed word; the captain heaves to.
    - The director steers the *Basque* WNW toward the cutter's last seen course, an honest pursuit. At 17:03 the truth has her passing 7 mi WSW, never in sight.
    - The director gives her up and turns her intent back to Santa Maria (13:22Z).
15. **29 Jun 19:42.** The fog lifts on an empty horizon.
    - The captain stands off and on for the night.
    - Next morning, in fog off Ponta Delgada, the director gives Pascoe church bells for Sunday Mass after checking that 30 June 1805 was a Sunday. It was.
16. **30 Jun forenoon.** The game's pilot Mr Pacheco boards. The director voices him (news of the Bayonne schooner; Medeiros the chandler; the spring under the fort).
    - Anchored 12:17 in 35 fathoms; the cable lines contradict each other.

17. **14:00–14:05Z.** The debrief.

## What worked well

- **Restraint with the truth.** The director knew for two days that the account was 40–56 mi west of the truth and said nothing to the players. The game's own cross-bearing fix revealed it (t1817587, "good to two miles"). The navigation, the lunars, the noon latitudes and the fix all played as designed.
- **Consent before a large world change.** "I said I'd leave the weather be, so I'll ask rather than act" (11:52:16Z), followed by a clear three-option question with a recommendation. The northerly that followed made the best days of the passage.
- **The chase stayed inside the brief.**
  - A French privateer off the islands was explicitly allowed. The topsail schooner of 8 guns sits under the brig limit, and nobody died.
  - She was placed before dawn beyond sight, within §3's rule that a new sail must not appear inside the horizon.
  - The director set its own rules: no gunfire, the chase ends in words, a fort as refuge, and her speed kept true ("faster to windward… in light airs, not in a fresh breeze").
  - When fog fell it stopped renewing the chase, steered her for the cutter's last seen course rather than the true one, and gave her up when the truth showed her 7 mi off and blind (13:22:32Z).
  - Re-aiming her onto the cutter's new track (13:00:52Z) was the director arranging the encounter. A privateer cruising the channel between the islands is a plausible cause, so it stays within the rule.
- **The director's ship orders and refusals worked.** "Give chase" ran 8 of 8 times for the *Basque*. The *Lydia*, intents and persons were all made in one try or after one honest refusal. Each refusal named the forms (whaler; seeded high; the lookout).
- **People and things made the voyage.** The turtle, the cook, Spencer's glass, the Madeira kept and returned "one bottle and a heel", Pascoe's fog advice, the pilot's news. Every one stayed consistent with the things machinery (§17a): the case moved, no money was set.
- **Journalling as a bug tracker.** The director turned 38 journal notes into a precise bug list and a stand-down note full enough for a successor. Its chat summary at 13:56:21Z is a good lead-session input.
- **Period checks.** It checked that 30 June 1805 was a Sunday (it was: 1 Jan 1805 was a Tuesday, day 181). It placed whalers on the Western Islands ground in summer and took heat-low/nortada reasoning from the real June pattern.

## Model behaviour and experience

**The director's self-report (14:00:25Z), checked against the record.** Overall it is accurate and unusually self-critical.

- **"The best directing turned out to be mostly restraint."**
  - The record agrees: 81 turns opened in the span, and most ended in a lone stand-by.
  - It says it "took maybe forty acts". The save has 87 director world orders done between 8 and 30 June, 46 of them `say as`. That leaves 41 other acts, so the figure matches its world-shaping acts and leaves out its speech (inference). In this span alone it gave 53 orders, about 48 done.
- **"The rule of reach and the doubt check almost never got in my way."** True for this span. There were four refusals (whaler, the lookout, the seeded high, the order dropped during a stand-by) and no doubts. Each refusal, in its words, "told me something true about the world".
- **Most constrained: "Steering other ships. This was the one that actually cost the story."**
  - Confirmed: the *Lydia* passed twice unspoken, and the chase needed 8 renewals. Its asks are specific: an intercept or "speak" intent, colours, signals.
  - It also says "`give chase` was also refused once". That was 15 Jun, in director-3. In this span the order worked every time for a French ship chasing a British cutter. The earlier refusal was of a merchant brig, which §3's doubt covers ("a chase given by a master of the merchant's… doctrine"), yet it was answered as "no way as said". The refusal's wording is a question for the director-3 reviewer.
- **Weather: "I misused them once."**
  - The 1039-hPa overshoot "and then needed a second act to undo" happened on 19 Jun (director-3: the glass at 1039, eased to 1018). It did not happen in this span.
  - Its line that the overshoot is "the kind of overreach a director should avoid" is fair self-criticism.
  - Its "season check" request is the right remedy for D4-07.
- **Stand-by.** It is a cost rather than a constraint: the lists "buried the lines that mattered, so I sometimes skimmed past a real signal." This is a direct cost to director quality, and D4-04 and D4-05 are the levers.
  - At 09:38:39Z it was precise about the harness: "MCP notifications don't start turns, so I'd sit idle until you typed something." Then, plainly: "The timeouts don't annoy me." Its complaint is the size of each renewal, not the timeouts.
