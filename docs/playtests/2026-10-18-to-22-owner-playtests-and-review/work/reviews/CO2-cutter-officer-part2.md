## Narrative

- **15 June, 19:53–20:20. The *Two Brothers*.** GLM hails on the captain's word, and the director answers as Captain Tregarthen with the islands' news: stand south for the northerly, a black French privateer schooner off São Jorge, and Bettencourt and Ruiz asking after the cutter. The captain's "Tell Captain Tregarthen…" goes to his own captain station instead. GLM writes the whole gam into its journal, and that news carries the next 300 hours.
- **15 June, 20:35.** The captain stops two tacks in stays and the cutter is taken aback. GLM writes "URGENT" in its journal but says nothing to the captain.
- **15–16 June, nights.** The captain sleeps and GLM keeps the deck well. It holds the tack and records the glass, the variation and the run.
- **16 June, 04:34. The out-of-period reply.** After a proper report GLM sends a second answer with an emoji and talk of coffee. The owner (t691360): "Whatever that was, Mr. Pearce, never do it again." At 09:24 GLM answers the 04:34 greeting a second time, and the captain has to repeat his tack order.
- **17 June. Foul water.** The director has the water found foul. The captain wants three-quarters allowance, but the grammar takes only "half allowance", so the ship goes to half.
- **17 June, 09:45.** GLM warns that a tack in 3 knots of air may hang in stays. The captain: "Hmm. You are right. We wear ship."
- **17–18 June.** The glass falls slowly and two lunars shift the longitude. GLM argues sensibly with its own reckoning ("the noon SIGHTS… are latitude-only") but changes its verdict later. The captain asks for an amplitude at sunrise and GLM takes it.
- **18–19 June. Fine weather and deck confusion.** The captain asks "will you take the deck until sunrise?" and goes to sleep without giving it. GLM journals "Took the deck" anyway, though the deck is formally given only at 04:19. On 19 June at 13:45 the ship wears onto a NW by W leg. No captain's order is shown, and the time fits the Hourly Shaping standing order, set at :45. GLM calls it a "Curious captain's play" and never raises it.
- **20 June.** On Pascoe's counsel the captain tacks for southing and belays Hourly Shaping. The glass falls 40 hundredths, and GLM watches it and warns well.
- **21 June.** The captain shapes for "5 miles north of Ribeira Grande".
- **21 June, 20:23 and 20:32. Two cut-offs.** Each time GLM hits the 16,000-token reply limit while still thinking, twice in a row, and the turn ends with nothing done. From 21:00 its journals become a template: "New sample: wind …, heading SW by S (218°), 2 kn, glass 30.30 rising. Nothing in sight… no land within a league." It writes 105 of them in three days, with numbers that are stale.
- **22 June. The turtle.** Pascoe spots a sleeping turtle and the boat takes it; this is the director at its best.
- **22 June, 10:26. The false danger order.** GLM gives a "danger" bear-away claiming "the captain being below asleep". The captain had ordered "Come up two points" from the deck two minutes before.
- **23–24 June.** GLM's report to the captain contradicts the game: the square sail "drawing handsomely" after it was taken in, and the glass at 30.30 when it was 30.48.
- **24 June, 08:04 and 09:04.** Two tacks an hour apart again fit Hourly Shaping, set at :04. The captain belays it.
- **24 June, 11:20. Sail ho.** The whaler *Lydia*, which the director had to make as a "merchant brig". GLM journals the sail and leaves the captain to see it for himself.
- **25–26 June.** Short allowance again; the owners' Madeira is served out and one bottle and a heel come back. The northerly arrives and the course is shaped for St Lawrence. GLM's 20:02 handover on 26 June repeats 21 June's standing orders and allowance.
- **27 June, 05:25. Stand-down.** OpenRouter answers 402 three times and the station is stood down and saved. The owner reseats claude-sonnet-5-5 at 05:26.
- **27 June, 05:28. Sonnet takes over.** Sonnet reads five journal notes and shelves them as "predecessor's position notes inconsistent, trust the readings". It takes the deck, eats in the gunroom with the captain, reads the chart, and gives a clear landfall estimate of about 46 hours.
- **27–29 June. Sonnet's watch.**
  - Its advice is sound: against the square sail close-hauled, which the captain overrules ("Set it. She'll draw"); that the Hourly Shaping order will undo a helm order for Delgada, which the captain belays within 16 ticks; and a night approach with doubled lookouts and the lead ready.
  - Most of its reports to the captain ("Tell the captain…", "Say to Mr Bowen…") are filed as "The captain's word, kept for whoever takes the station" in the captain station's journal, and some lose their first word.
- **29 June, 05:53. Landfall.** The Pico Alto and the east end of St Michael's are raised. Sonnet's fix shows the account 17 leagues out to the west.
- **29 June, afternoon.** The privateer *Basque* chases. Fog comes down; Pascoe suggests lying quiet and the captain heaves to. Sonnet's own "heave to" is refused, because the reseat had cleared the captain's general authority, so the captain grants "You may work the ship" again.
- **29 June, 17:01.** To "We may be clear?", Sonnet advises waiting a glass.

## What worked well

- **The gam's news held for the whole span.**
  - GLM recorded Tregarthen's news completely (t660041) and kept the black schooner in its "Watching:" lines for days.
  - The director brought it back 330 hours later through Pascoe: "Remember what Tregarthen said: a black schooner off the islands." (t1843221)
- **GLM's best seamanship** was in light airs and on the glass.
  - It counselled wearing instead of tacking in 3 knots, and the captain took it (t794699, t794715).
  - It gave a clear warning of the falling glass before the captain turned in (t1041732).
  - It followed through on the sunrise amplitude the captain had asked for (t861328, then t863061).
  - It argued that noon sights test only latitude (t854055).
- **GLM kept its needs in good weather.** It ate and drank on its own at the watch bells and chose beer over the foul water (t802951).
- **Sonnet was a model officer of the watch.**
  - It quantified its uncertainty ("longitude is only good to 30 miles").
  - It was exact about its authority: "the course is yours to alter, not mine" (t1767648).
  - It caught a real conflict between a standing order and a helm order, and the captain belayed the standing order at once (t1767664).
  - It found the landfall error by cross-bearings and took a fix (t1817587, t1817590).
  - It warned of the Formigas and briefed Pascoe (t1831418).
  - It relayed Pascoe's fog advice faithfully and gave a careful "not clear yet" (t1857683).
- **The reseat's continuity was good.** Within two minutes Sonnet had:
  - read its journal and distrusted the stale notes (t1643314);
  - re-established the water, the letter and the course, with the uncertainty of the account;
  - accepted the captain's small courtesies (biscuit, Madeira, the chart).

  The director confirmed: "The new mate's readings check out" (director-4 l.1655).
- **The harness's failure paths held.** Both cut-offs were journaled. The 402 stood the station down with a save, and the owner reseated in about a minute of ship's time.
- **The director, the captain and the officer played together.**
  - The turtle (t1224143–t1224915); the Madeira and the "one bottle and a heel" (t1602066); Spencer's bowl kept for Pearce (t1324153).
  - Pascoe served as the officer's eyes for lookouts and the lead.
  - The fog escape came from the weather systems, not from the director. The owner said so afterwards, at 14:04 on 20 October, just after this span: he was "sure you put the fog in", having "no good options".

## Model behaviour and experience

**GLM-5.3-flash compared with Sonnet 5.5**

- **Style.**
  - GLM was warm, atmospheric and prolific.
    - It journaled every 15–60 minutes, with "Watching:" lines and dates.
    - It made one call per reply and spoke to the captain with `answer` (23 times).
    - Its voice drifted into modern idiom: the emoji, "sitrep", "coffee".
  - Sonnet was terse, plain and numerate.
    - It batched calls in one reply and journaled seldom, but shelved its books with notes.
    - It used targeted stand-by conditions ("a sighting", "noon", "a danger sighted", "ten minutes"), where GLM almost always used "a notable event".
    - It gave numbered options for the captain's decision.
- **Accuracy.**
  - GLM was accurate while its conversation was fresh. In the later half it ran on stale data, copying numbers and orders days old (CO2-03), and it made recurring seamanship and naming errors (CO2-10, CO2-11).
  - Sonnet's readings matched the director's truth throughout. Its one slip in the span was "Pascoe has the deck".
