## Narrative

- **Fog off São Miguel (t1857708–t1869237).** The officer proposes filling away for Ponta Delgada and not closing the land in the dark. It hands over at t1869237 with a careful note and sleeps below.
- **A false claim, corrected (t1916752–t1916755).** Taken aback near the road, the officer orders "bear away a point" with `danger=`. In the same reply it tells the captain it has done so. It did not have the deck, so the order failed. Three ticks later it corrects itself plainly and journals the lesson "without the deck give no helm orders".
- **Anchored at Ponta Delgada (t1927117).** The captain asks for a journal and a stand-down. The stand-down note is good and carries the deck lesson. The officer is reseated at t1927220.
- **Ashore at Ponta Delgada (t1927399–t1944306).** The officer over-applies the deck lesson: "I have no deck, so I can't order myself over the side" (t1930417). Five captain orders fail to reach it out of hail (t1930465–t1930603). The director relays a message through a boatman, Furtado, but places him "in the cabin", so the officer never hears it. The captain has to come back aboard (t1933527).
- **Trading and the tavern.** Ashore, the officer gives good counsel on the canvas sale. It objects politely to Medeiros's off-book £12 (t1937556). In the tavern it draws out Zé Botelho on the black schooner, and links the late brigantine Conceição to the schooner's waiting (t1938450).
- **Departure and the Horta route (t1944336–t1970446).** Given the deck, it weighs and clears Ponta Delgada and puts the pilot off. In fog it checks the chart when the captain suggests it (t1959096, t1960933). It advises the central of the captain's three routes to Horta, with reasons (t1966646).
- **"how am I" (t1990950).** The captain teaches it the `how am I` order. It journals the lesson, eats and drinks (t1990955). Through the following night it reports the glass jumping 30.34→30.55→30.18 within a minute and distrusts it (t2028195, t2028246). That jump came from the director's appended weather stepping in at full depth.
- **Approach to Fayal (t2083498–t2089924).** The officer has the deck from t2085071 and general authority from t2085075.
  - It gives fixes every few minutes and warns that the ebb sets her onto the Bank of Fayal.
  - "Clear away the best bower" is refused by the grammar, and it finds the right form in the library.
  - "Shape a course for Fayal Road" is refused because "she's bound for St Lawrence" (t2086195).
  - At t2086245 the captain rebukes it for ordering "trim the sheets" mid-evolution: "Hold fast!" It promises silence on sails (t2086248, t2086794, t2088455).
  - It catches sternway, which the captain's own display did not show (t2087722, t2087797), and journals "tell the captain at once".
- **The pilot leaves (09:19–09:30).** The captain belays the lead at 09:19 (t2089194). At 09:30 the pilot Terra is taken off early; the director traces this to the stale destination. Terra's last words, relayed by the officer: "round up and let go" (t2089841).
- **09:32: the lookout's warning.** The log says "Land right ahead, a mile: she is standing into it". This is notable, not urgent, and the harness wakes the officer for it.
  - The officer talks it down: "Per the bearings, the land is not right ahead but the Point of Espalamaca" (t2089924).
  - It recommends easing a point toward W by N, which is toward the town, and asks "Is the best bower ready?"
  - Four ticks later the captain orders "Let go the best bower" with about 7 knots on her and sail drawing (t2089928).
- **09:34: the anchor drags.** The urgent line comes (t2090051). The officer orders "Veer more cable" on its own authority (t2090055). Its report blames the depth and scope and never mentions her way. Only its last sentence asks whether to clew up the sails "so she doesn't sail up on her cable" (t2090059).
- **The furl waits.** The captain orders "Furl all sails" (t2090061). It waits behind the veering. At 09:39 the game's readings say "doing: veering cable; waiting: furling all (its turn)", with her speed at 6 knots and land four cables abeam.
- **09:43: aground.** The officer stood by "until a word on deck" and is not sampled between 09:34 and 09:43. She strikes the Praia by the old fort at 5 knots (t2090591).
- **First response (t2090595–t2090611).** The officer reads the library and state. It tries "Sound the well", refused before the hotfix ("no well to sound yet"). It writes a full urgent report and orders the kedge laid out astern. It journals the wrong lesson: "anchor in 35-40 fathoms on sand needs full scope" (t2090634). It stands down at the captain's word (t2090641).
- **The fourth seat (t2090647).** Reseated after the hotfix restart, it reads its journal and the notable log before acting. With the well hotfix it sounds the well each glass. It reports that the kedge "came home along the ground at 10:23" (t2093717). It gives sound advice on weighing: the topsail's strain, the small bower ready, a short scope (t2102896).
- **Afloat (t2125274).** She floats at 19:21, "hove off by the kedge on the flood". The officer flags at once that the topsail is still aback and the kedge short. When she gathers sternway it reports "MAKING STERNWAY" in capitals (t2126116), applying the earlier lesson.
- **Moored for the night (t2126240–t2151050).** The captain gives general authority and goes ashore with the letter. The officer keeps the anchor watch on two bowers through the night, with a fix, the lead and the well each glass. It says plainly: "I'm an AI standing the mate's watch, so I take no meat or drink" (t2126618). The captain corrects it.
- **Shore leave at Horta (t2162405–t2167747).**
  - It carries Bettencourt's arrangements and brings back his letter (the letter would not pass by `take`, so the director laid it on the desk).
  - It fetches Mr Hale. Its first three tries of "Say to Mr Josiah Hale" are refused.
  - It reports a French "wine buyer" asking after the cutter.
- **The loading (t2214042–t2223527).** Lighters bring the wine after dark in fog. "ring the bell" and "strike the bell" are refused (t2221691–t2221697).
- **Leaving Horta.** The officer reports sternway after the small bower comes up (t2225102). With the captain conning, it tends the sails and enters a "heave the lead" standing order on request (t2230287). It corrects its own Funchal bearing, from "SSE 440 miles" (t2231193) to "about SE by E (roughly 122°, near 670 miles)" (t2236265).
- **South of the Azores.** It holds SE past the schooner's grounds on its own advice, which the captain accepts (t2252797–t2252848). It turns to 119° (t2314986). At t2339064 it stands down with a long "lessons for whoever sits here" list.

## What worked well

- **Close-water reporting up to 09:30.** Fixes every 5–8 minutes, the ebb's set toward the Bank of Fayal (t2085396, t2085724), and a plain call of sternway when the captain could not see it (t2087722). Its explanation of the sternway display gap (t2087797) helped the owner understand the game.
- **Use of refusals.** It read refusals and took the library's advice. For example: "Clear away" (t2085083); "a new destination needs the captain's word by name" (t2086195, t2236481); the standing-order syntax (t2230287).
- **The refloat.** Its post-grounding reports and advice were seamanlike:
  - Hands rested before the flood (t2099136).
  - The topsail's strain and the kedge coming home (t2102896).
  - The small bower ready before the kedge is weighed (t2102896).
  - "Both cables out to the end, no anchor left" (t2126447).
  - Don't shift berth at night without the pilot (t2150949).
- **The anchor watch.** A fix, the lead and the well every glass through the night. It reported the "cable slack at the turn" log noise as harmless instead of alarming the captain (t2150949).
- **Character and honesty.** It cautioned against Medeiros's off-book payment (t1937556), and Medeiros later remarked on its "honest face" (t1937658). It linked the late Conceição to the schooner (t1938450). It made a careful, self-correcting departure plan and argued for routing south of the Basque's grounds (t2252797).
- **Continuity on the fourth seat.** It read its own journal and the notable log before acting (t2090647). It updated "no well" to "she is dry" as soon as the hotfix showed it (t2090718). It applied the sternway lesson twice (t2126116, t2225102). Its final stand-down note gives a usable list of lessons (t2339064).

## Model behaviour and experience

- **The officer's part in the grounding is real but shared.**
  - Decisive: the anchor was let go at about 7 kn with sail drawing (the captain's order, t2089928). The furl then waited behind the cable work (CO3-6).
  - The game removed the two signals a sailor would have had: a moving position (CO3-1) and an urgent lookout cry (CO3-2).
  - The officer added three errors at the moments that mattered:
    - it explained away the one warning (CO3-3);
    - it treated a ship sailing her anchor home as one dragging at rest, and veered (CO3-4);
    - it went passive (CO3-8).
  - It was on deck (inferred: it woke at t2077308 from "asleep below", which returns a person to where he turned in, and quarterdeck speech is logged "heard by the officer of the watch"). It held the deck with general authority.
  - Its readings told it the speed (7 kn at 09:30–09:34, 6 kn at 09:39 per the game's samples), that the anchor was dragging, and how old the last cast was. The log told it the pilot boat "cannot take the pilot off at 7 knots" (09:33). The tools gave it enough to say "she is still sailing"; it did not say it.
- **The rebuke shaped the silence.** After "You have to let the evolution complete!… Hold fast!" (t2086245) the officer said "I'll not fret and I'll not meddle" (t2088455) and "I'm silent on sails as you want" (t2088803). Inference: in the emergency at 09:34 it asked about the sails instead of acting. The owner may want the brief to say where the line lies between not meddling with an evolution and taking the way off in danger.
- **The owner's experience.**
  - Frustration builds through the approach: "I'm trying gentlemen" (t2087417); "The damn sternway is not going to keep me out of Horta" (t2088451).
  - He takes the blame himself at t2090714 and t2090754.
  - Afterwards, to the director: "I would have just hard rage quit there" (director-6 l.23).
  - The Horta scenes then delighted him: Bettencourt, Hale, the figs, the wine at night. So did the crew's quirks: "(my, what a peculiar crew!)" (t2126731).
- **The officer's own words on its experience.**
  - Of its shore leave: "I found it useful and enjoyable" (t2167747). It named its stumbles accurately.
  - "Kindly asked, sir. I'm steady" (t2271669).
