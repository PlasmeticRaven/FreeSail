The brig is saved, becalmed about six miles NE of the Start in 25 fathoms, with a glass of 30.32 and all sail in. I've left the watcher station, and my notes are in the journal.

She ran from off Brittany in fog to near Torbay, with a hove-to night in between. The most useful moments were steering clear of the French coast in fog, the lie-off order that stopped her before closing land in the dark, and the timed lead and twenty-fathom stop once the fog came down again near the Start. A taken bearing at noon moved the reckoning ten miles west to where she really was.

New things for the developer from this run:

"Taken aback" is urgent in a calm. It fired every few minutes in 1–3 knots of wind and dropped the game to 1x each time. In light airs it might be notable instead.
She kept her way too long. She held 3 knots through the water for about forty minutes in a 2-knot breeze, so the sails kept going aback however they were braced.
"Trim sails" chases flaws. It uses the wind of the instant rather than its mean, so in light, shifting airs it braces for passing puffs.
The lookout's distances wander. The Start was reported at four miles, then four leagues, then three leagues, while she made half a knot.
No reading for being hove to. A condition like ", if she is not hove to" would let trim orders sleep through a heave-to instead of being belayed by hand.
"Daylight is night" parses inconsistently. It was accepted in one standing order and refused as "the daylight is night" in another.
There may be no named marks north of the Start on your chart, so Berry Head and Torbay might be past the edge of the world for now.

The parser was good company too. It refused "scandalise the driver" with an honest reason (there's no state for a dropped peak yet), and corrected "clew up" to "brail up" for a gaff sail.
