- **Morning watch, 8 bells (04:00)** (note): Free voyage, cutter Sherbourne, October. 04:00 off Ushant, 48°30'N 4°59'W, the light SW by S. No standing orders, no plan. Wind WSW 22 knots, a fresh breeze; glass low at 29.68, tendency not yet known; detached clouds; short chopping sea from the westward. Night until about 06:00, sunrise 06:31, sunset 16:56: under eleven hours of light. 12 hands on deck, 27 in all. She lies head SE, no sail set, which points her at the Molène plateau and the Le Conquet shore, a lee shore in this wind. First thoughts: reef before setting anything, get her head to the north, away from the Iroise, and watch the glass. A 100-mile crossing to Falmouth would mean a landfall after dark.

- **First dog watch (17:51)** (note): End of the free voyage, 17:51: hove to off St Anthony's Head in seven fathoms, good ground, after about 53 miles made good from Ushant in under 14 hours. No damage. Saved there for want of an anchor. THE BEST OF IT: Without a preset plan it felt like real seamanship. The captain fixed her by cross-bearings off the Lizard, Black Head and the Beast at 15:12, found her six miles west of the reckoning (the same westward set the Amazon had), and hauled to NNE to pass outside the Manacles. The weather was the finest yet: mares' tails at 08:00, an oily, hard-edged sky, squalls every half hour veering a point or two and blowing 28-35, the glass jumping after them. The tack off St Anthony's Head at 17:44 was a proper escape. MY OWN MISTAKE: The helm reading gives no side, and I read it as weather helm all day. At 17:06 it was lee helm. My advice to set the jib was wrong, and so may some of this morning's helm calls have been. FOR THE DEVELOPER, in order of how much they mattered: (1) No lights at night. After 17:26 the lookout reported nothing until 'the land about St Anthony's Head close aboard right ahead, a mile'. The Lizard lights and St Anthony's light were reported in daylight but not in the dark, which is backwards. Night pilotage is impossible as it stands. (2) Lead orders conditioned on 'land in sight' go dead at dark, just when they're most needed. The captain added a timed lead order, which worked. (3) Emergency handling: 'tack ship' queued behind the lead and waited. 'avast that' then belayed the tack, not the lead. 'tack ship' was refused until she was close-hauled. Helm orders in danger should preempt routine work. (4) Hands: 'trim sails' braces the furled square sail, topsail and topgallant yards first. That starved the main trim, the headsails and once the lead (16:23, missed for want of hands). (5) Reefs are additive, so a reef standing order fires again on each squall and stacks: the main reached four reefs, 16:58. A 'to N reefs' form, or a ceiling, would help. (6) 'Shoal Water' (heave to under 15 fathoms) hove her to in seven fathoms right after we escaped, where she drifted NE back toward the Head. A good order in open water, a bad one in an entrance. (7) Smaller: 'the Manacles' were never sighted this time, though we passed them 2-3 miles off (the Amazon raised them at 3 miles). The Old Wall and Black Rock were never sighted either. The lookout's list and a bearing taken gave different distances for one mark (five vs three miles). 'Making sternway' was shown at 4 knots. The log over-read by about 15% (noon fix). 'Heavy sea getting up / going down' chattered. state still shows apparent wind 0 kn. Thank you for the voyage. A good boat, and a hard, honest coast.
