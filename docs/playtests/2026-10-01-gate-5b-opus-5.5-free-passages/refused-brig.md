## The lead's reading

Most of these are the grammar doing its job in words (the colon after a standing order's name; `take a bearing of` wanting a mark; `the sails` not a group; `clew up the driver` corrected to `brail up`; `scandalise` refused with the reason), and three are the ship's (no ringtail or save-alls on the brig, the studding-sail booms rigged in). The ones to take: `the daylight is night` refused where `daylight is night` is taken (the article); `belay "blind lead"` and `resume standing order "trim by the wind"` want a standing order by its name case-blind and loosely; `the topmast stuns'ls` as a group of both masts' (the frigate has `the studdingsails` whole, the brig's halves by mast only); `square the sails` and `clew up the sails` want a whole-ship group (`the square sails`, which the cutter's file calls so); `tryce` (trice) is a period word for the driver's tack and could answer with the scandalise refusal.
