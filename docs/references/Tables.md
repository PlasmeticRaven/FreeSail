# Tables read from the page images

The OCR texts under this folder lose their tables. These were read from page images the owner supplied (2026-09-26) and are the versions the game's data should cite. Each carries its source page; a cell the reader was unsure of is marked.

## 1. Luce 1884, Chapter X "Sails", p. 171: Sails for a vessel of the *Trenton* class

The sail allowance of a ship of the *Trenton* class (a wooden screw frigate of 1876, ship-rigged), with the number of each sail carried and the number of the canvas it is made of. Canvas is numbered by weight, No. 1 the heaviest.

| Sail | No. of sails | No. of canvas |
|---|---|---|
| Fore-sails* | 2 | 2 |
| Fore-topsails | 2 | 2 |
| Fore-topgallant-sails | 2 | 4 |
| Fore-royals | 2 | 8 |
| Main-sails | 2 | 2 |
| Main-topsails | 2 | 2 |
| Main-topgallant-sails | 2 | 4 |
| Main-royals | 2 | 8 |
| Mizzen-topsails | 2 | 3 |
| Mizzen-topgallant-sails | 2 | 6 |
| Mizzen-royals | 2 | 9 |
| Flying-jibs | 2 | 5 |
| Jibs | 2 | 2 |
| Fore-trysails | 1 | 1 |
| Main-trysails | 2 | 1 |
| Storm-mizzens | 1 | 1 |
| Spankers | 2 | 2 |
| Fore storm-staysails | 1 | 1 |
| Fore-topmast staysails | 2 | 2 |
| Mizzen storm-staysails | 1 | 1 |

\* Footnote in the original: "One fore-sail, one fore and one main-topsail to be of No. 1 canvas." So of the two foresails and the two fore and main topsails allowed, one of each is a heavy-weather sail of No. 1 canvas and the other the working sail of No. 2.

What it gives the game: a working suit (courses and topsails No. 2, mizzen topsail No. 3, topgallants Nos. 4 and 6, royals Nos. 8 and 9, jib No. 2, flying jib No. 5, spanker No. 2), a heavy-weather set (a foresail and the fore and main topsails of No. 1), and storm canvas as separate sails of No. 1 (fore storm staysail, mizzen storm staysail, storm mizzen, trysails). The mizzen's sails are a number lighter than the fore's and main's at each level, and the second sail of each kind is the spare in the sail room.

## 2. Luce 1884, Appendix E, pp. 610–611: strength of flax canvas

Flax canvas, 20 inches wide, 80 running yards to the bolt. Strength is the weight borne by a test strip one inch wide and twenty inches long (Nos. 8 and 9: strips one and a quarter inches wide, as the text says), cut crosswise and lengthwise of the cloth.

| No. of canvas | Weight per bolt (lb avoirdupois) | Borne crosswise (lb) | Borne lengthwise (lb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 84 | 470 | 316 |
| 2 | 76 | 420 | 280 |
| 3 | 70 | 370 | 250 |
| 4 | 64 | 340 | 230 |
| 5 | 58 | 320 | 216 |
| 6 | 52 | 300 | 200 |
| 7 | 46 | 280 | 193 |
| 8 | 40 | 300 | 200 |
| 9 | 34 | 280 | 193 |

As printed, Nos. 8 and 9 bear the same as Nos. 6 and 7 despite being lighter; the text explains it, since their test strips are a quarter wider. Per inch of width they are therefore weaker: No. 8 about 240 lb crosswise and 160 lengthwise, No. 9 about 224 and 154.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | 82.3 | 1.12 |
| 2 | 73.5 | 1.00 |
| 3 | 64.8 | 0.88 |
| 4 | 59.5 | 0.81 |
| 5 | 56.0 | 0.76 |
| 6 | 52.5 | 0.71 |
| 7 | 49.0 | 0.67 |
| 8 | 42.0 | 0.57 |

Weight per square metre, for the sail's own weight: a bolt is 20 in × 80 yd = 37.2 m², so No. 1 is 1.02 kg/m² and No. 8 is 0.49 kg/m².

## 3. Admiralty *Regulations and Instructions*, 1806 (1808 printing), Section IX, Chapter I, Article I, p. 287: the daily allowance of provisions

To every person serving in His Majesty's ships, per day. Beer in wine measure; pease and oatmeal in Winchester measure; ".." is nothing that day.

| Day | Biscuit, lb | Beer, gal | Beef, lb | Pork, lb | Pease, pints | Oatmeal, pints | Sugar, oz | Butter, oz | Cheese, oz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sunday | 1 | 1 | .. | 1 | ½ | .. | .. | .. | .. |
| Monday | 1 | 1 | .. | .. | .. | ½ | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| Tuesday | 1 | 1 | 2 | .. | .. | .. | .. | .. | .. |
| Wednesday | 1 | 1 | .. | .. | ½ | ½ | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| Thursday | 1 | 1 | .. | 1 | ½ | .. | .. | .. | .. |
| Friday | 1 | 1 | .. | .. | ½ | ½ | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| Saturday | 1 | 1 | 2 | .. | .. | .. | .. | .. | .. |
| **Weekly, each man** | **7** | **7** | **4** | **2** | **2** | **1½** | **6** | **6** | **12** |

The columns sum to the printed weekly line in every case. The article continues (OCR text, lines 16700 onward): together with vinegar not exceeding half a pint to each man per week; a pint of wine, or half a pint of rum, brandy or other spirits, holds proportion to a gallon of beer; substitutions in fixed proportions (a pound of rice for a pound of bread, of pease, of oatmeal or of cheese, and so on); and the commander may shorten the allowance, the "savings" being paid for afterwards at fixed rates.

What it gives the game: a man's day is a pound of biscuit and a gallon of beer, with meat on four days (beef twice, pork twice) and banyan days between. A frigate's 264 men therefore eat 264 lb of biscuit and drink 264 gallons of beer a day, about 1.2 tons, which is why the beer ran out first and spirits and water took its place. The weekly ration per man is the unit the purser's accounts and the M4 consumption bookkeeping should use.
