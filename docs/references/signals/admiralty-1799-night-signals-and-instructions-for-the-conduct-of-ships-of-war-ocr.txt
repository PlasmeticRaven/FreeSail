DIRECTIONS 


RELATING TO 


NIGHT SIGNALS.

I. 

ALL signals made by the Admiral are to be repeated 
by the commanders of squadrons only, who are to repeat them 
in Succession, according to their seniority, allowing an 
interval of four minutes of time between the repetitions. 
But if the commander of any squadron ſhould fail to 
repeat a signal in his turn, the next senior commander 
of a squadron is to repeat it, after the expiration of six 
minutes from the firing of the last signal gun. 


The signal guns will be fircd at intervals of five seconds 
of time, or what is generally understood to be saluting 
time. But, in order to guard against any failure, by a 
gun's hanging or missing fire, and the mistake which such 
an accident might occasion, all guns fired within one 
minute of each other are to be considered as part of the 
Signal, 


II. 
All signals made by the Admiral, except such as have 
a particular answer assigned to them, are to be answered 
by one light, where it can best be seen from his ship. 
If it is a general signal, all ships are to shew a light as 
soon as it has been fully repeated. If a particular signal, 


then the ship to which it is directed.

III. 

The Admiral will answer all signals made to him by 
Shewing one light; and when the number of sails, or depth 
of water, is shewn, the Admiral will hoist his light, and 
haul it down, every time the light is hoisted and hauled 
down by the ship which makes the signal. When a ship, 
making signals for sails or soundings, is a-head of the 
Admiral, he will answer by shewing an additional light 
at the bowsprit end. 


. 


The quarters of the compass are to be signified in 
the following manner: 


For the N. E. two lights at equal heights. - For the N. W. 
two lights, one over the other. —For the S. E. three 
lights.—For the S. W. four lights. 


V. 

After the signal has been made for sceing sails, 
land, or danger, and the Admiral has answered it, then 
the quarter where seen is to be signiſied in the manner 
above directed. 

VI. 

When the Admiral has made the signal for ships to 
chace, and it has been answered, he will then haul down 
the signal, and, it necessary, denote, as above directed, 
the quarter in which he would have them chace, 


VII. 

When a ship makes signals for seeing sails, if the 
Admiral would have that ship chace, after having an— 
swered the signals, instead of making the signal No. 51, 
he will burn false fires, which that ship is to answer.

VIII. 

When a signal is made to the Admiral, which he does 
not perceive, it may be repeated, if thought necessary, 
by some ship between the Admiral aud the ship which 
made it. 


IX. 

When in the night a ship is taken aback, or by being 
brought into the wind is in danger of going about, the 
is to shew two lights at her ensign-staff, and one at her 
bowsprit-end, and fire muskets. The same signal is to 
be made by a ship if she drives when at anchor. 

X. 


When the signal is made to tack, or wear, each ship 


is to shew two lights at her ensign-staff, and one at her 


bowsprit-end, and continue them abroad until the fleet 
is come to the wind on the other tack. 

And when the signal is made to bring-to, or to make 
sail after lying- by, each ship, as soon as she has complied 
with the signal, is to signify the same by shewing two 
lights at her ensign-staff, and one at the bowsprit-end. 

XI. 

In coming to an anchor, ships are to have two lights 
at their ensigu-staff, and one at the bowsprit-end, which 
they are to continue out till all the ships have anchored. 

XII. 
In line of battle, ships are always to carry one light 


in their stern-lantern, and one at their bowsprit-end.

XIII. 

To prevent accidents in thick and stormy weather, 
ships are to signify which tack they are upon, by shewing 
one light at each cat-head ; and when crossing near 
another ship, upon a different tack, they are to have a 
third light ready, which, if they mean to bear up, they 
are to shew over the light at the weather cat-head ; but 
over that at the lee cat-head, if meaning to keep the 
wind of the other ship; | 


That is, If to keep the wind, two lights at the 1 20 
If to bear up, two lights at the weather J head. 
N. B. When ships are upon different tacks, and un- 
avoidably cross near cach other; the ship on the srAR- 
BOARD TACK is always to keep the wind, and that on 
the LARBOARD rack is always to pass to leeward, except 
in cases where extreme danger might arise, from a strict 
compliance with this mstruction, 
XIV. 

When the signal is made for tacking together, 
hauling to the wind, or altering the course, the Admiral 
will, soon after the signal has been fully repeated, mark 
the time for the execution, by burning false fires or rockets, 
or by firing one gun, when the ships are immediately to 
execute the purpose of the signal together. 

XV. 

Frigates of the commander in chief's squadron, that 
are not upon the look- out, are to keep near him to wind- 
ward; and when the Admiral would speak with one of 
them, he will shew two lights at the ensign-staff, upon 
which the nearest frigate, or she who first perceives the 
Signal, is to bear down within hail to receive orders.

I F an enemy's fleet, or any strange fleet be in sight

of them, and to place themselves in such a manner as to 
be able to give the Admiral all necessary information by 
signals, and to discover all signals made by him; the 
ships nearest to the Admiral repeating very carefully the 
signals made by him, or those made to him by the other 
ships.
