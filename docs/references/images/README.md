# Reference images

Pictures kept as visual references for vessels the library will build (proposal §12 item 11, revised 2026-09-26). Each is a public-domain work; the reasoning is stated so that it can be checked. The owner found them by image search; the build session traced their provenance on 2026-09-26.

| File | What | Provenance and status |
|---|---|---|
| `tartane-roux-school-watercolour.webp` | A Mediterranean tartane under sail: a lateen mainsail on one raking mast, two headsails on a long bowsprit, a small lateen mizzen right aft, a boat in tow; signed "Roux" at lower right. The reference for the third vessel's sail plan and vibe. | Signed by a member of the Roux family of Marseille marine painters (Joseph 1725–1789, Ange-Joseph Antoine 1765–1835, and Antoine's sons, the last of whom died 1882). The exact work has not been identified; whichever Roux painted it died more than a century ago, so the work is in the public domain everywhere. |
| `bilander-line-drawing-psf.webp` | A bilander under sail, pen line drawing: the fore square-rigged, the main with a trapezoidal lateen mainsail on a long yard slung at about 45°, square topsails above it. | Pearson Scott Foresman archive drawing, donated to the Wikimedia Foundation and released into the public domain worldwide (Wikimedia Commons, `File:Bilander (PSF).png`, from PSF B-90006; VRT ticket 2010061110041093). |
| `bilander-gwyn-c1780-nmm-pag3828.webp` | "A Bilander": pen, ink and watercolour of a British bilander under all sail with a separate view of her stern, red ensign at the peak. | Edward Gwyn, *British bilander showing sails and rigging and separate view of the stern*, c. 1780, page 21 of the Edward Gwyn sketchbook, National Maritime Museum, Greenwich, object PAG3828. The drawing is in the public domain by age; the museum offers licensing of its photographs, and a faithful photograph of a two-dimensional public-domain work carries no new copyright in the United States, where this repository is hosted. Credit: National Maritime Museum, Greenwich, London. |

Falconer 1780 has an entry BILANDER, and Steel 1794 gives the cutting of lateen and settee sails; see `../README.md` for the texts.
