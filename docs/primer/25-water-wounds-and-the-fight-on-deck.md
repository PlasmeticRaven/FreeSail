# 25. Water, wounds and the fight on deck

Chapter 22 fought the guns. This chapter is what the guns do to a ship and her people, and
the two fights that are not the guns': the fight against the water, and the fight on deck.
Everything in it is a state of the ship or the muster that the readings give and the log
says, never a figure set.

## The well

Every ship makes a little water. The carpenter sounds the well each watch and the watch pumps
her dry at the change of the watch without a line in your log; that is the seams working in
a seaway, two inches a watch, and you will not hear of it. You hear of the water when it
gains: a shot hole below the waterline, the ground she struck, a seam started under a
labouring ship (the director's `aboard: a seam found working`), or the sea she ships over
the hatches in a heavy sea.

    the well           the carpenter's feet: "a foot of water in the well, and gaining;
                       the pumps gaining on it"
    the water in the ship  the tons, and every source by name with its rate (also `the
                       water aboard`, `the water in her`)
    the leaks          the shot holes with their places, the seams, the ground
    the pumps          manned or not, by whom, the rate, when they were spelled

A shot hole takes in water by the hole's size and its depth under the water, so an
18-pounder's hole half a metre under a frigate's waterline is some eighty-five tons an hour,
and the heel that buries it quickens it. The frigate's two chain pumps lift eighty tons an
hour each with four men at the brakes and her two hand pumps fifteen each with two; a
cutter has the hand pumps alone. `man the pumps` takes the hands from the pool as a standing
work (in action, from the trimmers by the fighting book's priority), the keeper spells them
every half hour (`spell the pumps` sooner), and they suck when the well is dry, kept by
spells while a leak runs; `secure the pumps` stands their crews down (refused when nobody
is at them). Where the pumps lose, the log says so ("the pumps losing: the well
gains ten tons an hour on them"), and the water takes her stiffness before it takes her: a
foot of free water across the hold is a third of a cutter's metacentric height, and she
rolls deeper and heels further for it. At three fifths of the water that founders her the
log says she is settling; at half her displacement she founders, held where she lies, her
sails furled, the game's ship lost. `batten down the hatches` (ten minutes) stops the sea
she ships in a gale from going below, and `open the hatches` (ten minutes) takes the battens
off again; each is refused when the hatches are so already.

## The carpenter's and the boatswain's work

Every repair is an evolution: hands and time from the files in `data/evolutions/`, competing
with the guns and the sails as every evolution does, so that the fight's decisions live where
they lived.

                            minutes above the water, twenty within half a metre under

`the damage` names everything: the lines parted, running and standing, with the order that
mends each, the sprung spars, the jury masts, the holed sails, the shot holes by place and
whether plugged, and the well.

## The surgeon

A man hit is killed, or wounded lightly or dangerously, and the wound has words: a splinter
in the thigh, a musket ball in the shoulder, an arm taken off at the elbow. He goes below to
the cockpit, where the surgeon takes the dangerous wounds himself (half an hour each) and his
mates the light (ten minutes); in a cutter with no surgeon the messmates dress the light in
an hour and the badly wounded wait their day. The lightly wounded come back to the bill
"hurt and at light duty" for a week or two; the dangerously wounded are judged at a day,
when three in ten die under the surgeon's hands (six in ten under their messmates') and the
rest are invalids for the voyage. The sick list has the men the water and the allowance put
there: with the water foul two in a hundred a day take the flux, past a week of short
allowance one in a hundred is worn down, and with the water out the company sickens from the
second day and dies from the fourth, each death in the log with its cause.

    the sick list      by name, each man's hurt; the men at light duty; the dead to bury
    the wounded        each wound, light or dangerous, and how many wait
    the surgeon        borne or not, in the cockpit or the sick berth, at work or idle
    the bill           "four killed, five wounded (five dangerously), eleven hurt and at
                       light duty of 264; the hands firm"

The butcher's bill is said ten minutes after the last shot. The dead lie until the morning
and are committed to the deep at eight, the ship's company at the gangway, and struck from
the books (`bury the dead` sooner); the surgeon's morning report follows. A named person is
a man of the muster like the rest: the first lieutenant hit is told so, and the muster says
"wounded (a splinter in the hand), on deck" of him.

## When it is you

If you hold a station, you are hit as the men at your part are, and the quarterdeck is the
most exposed place in the ship. The line is yours alone and urgent: "You are hit: a splinter
in the hand. You can keep the deck (your orders go and your words carry, and you tire the
faster), or go below to the surgeon to have it dressed ('go below to the surgeon') and come
on deck again." Keep the deck with a light wound and a mate dresses it where you stand after
an hour. Keep it with a dangerous one and you collapse within the half hour and are carried
below, where nothing of the deck reaches you until the surgeon has dressed you; and a
dangerous wound keeps you under his hands until his judgement at a day, which ends the
station one way or the other. Killed, the station ends as a death does: told in words,
written in the journal, stood down with the reason; the command passes by the bill and the
voyage goes on under whoever commands. The rules are the same for a model and for you.

Thirst and hunger meet their ends the same way: three days without water puts you down,
four kills you, and the lines before it told you so.

## The marines and the small arms

A frigate carries forty marines under a lieutenant and a sergeant, a brig-sloop twenty under
a sergeant; the cutters, the schooner, the lugger and the trader bear none. Their kit is the
nation's (`data/marines.yaml`): the British Sea Service musket and the sergeant's halberd,
three rounds a minute and the detachment kept on the poop and the gangways; the French the
Charleville, soldiers embarked among them, a slower volley and two in five of them in the
tops as sharpshooters. At the beat they serve the quarterdeck carronades; your order takes
them to their muskets, and the carronades go short by them.

    marines, fire by volley     within musket shot (a hundred yards) of a near deck: a
    marines, fire at will       volley every twenty seconds, or each man at his own rate
    marines, hold your fire     the muskets grounded; back to the carronades
    marines to the tops         the kit's share aloft as marksmen; `marines to the gangways`
                                brings them down again
    serve out the small arms    five minutes with the gunner's party: cutlasses, pikes,
                                pistols and tomahawks to the boarders, the muskets of the
                                stand of arms to the small-arms men
    small arms, fire at will    the small-arms men's muskets within musket shot;
    small arms, hold your fire  grounded again

The hits fall on the men exposed on the other deck, the quarterdeck's officers and helmsmen
two in five (history's lesson), and her people fire back by their own book. Her log says
"musketry from her tops: two wounded"; yours says what your marines did.

## The fight on deck

    lay her aboard          the helm closes her to pistol shot and under
    grapple her             within thirty metres: the grapnels fast, the two ships locked
                            together and drifting as one
    boarders away           the bill's boarders (a frigate's sixty) with the marines over
                            the side
    repel boarders          the gun crews up with the pikes and cutlasses, the guns silent,
                            every fit man to hold the deck
    cast off the grapnels   free of her (your boarders recalled; refused with hers on your
                            deck)

The crossing costs the boarders her marines' volley and whatever guns bear on them; then it
is a round a minute, each side's strength its men by their arms (served out or not, the
bayonet) and their temper and their cover, each side losing by the other's strength. The deck
is carried when the defenders' strength falls under half the boarders' or their temper
breaks: her colours come down by your boarders' hand, and the prize is not yet in the game.
The boarders are repulsed the other way, or after a quarter of an hour contested; they cast
off, and the hands' temper pays for it. A hundred boarders against a frigate's whole company
awake on deck are driven back in a round; against her at quarters, her gun crews at the guns,
with the arms served out, they carry her in six. The rules captains fight by their doctrine
(`board_odds`, `board_when`: a privateer boards at once with even odds, a King's ship a ship
whose fire has slackened with the odds half again in his favour), and a captain whose deck is
boarded repels of himself.
