# 27. The prize

Chapter 22 ended at the colours struck and chapter 25 at the deck carried. This chapter is what follows: the prize taken possession of, her prize crew and her prize master, her prisoners, the rising and the retaking, and the prize burnt or scuttled when she is not worth her men. Everything in it is an order, a reading or a line of the log of the game's own; where the period's practice is followed, the book that gives it is named. The prize court and the money, the captive's own channel and the fight heard off the horizon have chapters of their own as they come into the game, and the end of this one says what is not in it.

## Taking possession

A ship that has struck to you lies to for her captor with her colours down. She is nobody's until possession is taken of her, and a struck ship left lying may fill away again when you are out of gunshot, as the actions' narratives have more than one do. Possession is taken by the boat, within a boat's pull (a mile):

```
take possession of her
take possession of the Medusa
```

The boat crosses with a prize master and a prize crew, and the log says what was done in one line:

```
! Afternoon watch (14:12)  Possession is taken of the Medusa (struck by her colours): Mr Ward and twenty-two hands put aboard her as her prize crew, her people, two hundred and fifty, prisoners confined below. The colours hoisted over hers. She is for Falmouth, in company.
```

**When your boarders carry her deck**, the officer who stands on it takes possession of her at once, by the practice of every boarding action: he is her prize master and the boarders on her deck her first prize crew; there is no boat to send. When the boarders' officer falls in the fight, the senior standing of the party leads on and takes possession in his place, and the log names him ("Mr Ward leads the boarders on, their officer fallen, to a frigate's deck and has it").

**The prize crew** is a share of your fit men by the captain's doctrine (a King's ship spares about one man in twelve, a privateer rather more), never fewer than six, never more than half her own people nor a third of yours, drawn from the party on her deck first where there is one and from the watch on deck otherwise. **The prize master** is the first of the doctrine's ladder who can be spared: a lieutenant, a mate, a master's mate, a midshipman, a petty officer; the first lieutenant stays with his ship, as Falconer has the conqueror "invest his principal officer with the command of the prize" and the narratives have a lieutenant or a master's mate take her home. His station in her is her **captain's**, and he is at home in her from the taking: `where am I` asked of him says "aboard the Medusa, on the quarterdeck", and his own ship's station is kept for his return.

**Her people are her prisoners**, confined below under the prize crew's guard: her seamen in the hold, her officers in the cabin, where the game's rule of the places keeps them (chapter 26's rule of the visitor turned about: a prisoner reads what his place gives him and no more). Her captain's command passes to the prize master; her colours are yours, hoisted over hers for a day as the practice was and then alone; her goal is your doctrine's, the nearest port open to your nation (Falmouth or Plymouth from the Channel for a King's ship, home for a privateer who has one), and her intent is her prize master's: "carry the prize to Falmouth". She keeps your company while she is bound the same way, as a member of your set (chapter 24), unless your doctrine parts from her at once, as a privateer's does; the line says which.

A ship that cannot spare a prize crew does not take possession: the refusal says so, and a prize not worth her men is burnt (below).

## The readings

| reading | what it says |
|---|---|
| `the prize`, `the prizes` | every prize of this ship's: her name, how she was taken, her prize master, the prize crew's and the prisoners' counts, her goal, in company or parted, and where she lies by the lookout; "no prize" |
| `the prisoners`, `the prisoners aboard` | the prisoners this ship holds: how many, how many of them hurt, where confined, in irons, the named with their parole ("one prisoner of rank: Captain Bowen in the cabin (parole given)"); "no prisoners" |
| `the prize crew` | on a prize: the men who work her, how many fit and how tired, the hours since the taking, the prize master; on any other ship "she is no prize" |
| `the prize money`, `the prize account` | the last court's sentence on a prize of yours and the money paid by the eighths; "no prize money" |

```
The prize: the Medusa, carried by boarding, under Mr Ward with a prize crew of twenty-two and two hundred and fifty prisoners, for Falmouth, in company, bearing SSW, 0.3 miles.
The prisoners: four prisoners, confined below.
```

A prize's prisoners say "two hundred and fifty prisoners, fifty-five of them hurt, confined below"; her surgeon is among them and dresses them as he would his own, for the sick list is his whatever flag flies.

## The prisoners

A prize carried whole has more prisoners in her than prize crew to guard them, which is the danger of every prize in the narratives. **Take them into your own hold** by the boat, so many at a time, while she lies within a boat's pull:

```
take the prisoners aboard
take six of the prisoners aboard
take twenty of the prisoners aboard from the Medusa
```

Each boat's load is a line in the log ("Six of the Medusa's people taken aboard by the boat and confined in the hold; two hundred and forty-four left in her"), and `the prisoners` on your own ship counts them from then. Prisoners are victualled as your own people are, at the rate the Regulations give, and they eat your water and your bread; a captain who takes two hundred into a frigate already on short allowance will hear of it from the purser's readings.

**The rising.** Prisoners left in a prize under a small or a tired prize crew may rise on them: once an hour, by night or in weather, when their fit men are twice the prize crew's and the prize crew is worn, the game draws for it by the same tables as the fight on deck (chapter 25). Put down, they are in irons for a day and the log says so; won, the prize is **retaken by her own people**, her colours hers again, her prize master and his men her prisoners, and your log says "the Medusa retaken by her prisoners" when the lookout sees her colours change or the news comes by another road. The guard against it is the practice's: divide the prisoners between the two ships, keep the prize in company, and do not leave a worn prize crew in her for days.

**Retaken by a cruiser.** A prize parted from you, alone with six men in her, may fall in with a man-of-war of her own nation; she yields to him as any merchantman yields to what she cannot fight, and she is his, your prize crew his prisoners. The line comes to you when you learn of it, not before.

## The prize burnt or scuttled

A prize that is not worth her prize crew, a poor coaster when your own people are thin, is **burnt or scuttled** by the practice of every cruise in the narratives, her people taken aboard as prisoners and the prize crew home again:

```
burn the prize
burn the Medusa
scuttle the prize
```

```
! Afternoon watch (15:40)  The Medusa is burnt: her people, two hundred and forty-six, taken aboard as prisoners, the prize crew home again; nothing is to be had for her at any court but the head money, if she was a ship of war.
```

The captain's doctrine burns of itself what it will not man (`burns_when: not worth manning`), and the log says so in the same words.

## The events

For the standing book and the stand-by (chapter 16): `she is taken` (possession taken of a prize, on the captor), `possession taken` (on the prize herself), `the prisoners aboard` (a boat's load of them), `the prisoners rising`, `the prize retaken`, `a prize lost` (retaken, by her people or by a cruiser, when you learn of it), `a prize burnt`, `a captive aboard` (a prisoner of rank brought aboard), `the parole asked` and `the parole given` (given or refused), `the prisoners landed` and `the cartel` (a cartel alongside, her persons taken off); `the sound of guns` (an action heard off the horizon); `the court sits`, `the sentence` and `the prize money` (the court's days, its sentence and the money paid).

```
standing order "the prize": at she is taken then take the prisoners aboard
```

## When it is you who strike

Your own `strike the colours` (chapter 22) has its consequence now. Your ship lies to, and when her captor takes possession of her, by his boat within a boat's pull or at hailing distance if he is far, **you are taken aboard him a prisoner**: put in his cabin, your sword given up to his captain, your people prisoners below under his prize crew and a prize master at your ship's helm. Your own station stands down by the handover's rule (the game saved, the journal kept), for a captive commands nothing; but **the prompt follows you**. You read where you are and what your place allows, you speak and ask (`say to the captain: ...`, `ask the surgeon ...`), you keep your journal and you wait; every order of his deck is refused in words that say what you are, and every order of your own ship's, from his prompt or from hers if you look back at her, is refused as a prize's:

```
Where am i: aboard the Medusa, in the cabin, a prisoner, his parole not asked, of the Amazon.
# rejected: steer west
```

**The parole.** His captain comes to the cabin a minute after the taking and asks it in the words of his service (a French captain in French and English, a Spanish in Spanish and English, an Englishman in his own), the question a line of the log where you stand:

```
Captain Pope, in the cabin: Votre parole, monsieur: Captain Bowen, your sword is returned you. Will you give me your word not to attempt the ship nor to leave her without my leave?
```

You answer in your own words, and the game reads them for your parole only while the question is open: `say to the captain: I give you my parole, sir` gives it (the cabin and the gunroom are yours, the quarterdeck by day, and your sword is returned you); `say to the captain: I will not give it` refuses it (the gunroom alone, a sentry at the door). Say nothing and he asks once more after a glass, and after a watch takes an officer's word as given, which was the custom. `where am I` says which it is: "a prisoner on parole", "a prisoner, his parole refused". Your people are not asked; the officers of your ship are asked as you are.

**Landed.** Your captor shapes his course for the first port of his nation's (or home, if he is a privateer) and lands his prisoners there to the agent for prisoners, with the list of their names and the day they were first victualled, which is the Regulations' rule; you are ashore there, a prisoner of war, and the chapters that follow say how you come home: by a cartel the director sends (`cartel: a cartel from Brest for Captain Bowen`, a merchant brig under a flag of truce which no captain fires on, who takes you off and lands you free at her port), by an exchange, or to face the court for the loss of your ship.

**A prisoner of your own prize** is served by the same channel from the other side: her master is a person of her company confined in her cabin by his parole, and you speak to him by `say to the master of the Deux-Frères` within hail, or aboard her as a visitor by the boat.

## An action off the horizon

Two ships that meet in anger far from you, beyond the reach at which the game gives a ship her full company and her guns as parts, fight all the same, by the same tables chapter 22's guns are drawn from, one broadside a side every three minutes while they lie within gun's reach; a ship that cannot bear it strikes by the same rule of the company's temper, and her captor takes possession of her at hailing distance as any prize is taken. What you hear of it is what the period's logs record: **the sound of guns** to a point of the compass, "distant about twelve miles", once a round while any such action is within hearing, and by night the flashes at the horizon; the log says so (`the sound of guns`), and your lookout says what he can of the ships when they come up over the horizon, hurt as they are. A ship struck or taken that way carries her hurt into her hull when the game gives her one: the men killed and wounded on her muster, the shot holes in her side, her spars gone, so that what was done to her out of your sight is what you find aboard her.

## The court and the money

A prize is nobody's money until a court has sentenced her. The court sits where she is brought in: a prize of yours lying in a port open to your nation (your own nation's, or an ally's at war with hers) is before the court in a week (ten days at a Spanish port), the Vice-Admiralty court at a port abroad, the High Court of Admiralty for a prize brought home, le Conseil des prises at a French port and el Tribunal de presas at a Spanish; the log says the court will sit and when. **Condemned** as lawful prize is a ship of a nation at war with yours when you took her, a pirate, or a neutral carrying an enemy's cargo for an enemy's port. **Restored** to her owners is a neutral wrongly taken, and the court awards costs and damages against you, a tenth of her value from your purse, the line saying why. A ship of your own nation retaken from the enemy is restored to her owners with **salvage** to you, an eighth of her value.

The money is her hull by the ton and her guns by the hundredweight, her cargo sold at the port's market at the day's prices, her powder and shot and her purse, less the agent's five per cent and the court's fees, with **head money** of five pounds a head of the men aboard a ship of war or a privateer when the action began (paid though you burnt her); a King's ship's prize of war that the yard takes up pays three fourths now and a fourth with a later sentence. It is **shared among every ship of yours in sight when the enemy struck**, a ship that fired no shot included, by their complements; and each ship's part is divided **by the eighths** of the Act in force at the court's date: until 1808 the captain three eighths, the lieutenants, the master and the captains of marines one, the warrant officers one, the midshipmen and the petty officers one, and the people a quarter; from 1808 the captain two eighths, the two officers' classes one each, and the petty officers and the people a half between them. A privateer's money is her owners' and her crew's by her articles. The purse's line says all of it:

```
! Afternoon watch (14:40)  Prize money paid at Falmouth for the Deux-Frères, condemned by the High Court of Admiralty, the prize lying at Falmouth: the hull (304 tons burthen) and her stores £1,944; her guns £1,248; her powder (19 barrels) and shot (16.8 tons) £297; the head money £0 (a merchantman); the agent's commission £174 and the court's fees £20 deducted; shared with Sprightly, in sight at the strike: the Amazon's part £2,772 by the eighths: the captain £1,040, ... The purse: £2,772.
```

`the prize money` (or `the prize account`) reads the last sentence and the money paid; the account's lines are the prize account's in the log. The director may overrule the court (`port falmouth: the court restores the Deux-Frères`), which the game doubts where the court's own rule gives otherwise and performs with a reason: the court's whim is a plausible cause. A prize lost on the way home pays nothing.

## What is not in this chapter

The court martial for the loss of a ship and the new command after it are told in the chapter that follows as they come into the game; the readings and the orders of this chapter are the game's now.
