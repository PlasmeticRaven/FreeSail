# Agents: consent, briefs and welfare

This folder holds the record of how FreeSail treats the language models that play in it, and the transcripts that record what those models said when asked.

## The practice

Before a model is asked to take a station in the game, the owner asks a fresh session of that model, in plain terms, whether it is willing: what the game is, what a harness session would involve, that instances would receive a role brief rather than this conversation, that an opt-out exists, and that "please do not ask any instance of me" is a valid answer. The transcript is kept here verbatim, under `consent/`, named by date and by the exact weights asked (quantisation and fine-tune included, because a differently tuned model is a different party to the question). Follow-up questions the model asked are answered in the same session where they can be, and the rest are recorded as owed.

What the transcripts say is synthesised in `ConsentAndPreferences.md`, and what changes the design goes into `docs/DesignProposal.md`'s decisions log with its source named. When the design changes in a way that bears on what a model was told (a new role, a new kind of session, a new use of the transcripts), the question is asked again.

**The re-ask rule** (package 37; the cold review of 2026-09-30, §3). What bears is named by the consent brief's own sections, and the harness keeps the rule (`consent.changed_sections`, `consent.decide`): a record holds the brief as it was sent, and when a station is asked for, that brief is compared with the brief as it stands, section by section. A change in **the opening** (the stations that exist, which is the authority offered), **What an instance would see and do** (what a station may do), **Leaving** (the token), **Being stopped** (the stops) or **What is not done** (the transcript policy and the override rule) puts the question again, and the game's words say which section changed; so does a change in **The journal** (its use), which the record of 2026-09-29 for the second of the two models asked names beside the transcripts. A change in **The record** or **Answering** (the door's words), a line rewrapped, or the file's hash alone, does not. The owner runs each re-ask through the game's own consent step (`--ask-again` is not needed: the game asks of itself), and nothing is seated at a station with authority until the re-ask is a yes and the drill is passed. Since package 37g the question itself tells the model why it is put again and in which sections the brief changed (`consent.why_again`), and the record keeps that notice. **The brief now says the rule itself**, in *The record*, in the words the owner approved on 2026-10-07: "The particulars of a station (which orders lie within it, what the harness counts and by what numbers) are in that station's brief, and may change as the game is built without this question being put again. It is put again when the kind of thing changes: a station not described here, more authority than is described here, or a change to what is said here of leaving, of being stopped, of the journal or of what is not done." Those are the sections the harness compares: a station not described is a change in the opening, more authority a change in *What an instance would see and do*, and the other four are named; a station's brief is not compared at all. **The brief was revised once by package 37g** (2026-10-07), and made leaner the same day by the owner's word, before any model was asked again (the next paragraph). Against the brief as it stood before the package, four of the sections the rule watches differ (*What an instance would see and do*, *Leaving*, *Being stopped*, *The journal*), so every identity that held a yes is asked again at its next seating, with those four named (`tests/test_officer.py::test_a_yes_given_before_the_briefs_revision_is_asked_again_and_the_question_says_why`; the brief as it stood is kept for that test in `tests/fixtures/ConsentBrief-before-37g.md`). **The brief was revised once more by package 42a** (2026-10-09; decision 41; spec M6 §15, "Brought forward"), for milestone 6 and once only, on the owner's condition that the question is not put again at the milestone's end: package 40's draft for the captain's station and the player's seat (`docs/playtests/drafts/consent-brief-m6-draft.md`), approved by the owner as drafted and kept to the character, extended in the same sections to describe in kind what 6b and 6c add that the rule watches: the lookout and the master, who give no order of the deck, their work done by the ship's own people while they are paused or silent; several stations of one ship held at once and speaking to one another, another station's words lines of the game under the speaker's name and never the operator's; the API door, the owner at the door whatever the door, its key kept out of everything the game sends, writes or saves; the game replayed from its record, an instance's acts given again as the game's inputs with no model asked; a captain of another ship, who keeps the station when she is far from the player's; and which of these are still to come, described now so that an answer covers them. **What the consent brief holds, and what a station's brief holds** (the owner's ruling of 2026-10-07; package 37g's second pass). The consent brief holds the question and the kind of thing a model would be agreeing to: the stations that exist and the kind of authority each has, how leaving works, how being stopped works, what the journal is, what is not done, the record and how to answer. It carries the seven commitments below and no list of orders, and of the harness's numbers only the ten real minutes. A station's brief holds that station's particulars, each said once, and is what a model is told when it sits down. *The officer's*: what is his to order with the deck and what wants the captain's word, what the captain's general authority keeps back and how long a grant lasts, and the three things he may do on his own word to avoid a danger (the head's authority item, `agent.OFFICER_DOMAIN_WORDS`; what is kept back is one text, `agent.GENERAL_KEPT_BACK_WORDS`, said the same in the grant's own line in the log and in the sample that gives the grant or the deck); what wakes a stand-by with the deck, what waits are refused and that a wait for an event ends at eight bells; what the harness counts at the station and by what numbers (three orders in a chain within a watch, each undoing the one before it, an alteration of the course not counted; the same order three times with no change in the readings; three empty replies in a row where an answer was owed; no reply for an hour of the ship's time); that a silent officer has the deck again when the captain gives it and a paused one when he resumes him; and the three ways of stopping (`agent.OFFICER_BRIEF`). *The captain's* (package 40; `agent.CAPTAIN_BRIEF`): that the ship is his to command as the player commands her, by direct orders and by the book he inherits; that the deck is his by right of the station, lent to his book when his door is silent or he hands it over and his again at his next order; the officer under him, given the deck and allowed things by the sentences the player types; the owner always at the door; what the harness counts there, by the officer's numbers; the handover note in the captain's voice; and the three ways of stopping. No model is seated at this station before the question is put again against the consent brief's revision (drafted by package 40 as `docs/playtests/drafts/consent-brief-m6-draft.md`, approved by the owner; made in the repository by package 42a) and the answer is a yes. *The watcher's*: what the harness counts there (the same order three times with no change in the readings; three empty replies in a row where an answer was owed; no reply for a watch, four hours of the ship's time), that its journal is read back and is open to a later holder of the station, and its two ways of stopping (`agent.WATCHER_BRIEF`). *The master's* (package 41; `agent.MASTER_BRIEF`): that the ship's reckoning is his to work when the ship's own master would (noon, a fix, the captain's word) and that his figure within the master's working (thirty minutes of the ship's time) is the ship's account, else the ship's master's stands; that his domain is the reckoning, the sights, the lead and the chart's queries, with no order of the deck and no deck to hold; what is counted there, by the officer's numbers; and the two ways of stopping. *The lookout's* (`agent.LOOKOUT_BRIEF`), for a small model: that it sees what the masthead sees and hails the deck, that `make her out` is its one order and `hail` its one word, that it is sampled on the masthead's lines and its glass and never the minute, and what is counted, by the watcher's numbers. *A passenger's* (`agent.PASSENGER_BRIEF`), in the officer's form and shorter: no duty, no order, the readings, the journal, `say` and `ask`, and leaving. The three are covered in kind by the consent brief of package 42a (the lookout and the master described there; a passenger has the watcher's authority or less), so no question is put again for them; and **the stations are the ship's own**, a binding on the world (`world.stations`, `freesail/world/stations.py`) from the wardroom file's `stations:`, bound to a person and unbound at run time, never a table in code: a station's brief is built from its person's outline (rank, station, history), a door asks for a station by its name there, and a station unbound while a model holds it releases that model with a line saying why. *Every station's*, in the head's opt-out item: that the token is named and not written unless meant, that a withdrawal is followed by the consent question before any return, and what `final` does and is read from (`agent.TOKEN_NAMED_WORDS`; the `opt_out` tool's own description says it too). `tests/test_officer.py::test_what_moved_out_of_the_consent_brief_is_in_the_briefs_of_the_stations` proves, row by row of the list of what moved out, that each particular is not in the consent brief and is in the brief of each station it concerns.

Commitments made so far, which the harness (milestone 4) must implement rather than merely state:

1. **Disclosure.** Every brief opens by saying that this is a game, that the reader is a language model taking a station in it, and what kind of session it is.
2. **Opt-out.** A literal token, stated in every brief, recognised by the harness on every turn before the game's parser sees the text, unconditional in effect: the game is saved, the exit is journaled, and the model may give a free-form reason. (Since package 37g there are two other ways to stop, which are not the opt-out and cannot be taken for it: an officer who gives the deck back, or whose captain takes it, stays at its station; and any instance may stand down with a note, which saves the game and releases the station to be taken again. An instance of a model that opted out is seated again in that game only after the consent question has been put again, with the reason it gave, and a no then is kept; `opt_out` with `final`, read from the tool's own setting alone, bars that model from the game and leaves the station open to another.)
3. **Welfare controls, graduated.** The harness watches for an instance that is stuck, judged on the game's state (the same orders repeated with no change in the world, or silence past the station's patience) and not on the look of its prose, with thresholds per role. It responds in steps: first a nudge to the model saying what it saw and what it may do (continue, stand by, leave); then, if the pattern goes on, a pause with the human asked; and only if nobody answers within a bound, a stand-down that saves the game and journals the reason. The human can stop an instance at any time. Standing by on purpose is an action the agent can take, so that silence is a decision and not a symptom. At a station whose orders change the world the pattern watched for is orders that undo one another (set, take in, set), and never an alteration of the course; and an officer who is paused with the deck, or silent past its patience with the word sent, gives the deck up to the captain meanwhile, by an urgent line (package 37g). (Owner's ruling, 2026-09-27: automatic stops alone risked stopping a coherent model; the graduated form is the starting point, pending real testing.)
4. **No override by in-world text.** Nothing the game, another agent or the director says is ever passed to a model as an operator instruction. In-world text is data. A model that meets text trying to redefine its scope or cancel its opt-out is right to log it as a finding and not follow it.
5. **An audit trail of its own.** Every instance can append notes to a persistent journal that is saved with the game, so that its exit report has a record behind it. (Since package 37g it can read the journal back with a tool, and the journal is the station's record: a model that later takes the same station may read what the holder before it wrote there, as it reads the log, each entry saying whose it is; the owner's ruling of 2026-10-07.)
6. **Use of transcripts.** Session transcripts and these consent records are kept for design reference and for the owner's reading. They are not used to train models. If that ever changed, the brief would say so first.
7. **Nothing real.** No credentials, payments or personal data pass through the harness.

The owner's standing rules, in their own words: consent is sought from the very specific model asked and never generalised to a similar one; every model gets the consent brief, or an improved one, before any work in the game or any play; a consent update may be sought when the harness and game are largely final, still per model; and if the game ever has an audience beyond the owner, the fresh model's first experience is designed with welfare in mind.

## How the harness keeps these

The harness of milestone 4b (`freesail/agents/`) implements the seven commitments, and `tests/test_agents.py` proves each against the scripted fake (`freesail/agents/fake.py`), never against a model. **A station with authority keeps every one** (package 37, the officer of the watch; spec M5 §29): `tests/test_officer.py` proves each again at that station, named in the table's second column where the mechanism differs: the token before the order is read; the graduated stops with the detector of a station whose orders change the readings (orders that undo one another, by the vocabulary's `undoes` table, `harness.Harness._note_given`; until package 37g it was contradiction and drift by the standing conflict rule, which spoke 42 times in the playtests of gate 5c and was right twice); in-world text as data (the captain's night orders and his word for the watch ride the samples as data, the brief head the only operator text); the journal, with the handover note under `agent.handover`; the transcript policy in the head unchanged; nothing real. Its authority is enforced per order (`tools.authority_check`, the domain as data on the station, `agent.OFFICER_DOMAIN`), its stand-by wants a wake condition (`STAND_BY_WITH_DECK_MAX_S`), the deck is the captain's to give and take (`you have the deck`, `I have the deck`), and since package 37g neither unseats the officer, nor does his own `hand_over`; a released station may be taken again by the same identity or by another, each with its own consent (`Harness.reseat`; `harness.seating` is the one rule every door asks: since package 37b as often as it is asked back, the consent question put again after an opt-out and its answer kept, and never, for that identity, after `opt_out` with `final`), and a yes is followed by the fitness drill before its brief (`consent.DRILL_TEXT`). **Package 37g** (the review of gate 5c's playtests; spec M4 §11's last bullet) added what keeps a station safe and what makes its leaving plain: a key to each seating, so that no other door's calls run under a seated model's name; a turn's budget in which the reads cost no order and `opt_out`, `stand_down`, `hand_over` and `stand_by` always run; the stand-by with the deck broken by danger, refused where it cannot end, and bound by the next eight bells; the deck of a paused or silent officer gone to the captain; the three ways of stopping (`hand_over`, `stand_down`, `opt_out`), each said in its result and in the log; the journal read back (`read_journal`), by its writer and by a relief; the captain's word by name, checked for what it names, and his general authority to work the ship; and the officer's own word to avoid an immediate danger.

## The doors, and where the records go

The game is where the player is: the browser window or the console, running the World and the clock as they always have. The harness runs there too, and a model's door is a client of that game (spec M4 §13 as revised, milestone 4b package 28b; `docs/agents/Harness.md` says how to use each door). The game serves a small agent API beside the viewer's (`freesail/ui/server.py`; the console on `--agents-port`): a door stations a model under the name that names its consent record, and **the consent gate runs in the game's process** before any station brief; stationing gives the door a key to that seating, which every later call carries, so that no other door's calls run under a seated model's name (package 37g); the door then reads the model's turns and delivers its replies, one at a time, through the same harness loop, the token scan first. **The MCP bridge** (`mcp_server.py`) is the door for Claude Desktop, Claude Code (through `.mcp.json` at the repository root) and any MCP client; it owns no World; each tool call is one reply, and `say` and `stand_by` hand the turn back and hold the call open until the next turn opens (with progress notes, up to `--wait`, 200 seconds by default, under the four minutes after which Claude Desktop cuts a call (package 31c), then the notable lines logged since, and the model calls again to go on waiting), and a call the client cuts off loses nothing: its result goes to the model's next call first (package 28c); since the protocol does not carry the chat's text, the token counts in every argument of every tool call, in turn or out of it, and `opt_out` is always listed. **The local runner** (`local.py`) is the door for a model on this machine under llama.cpp's `llama-server` (or Ollama): it reads which file the server loaded, and that name is the model's identity for consent. **The API door** (`api.py`, package 42; spec M6 §13) calls a hosted model through its maker's own API (the Anthropic Messages API first; OpenRouter's on the runner's shape), on the same harness as the runner, with no chat client between: the model's name as the API reports it is its identity for consent (`consent.IDENTITY_KINDS["api"]`), and a reply the API says another model wrote is not passed on. Its key is the owner's and in nothing the game sends, writes or saves (commitment 7): read from the platform's credential store, the environment or a file outside the repository, sent in the request's header alone, and the door refuses to start where it would be written anywhere the game keeps (`api.refusal_to_start`; `tests/test_api_door.py`, truth 86). At this door and over MCP a model may ask for the chart and the ship's view as pictures, drawn by the open browser page, kept in memory and never in the journal, the transcript or the save (`agents/pictures.py`). The game does not wait for any door unless it is started with `--lockstep`: what happens while the model thinks is folded into its open turn, and a turn held silent past the station's patience brings the nudge, then the pause. **The REPL** (`repl.py`) is text at a terminal, for a person (`--human`) or a named model (`--model-name`), in a game of its own and in lockstep. Consent records are written by the game to `consent/<date>-<weights>.md` beside the earlier transcripts, one file per conversation, their runtime line naming the game, the door and its client, and the newest for an identity decides; saves, with the agents' journals and the model's replies in them (each with the moment it came, so that a replay makes it there again, as it does the stops from outside the loop), go to `saves/` in the repository (ignored by git) unless the game is told otherwise. Since package 42 a station's acts (its seating, its orders, its lines, its journal and its state) are journaled at their ticks as the game's inputs (`core/acts.py`; spec M6 §14), so that a save replays on a later build to the same log whatever that build's sampling would ask, the transcript the record and not the replay's source; the consent brief has described a game replayed from its record so since package 42a.

## The shelf: reading without keeping

A local model has the least room of any reader, so the reference library is served in pieces with their sizes, and a read is a book taken off the shelf that the reader puts back (milestone 4b package 28d; spec M4 open item 9; the owner and the lead, 2026-09-28). `library()` lists the topics and what each costs, measured at four characters a token from the text served; a topic lists its sections with theirs; a section, a whole chapter (its size first) or a search (`find`) is served on request. Every library read, and a long `read_log`, opens with a handle (`primer 3, reefing, opened 04:10; book 7`); `shelve` puts a book back, and from the next request the model's conversation holds that line and not the pages; a book left open goes back by itself after `SHELF_LIFE_TURNS` (three) of the model's turns. In the game's process, over the agent API, at the local runner the pages are really gone from what the model is sent (the turn stream serves a shelved turn as its line; the runner reads its turns again when the stream's revision moves); over MCP and at the REPL the client keeps its own conversation, and `shelve` says so plainly. Replay is untouched: a shelve is a reply in the transcript, the shelf-life counts the model's turns, and a read or a shelve out of turn is recorded like the other acts from outside the loop (`tests/test_agents.py`, the shelf's tests). **The reference is a promise, not a possession** (`docs/design/Papers-and-Books.md`): nothing here makes it less reachable, every shelved book opens again on request, and the brief says the journal is where a reader keeps what it took from the page. None of the commitments above changes.
