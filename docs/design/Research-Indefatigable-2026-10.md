# Research note: HMS Indefatigable, the 64 of 1784 and the razee of 1795 (October 2026)

Phase 1 of the ships workflow (`Plan-ShipsWorkflow.md` §2), for the design and build phases.
It covers HMS *Indefatigable* as built, an Ardent-class 64 launched at Bucklers Hard in 1784,
and as razeed in 1794-95 into a heavy frigate rated 38 and then 44. The razee is the main goal
(the plan, §0). Two research rounds were run, and each was checked by an independent
re-reading or re-measurement. The earlier confirmation note of the owner's Vanguard material
(`owner-notes/indefatigable/research-note-2026-10-25.md`) and Winfield's entries from the
owner's copy are folded in. Where a researcher and a checker differ, this note gives the
checker's figure and the checker's access level, and says so.

The marks:

- **read**: the page, image, code or catalogue text was seen. "read (OCR)" is a text layer
  read without the page image. "tentative" is a reading at the limit of the image.
- **catalogue**: from a catalogue entry only; the document itself was not seen.
- **measured**: off an image; the image key (below) gives the image and its scale, and the
  uncertainty is stated.
- **secondary**: a modern book or Wikipedia, in copyright or unchecked; a pointer only.
- **memory, unverified**: not read during this research.
- **judgement**: reasoning, by the researchers or by this note.

Winfield and the owner's Vanguard notes are cited by page and paraphrased, never quoted.
Quotations are from public-domain pages only, and none runs past 30 words.

**Units and datums.** Lengths in English feet and inches, with metres in brackets. Tons are
tons burthen unless "tons of 35 cu ft" (displacement; one is 1.016 t). Positions along the
ship are feet forward of **AP**, the rabbet of the post at the lower deck, which is the
after end of the 160-ft length on the gun deck (the drawn after perpendicular of ZAZ1407
and the zero of every scale strip used). In metres they are given from the
**mid-perpendiculars**, 80 ft forward of AP, positive forward. Heights are above **K**, the
lowest drawn line of the keel. On ZAZ1407 that line is the bottom of a band 0.53-0.55 ft deep
under the main keel, probably a false keel (judgement, §2.3.6). The main keel's lower edge
is about 0.5 ft above K, and the rabbet 1.1 ft above that.

## 0. In brief

- **What she was.** An Ardent-class 64 (Slade's design of 1762), built by Henry Adams at
  Bucklers Hard and launched in July 1784. She was never in commission as a 64. Razeed at
  Portsmouth from September 1794 to February 1795 by taking off the upper works down to the
  64's upper deck, she became a frigate of 26 long 24-pounders on one gun deck. Pellew
  commanded her from 1795 to 1799. Broken up at Sheerness in 1816.
- **The hull is measured.** The lines, the load line, the decks, the ports, the profile
  below water, the lateral plane and the rudder are measured on the public-domain Commons
  originals, and the razee's waterline on her own proposal draught. She was not re-built
  below the upper deck, so the razee is the 64's hull floating 1 ft 2 in to 1 ft 5 in
  higher (§2.3).
- **The rig is the weak point.** Steel's 1794 table gives every spar of a 64. No mast or
  sail plan of the razee survives at Greenwich. The razees were first given a reduced rig,
  proved slow and unmanageable, and *Indefatigable* became an excellent sailer after Pellew
  altered her to his own plans (Osler). What he altered is not recorded. She had a gaff
  mizen by April 1796. The kit's account is that the 64's masts were kept, shortened at the
  heel; the deck taken off measures 6 ft 3 in to 6 ft 9 in (§3).
- **The fits.** As a 64: 26 x 24, 26 x 18, 12 x 9 pounders and 500 men. The razee's
  establishment: 26 x 24, 12 x 12 and 6 x 42-pdr carronades, with 310 and then 330 men. As
  carried in 1796: 26 x 24, 2 x 12 and 18 x 42-pdr carronades, with 330 men and boys (§4).
- **The truths.** A dozen dated accounts of the razee under sail; nine of her sister
  *Agamemnon*, built to the same draught by the same builder, stand in for the 64 (§7). No
  knots for either, except a mean of 11.2 kn computed over the 15-hour chase of 1796.

**Her condition.** After grounding on 7 May 1795 she was docked at Plymouth in August with 27
floors and first futtocks broken; the narrative judges that a frigate's frame could not have
stood it (Osler pp. 112-113, read). In October 1812 pilots refused to take her into Yarmouth
Roads for her great draught (ADM 1/1809/173, catalogue).

### 2.2 As razeed, 1794-95

**What was cut away.** The three 64s of 1794 (*Anson*, *Magnanime*, *Indefatigable*),
"excepting the portion of bulwark that lay abaft the gangway-entrance, ... were cut down
level with the upper deck" (James vol. 5 p. 428, OCR l. 21885-21887, read). The 64's
quarterdeck, forecastle and their sides came off above the upper deck, except a short
roundhouse (poop) aft: the December 1794 warrant shortened it to the beam abaft the mizen
(ZAZ2371's N.B., read, tentative), it was still there in August 1795 (Osler p. 113, read),
and poop and taffrail were reduced by April 1797 (ADM 1/1719/85, catalogue). Its height and
extent were not measured. The 64's upper deck became the razee's quarterdeck and forecastle;
the 64's lower deck her gun deck; the 64's orlop her lower (berth) deck (the titles of
ZAZ2373-2375, read; *Magnanime*'s ZAZ2543-2547, catalogue). In the waist the old upper deck
was taken away, leaving gangways along the sides (ZAZ2375, read) and the boats on skid beams
(the owner's notes (Vanguard kit), picture 4) (judgement).

#### 2.3.4 Stability

- **KM** (measured from the 1407T traces, ±0.7 ft): the 64 at WL1, KB 11.5 and BM 10.7 ft
  above the rabbet (checker's BM 10.9), so KM 22.3 ft above the rabbet, about 24.0 ft
  (7.3 m) above K. The razee at her line: KB 10.8, BM 11.3, KM 22.1 ft above the rabbet, about
  23.8 ft (7.25 m) above K.
- **GM** (judgement): no period figure for her KG or GM was found. Taking off the 64's upper
  works, 26 x 18-pdrs and 12 x 9-pdrs lowered KG by roughly 1 ft against a KM hardly
  changed. If the 64's GM was 4-5 ft (1.2-1.5 m), the razee's would be 5-6 ft (1.5-1.8 m),
  stiffer than the frigate-36's 1.3 m. *Magnanime*'s rolling in 1799 (§3.3) fits a stiff ship
  rather than a crank one.
- **Ballast** (Steel 1794 vol. II, 'The Practice of Stowing Ballast', OCR l. 5604-5637, read
  (OCR)): the iron ballast is stowed fore and aft beside the keelson and winged up three or
  more pigs above the floor heads amidships, the shingle spread over it, and the ground tier
  of casks sunk a quarter of their diameter into the shingle. A full, low-built ship needs
  her weights higher, lest she roll away her masts. The Admiralty's form for a ship's
  qualities asks the iron and shingle ballast aboard (Regulations 1806, OCR l. 27478, read
  (OCR)); her own returns were not found. No figure for her ballast was found.

### 3.2 The razee's first rig, 1794-95

- **What Osler says** (pp. 107-108, OCR l. 3859-3898, read): "One part of the plan was to
  reduce their masts and rigging in proportion to the diminished size of their hulls. All of
  them proved slow and unmanageable ships". The Comptroller of the Navy blamed faulty
  stowage of the ballast and hold. Pellew declined the command rather than risk his credit in
  a worthless ship, was allowed to alter her to his own plans, and "she became an excellent

### 3.3 The razee as altered, 1795 on

- **The sister's analogue** (catalogue): on 6 June 1795 at Plymouth, Schomberg of *Magnanime*
  asked leave to cruise a few days to try her new masts and yards (ADM 1/2491/60). On
  3 August 1795 at Cove he sent her stowage plan and reported her sailing extremely well,
  equal to *Diana* or *Seahorse* (ADM 1/2491/62). The catalogue says she had "lost" 64-gun
  masts and yards; the enclosure of 10 June is described as her stowage as she sailed "with
  64 gunships masts and yards". The two contradict each other; the letter is at Kew. In 1799
  her rolling and leeway were cured by moving the shingle ballast and the main mast and
  adding a gripe and a false keel (ADM 1/2599/68).
- **The kit**: the 64's masts restored, the lower masts shortened at the heels so that the
  courses hang right over the lowered sides (the owner's notes (Vanguard kit), V11). No period
  document stating the cut was found.
- **The cut measured** (judgement on measured decks, 1280T, checker): one deck came off.
  Cutting each lower mast by the height of the 64's deck above the old upper deck at that mast
  keeps each head at the same height above the working deck: the mizen about 6 ft 9 in, the
  main about 6 ft 7 in, the fore about 6 ft 3 in, or 6 ft 6 in ±6 in overall. The lower masts
  become about 94 ft 5 in (28.78 m), 83 ft 4 in (25.40 m) and 79 ft 3 in (24.16 m), within 2 ft
  of Steel's 50 (92-0, 81-6, 78-9). That may be where the kit's "50's rig" comes from
  (judgement). R1's 7 ft 0 in rested on a forecastle height that was a rail line.
- **A gaff by 1796** (read): in the Virginie action she lost "her Gaff and Mizen-Top-Mast",
  both leech-ropes of the main topsail were cut, and with "no After-Sail to back" she shot
  ahead (Gazette 13887 p. 388, page image; James vol. 1 p. 361, OCR l. 24826-24832). Osler
  adds the fore yard, and the falling mizen topmast disabling the main yard (p. 127). Her
  sister *Agamemnon* set a driver in March 1795 (§7.2).
- **Masts and yards incidents** (catalogue): Feb 1795 at Falmouth, the carpenters of *Concorde*
  and *Argo* first judged her mainmast safe, then found its bolts and nails needed tightening
  (ADM 1/2312/103, /107); 2 Feb 1796, *Révolutionnaire* in company damaged her yards in gales
  (ADM 1/1621/84); 9-10 June 1797 her fore yard carried away and *Cleopatra* was ordered to
  supply her main yard (ADM 1/2315/20); 30 July 1795 *Anson*'s masts and yards lay in Plymouth
  Dockyard (ADM 1/1717/167). In Dec 1795 *Révolutionnaire*'s mainmast was moved 6 ft aft for
  want of after-sail (ADM 1/1620/205). None gives her own dimensions.

vol. 2 p. 13; Troude pp. 55-57). Her after-sail in 1796 was the gaff mizen and the mizen
topsail. No source mentions a driver, staysails or a spritsail for her.

## 7. How she sailed: truth candidates

Each gives the date, place, wind and sea, sail, behaviour and source. Public-domain print can
be pinned as written; catalogue paraphrase only qualitatively. Speeds marked "computed" are
this note's arithmetic.

### 7.1 The razee

1. **1794-95, before the alteration** (all three): "All of them proved slow and unmanageable
   ships"; the Navy Board blamed the stowage of ballast and hold (Osler p. 107, read).
   Qualitative.
2. **Early 1795, after it**: an excellent sailer (Osler p. 108, read). On 29 March 1795 Hunt
   of *Concorde* reported her sailing qualities excellent (ADM 1/1912/168, catalogue).
3. **7 May 1795, off Finisterre**: wind northerly, the water smooth; chasing under all sail
   with royals and topgallant studding sails; she drew 19 ft 6 in abaft, struck a rock with
   18 ft over it, and was sallied off (Osler pp. 110-111, read). A static draught truth.
4. **31 August 1795**: strong gales and squalls, the wind flying round from W by S to NE, SE
   and SW, a very high sea; hove to under a close-reefed main topsail, topgallant yards down;
   pitching much, a deep plunge aft (Osler pp. 113-114, read).
5. **20 April 1796, the chase of the Virginie, off the Lizard**: wind SE, fresh (Clowes vol. 4
   p. 497; Troude), "off the land" (Osler p. 125). From lying to, first close-hauled, then
   running large after the Virginie bore away; royals and studding sails set until within
   gunshot a little before midnight. "The superior sailing of the Indefatigable gave me the
   Satisfaction of coming up with her, after a Chace of Fifteen Hours, and running One Hundred
   and Sixty-eight Miles" (Pellew, Gazette 13887 p. 388, read): a mean of 11.2 kn (computed),
   including the start. She outsailed the Virginie on a wind (Osler p. 126, OCR l. 4472). Pellew
   judged the Virginie's sailing little inferior to hers, and called her the finest ship and
   fastest sailer in the French navy (Gazette). *Concorde* and *Amazon* were run out of sight by
   evening (Osler p. 125). The same page has *L'Unité* taken under a press of sail "at the Rate
   of Ten Knots" (Cole's letter).
6. **The night of 20-21 April 1796**: 1 h 45 min under a crowd of sail; with gaff and mizen
   topmast gone and the main topsail useless she had "no After-Sail to back" and shot ahead
   of her opponent (Gazette; James vol. 1 p. 361, read). A handling truth for the loss of
   after-sail.
7. **13 January 1797, the chase of the Droits de l'Homme**: wind W, blowing hard, thick and
   hazy (Pellew); the sea rising. Studding sails and topgallants until within gunshot, then
   courses up and topsails close-reefed. The 74, after losing her topmasts, went about five
   knots under courses and mizen topsail, and James judges the frigates "probably running a
   third faster" (vol. 2 p. 13, OCR l. 1013-1018). By 5.30 p.m. she was between seven and eight
   miles ahead of *Amazon* (James vol. 2 p. 13; Clowes vol. 4 p. 302), about 1.5 kn faster over
   five hours if they started together (computed). Troude has the 74 at 11 knots when she
   sighted them (Gazette 13972 p. 53; Troude pp. 55-57; read).
8. **13-14 January 1797, the action and the lee shore**: "the Sea was high, the People on the
   Main Deck up to their Middles in Water" (Gazette 13972, read); some guns broke their
   breechings four times over; every mast wounded. At 4.20 a.m., land seen with the wind dead
   on shore, she set all she could carry and wore twice; "Happily, the sails stood well; the
   Indefatigable continued to gain by every tack" (Osler p. 155, read). She weathered the
   Penmarks at 11 a.m. by half a mile (Pellew) or three-quarters (James vol. 2 p. 18).
9. **About 25 October 1797, near Tenerife**: the Hyène, a fast flush ship of 24 long 8s, "led
   the Indefatigable, who was herself a good sailer, a chase of eight hours" (James vol. 2
   p. 102, read). Wind not stated.
10. **7-8 August 1798**: a chase of 24 hours before the new coppered corvette Vaillante was
    taken (James vol. 2 p. 258; Osler p. 178; read). Wind not stated.
11. **Practice, 1796** (Osler pp. 139, 177, read): close to the French admiral under easy sail
    with studding sails ready; reefs shaken out and studding-sail booms rigged out before
    daylight. Not a performance.
12. **22 August 1805, off Brest** (§8.3): wind N by E, hazy; a tactical datum, not a speed.
13. **Analogue only** (catalogue): *Magnanime* with a 64's masts and yards sailed equal to
    *Diana* or *Seahorse*, August 1795; in 1799 she rolled and went to leeward until her
    ballast and main mast were moved and a gripe and false keel added.

### 7.2 The 64, by her sister *Agamemnon*

*Indefatigable* never sailed as a 64 (§1.4, V4). *Agamemnon* was built by the same builder to
the same draught (§2.1), so her accounts stand in for the 64 (judgement).

1. **10 February 1793, Chatham**: "the finest 64 in the service, and has the character of
   sailing most remarkably well" (Nelson to his brother, Nicolas vol. 1 p. 299, OCR l. 18885,
   read (OCR)).
2. **About 20-25 May 1793, ten leagues off the Lizard**: "My Ship sails well; very few will
   outsail her" (to his brother, Nicolas vol. 1 p. 307, l. 19266, read (OCR)).
3. **5 June 1793, 16 leagues SW of Scilly**, Hood's eleven of the line, foggy with drizzling
   rain: "Agamemnon sails admirably; we think better than any Ship in the Fleet" (to his
   wife, from Clarke and M'Arthur, who misdate it 6 July; Nicolas vol. 1 p. 307, l. 19288, read
   (OCR), checker).
4. **20 August and September 1793, the Mediterranean**: "Agamemnon sails well" (p. 319);
   sent off with letters as "a fast sailer" (p. 324) (read (OCR)).
5. **22 October 1793, off Sardinia**: five French sail by the wind; she chased a frigate with
   every sail set, the chase steering "two points from the wind" and the 64 keeping her two
   points on the bow (the point of sailing is the chase's, not stated for the 64). The frigate
   yawed to fire broadsides, which her superiority in sailing let her do, and "whilst the
   breeze continued fresh, the Chase and ourselves left them fast" (two frigates, a third and
   a brig); calm at 9 a.m. (Nelson's journal, Nicolas vol. 1 pp. 334-335, OCR l. 20826-20848,
   read (OCR); James vol. 1 pp. 117-118, read). She had 345 men at quarters (James), about 155
   under her 500.
6. **6 January 1794, off Calvi**: the Melpomene, Admiral Trogoff told Nelson, was the finest
   frigate out of France and the fastest sailer; "the others we could outsail" (Nicolas vol. 1
   p. 346, l. 21409-21411, read (OCR)).
7. **13 March 1795, the Ça Ira**: a general chase in fresh breezes, squally and blowing very
   fresh; she came up with the dismasted Ça Ira, Hoste calling her "one of the fast sailers";
   Nelson had the helm put a-starboard and "the driver and after-sails ... braced up and
   shivered", so a 64 of the class set a driver by 1795; later she tacked and worked as
   exactly as if turning into Spithead (Nicolas vol. 2 pp. 12-14, OCR l. 3213-3255, and App.
   p. 464, l. 32078; James vol. 1 p. 286; read (OCR)).
8. **7-8 July 1795**: chased 24 hours into San Fiorenzo by seventeen of the line and six
   frigates in fickle winds, at times hard pressed; she escaped (Nicolas vol. 2 pp. 49-50,
   l. 5598; James vol. 1 p. 296; read (OCR)).
9. **4 June 1796**: worn after three years in commission, she still outsailed the 64 *Diadem*:
   "in point of sailing she is much inferior" (Nicolas vol. 2 p. 181, l. 14302, read (OCR)).

Her logs, which would give knots, are catalogue only (§10).

### 7.3 Lighter, stiffer, faster

The plan's truth pins the razee against the 64 (`Plan-ShipsWorkflow.md` §2, Phase 5):

- **Lighter**: about 1,870-1,925 tons against 2,090-2,200, the difference about 240 tons, and
  1 ft 2 in-1 ft 5 in less draught (§2.3; measured and judgement).
- **Stiffer**: no period figure. KM is almost unchanged and KG lower, so GM rises by about
  1 ft (judgement, §2.3.4).
- **Faster** (judgement on read sources): the razee outsailed the Virginie on a wind and caught
  her, the Virginie being called the fastest sailer in the French navy (1796); the 64
  *Agamemnon* was outsailed in a fresh breeze by the Melpomene, called France's fastest frigate
  (1793-94), while outsailing the line and lesser frigates. The ranking is: the razee, then a
  crack French frigate, then the 64 in good trim, then the line. Different ships, crews (the
  64 short-handed), weather and years.
- **No period knots** for either, beyond the computed 11.2 kn mean of 1796. The frigate-36's
  13.0 kn came from sailing-quality reports (`data/ships/frigate-36.yaml`, hull).

## 8. The scenarios' history

### 8.1 April 1796, off the Lizard

- **Pellew's squadron** (Gazette 13887 p. 388; Clowes vol. 4 pp. 496-497; read):
  *Indefatigable* 44 (Pellew); *Révolutionnaire* (Francis Cole); *Amazon* 36 (Robert Carthew
  Reynolds), the game's frigate-36; *Concorde* 36 (Anthony Hunt); *Argo* (her captain not
  seen). Winfield also has her with *Amazon* in Pellew's squadron in 1796-97 (p. 100).
- **Wednesday 20 April**: the squadron lay to under the Lizard while the prize *L'Unité*
  weathered it. A ship came in from seaward, did not answer the private signal and tacked
  away. Pellew sent *Révolutionnaire* with the prize and *Argo* to Plymouth, and chased with
  *Amazon* and *Concorde*. The wind kept the chase from steering for Ushant, "or she must have
  escaped" (Gazette). The action began a little past midnight. Pellew thanks Hunt and
  Reynolds for keeping after him through the night's many courses, "their Distance astern
  being so great"; *Concorde* came up under the enemy's stern at the end (Gazette, read).
- **The Virginie**: Pellew gives her 44 guns (18- and 9-pdrs), 340 men, sailed singly from Brest
  four days before to cruise off the Lizard, 158 by 43 ft (Gazette). James gives 339 men and
  boys and 151 ft 3½ in by 39 ft 10 in extreme, 1,066 tons against her 1,384 (vol. 1 pp.
  361-362, read). Pellew's letter is dated Falmouth, 23 April.
- **The wind**: SE (Clowes, from the log; Troude), against Osler's "off the land" (p. 125).

### 8.2 January 1797, with *Amazon*

The Droits de l'Homme chase and action of 13-14 January 1797 (§7.1, items 7-8), with *Amazon*
in company, are the other period action that puts the game's frigate-36 beside her.

### 8.3 August 1805, off Brest

- **20 August**: the 44 *Indefatigable* (John Tremayne Rodd), with the 38 *Niobe* and two or
  three smaller vessels, was reconnoitring Brest from about four miles S by E of the Black
  Rocks, and at 6.30 p.m. saw the French advanced squadron getting under way (James vol. 3
  p. 311, OCR l. 16805-16813, read).
- **21 August**: the French fleet, 21 of the line and 5 frigates, stood out and anchored between
  Camaret and Bertheaume; Cornwallis, with 17 of the line, lay about three leagues S by W of
  Ushant in a moderate breeze at N by E (p. 312, read).
- **22 August**: the weather hazy, the wind still N by E. At 9 a.m., being ahead, she stood
  towards the Alexandre 80 (Rear-Admiral Willaumez), leading the French out in line of battle;
  at 9.30 the Alexandre's broadside "was answered by the latter's main-deck guns, the distance
  being too great for the carronades. On this the Indefatigable tacked" (p. 313, l. 16952-16957,
  read). The French then tacked back for the harbour's mouth.
- **Her captain**: James names Rodd on 20 August (p. 311); Winfield has Rodd from 10.1805
  (p. 100). Graham Moore asked two months' sick leave at Plymouth on 1 July 1805 (ADM
  1/2149/123), and on 5 November Rodd wrote that Moore had taken her first lieutenant to the
  *Fame* (ADM 1/2408/83) (catalogue). So Rodd may have been acting from the summer.
- **Her guns in 1805**: carronades on the quarterdeck; in November Rodd asked to keep them "as
  before" instead of 9-pdrs lately ordered, she being fitted for carronades and her channels
  differing from a regular frigate's (ADM 1/2408/84, catalogue).
- **Earlier**: under Moore she took the Spanish treasure frigates off Cadiz on 5 October 1804
  (James vol. 3 pp. 286-289; Winfield p. 100). She was laid up at Plymouth in 1802-03
  (Winfield p. 100), so a scenario of 1802-03 should not have her at sea (judgement).
- The plan's scenario puts her on the Ushant station in a westerly. In the accounts read, the
  French came out of the Iroise in a northerly to north-easterly wind (1805) (judgement on
  one account).

## 10. What is open

1. **The razee's rig after the alteration**: no mast or sail plan survives at Greenwich. What
   Pellew altered in early 1795 (masts, stowage, or both) is not recorded in print. The holders:
   NMM PEL/1/1 (Pellew's letterbook 1795-98), ADM 1/2312 (his letters of Jan-Feb 1795; /108's
   enclosure missing), and *Magnanime*'s ADM 1/2491/62 at Kew.
